# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:13

**Genesis 1:13 —** “And there was evening and there was morning, the third day.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayəhî-ʿereḇ wayəhî-ḇōqer yôm šəlîšî*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And there was evening, and there was morning, the third day.”**

---

# 1. “And there was evening” — וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב (*wayəhî-ʿereḇ*)

Genesis 1:13 begins with:

**וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב**

*Wayəhî-ʿereḇ*

**“And there was evening.”**

The phrase repeats the familiar conclusion to the creation days.

The Hebrew verb **וַיְהִי (*wayəhî*)** comes from the root **היה (*h-y-h*)**, meaning **“to be,” “to become,” or “to happen.”**

The noun **עֶרֶב (*ʿereḇ*)** means:

**“evening.”**

This marks the completion of the third day's period of activity.

---

# 2. “And there was morning” — וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר (*wayəhî-ḇōqer*)

The phrase continues:

**וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר**

*Wayəhî-ḇōqer*

**“And there was morning.”**

The noun **בֹּקֶר (*bōqer*)** means:

**“morning.”**

The repeated pairing of **evening** and **morning** provides the temporal framework for the creation days.

---

# 3. Evening and morning

Genesis 1 repeatedly presents the pattern:

**evening → morning**

This occurs after each successive day.

The formula demonstrates that the creation account is structured in identifiable periods of time.

Genesis does not simply describe an undefined sequence of events.

It repeatedly marks the completion of each day.

---

# 4. “The third day” — יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי (*yôm šəlîšî*)

The verse concludes:

**יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי**

*Yôm šəlîšî*

**“the third day.”**

The noun **יוֹם (*yôm*)** means:

**“day.”**

The ordinal **שְׁלִישִׁי (*šəlîšî*)** means:

**“third.”**

Therefore:

**יוֹם שְׁלִישִׁי**

means:

**“the third day.”**

---

# 5. The completion of Day Three

Genesis 1:13 concludes the events associated with the third day.

Day Three included two major developments:

### First

The waters were gathered together.

### Second

Dry land appeared.

### Third

The earth produced vegetation.

Therefore Day Three contains both:

**the formation of terrestrial space**

and

**the beginning of terrestrial productivity.**

---

# 6. Genesis 1:9–10 and Genesis 1:11–12

The third day can be divided into two closely related sections.

### Genesis 1:9–10

God establishes:

**dry land**

and

**seas.**

### Genesis 1:11–12

God establishes:

**vegetation**

and

**fruit-bearing trees.**

Verse 13 now closes the entire sequence.

---

# 7. The recurring creation-day formula

Genesis 1 has a repeated structure:

**God speaks.**

**Creation responds.**

**God evaluates.**

**Evening comes.**

**Morning comes.**

**The day is completed.**

Genesis 1:13 provides the final part of this pattern for Day Three.

---

# 8. The significance of “third day”

The phrase:

**“the third day”**

marks progression.

Creation is unfolding according to an ordered sequence.

Day One established light.

Day Two established the expanse.

Day Three established dry land and vegetation.

The account is therefore progressively moving toward a world prepared for living creatures.

---

# 9. Day Three as a major transition

The first two days primarily establish divisions within the created environment.

Day Three introduces something different:

**productive life.**

Vegetation appears.

The earth begins producing.

The created environment is becoming capable of sustaining increasingly complex forms of life.

---

# 10. From unformed to ordered

Genesis 1:2 described the earth as:

**“without form and void.”**

By the end of Day Three:

**land has been distinguished from water**

and

**the land is covered with vegetation.**

The movement is therefore:

**unformed → ordered → productive.**

---

# 11. The forming and filling structure

The third day contributes to the larger structure of Genesis 1.

The creation account can broadly be understood through two movements:

**forming**

and

**filling.**

Day Three forms the terrestrial realm:

**land and seas.**

Then it begins filling that realm with:

**vegetation.**

Later days will fill the prepared environments with living creatures.

---

# 12. Day Three and the preparation of the earth

The earth is increasingly prepared for the creatures that will inhabit it.

By the end of Day Three, the land contains:

**vegetation**

**plants**

**fruit-bearing trees.**

The environment is therefore prepared for subsequent life.

---

# 13. God's orderly work

The phrase:

**“the third day”**

demonstrates sequence and order.

God does not create randomly.

The work proceeds according to a deliberate pattern.

Each stage prepares for the next.

---

# 14. Evening and morning as boundaries

The evening and morning formula functions as a boundary marker.

It identifies the completion of one creation day and the transition toward the next.

This formula appears repeatedly throughout Genesis 1.

---

# 15. The meaning of “day” — יוֹם (*yôm*)

The Hebrew word **יוֹם (*yôm*)** has a range of meanings depending upon context.

It can refer to:

**a day**

**daylight**

or sometimes a broader period of time.

However, Genesis 1 repeatedly combines **yôm** with the sequence of **evening and morning** and with ordinal numbers such as:

**first**

**second**

**third**

and so forth.

The passage therefore presents a clearly structured sequence of creation days.

---

# 16. The third day is complete

Genesis 1:13 does not introduce a new creative command.

Instead, it closes the preceding activity.

The vegetation has appeared.

God has declared it good.

Now the third day reaches its conclusion.

---

# 17. The goodness of Day Three

Although verse 13 itself does not repeat:

**“God saw that it was good,”**

that declaration has already appeared in verse 12.

Therefore the events of the third day conclude after God has evaluated the vegetation as good.

---

# 18. God's evaluation and completion

The sequence of verses 9–13 can be summarized:

**God commands the waters to gather.**

**Dry land appears.**

**God names the land and seas.**

**God declares the result good.**

**God commands vegetation to appear.**

**The earth produces vegetation.**

**God declares the vegetation good.**

**Evening and morning complete the third day.**

---

# 19. Divine sovereignty over time

God's creative activity occurs within an ordered temporal sequence.

Time itself is not outside God's authority.

The days of creation are marked by God's work and God's ordering.

---

# 20. Time belongs to the Creator

The creation account presents time as part of God's created order.

The sequence of:

**evening**

and

**morning**

belongs to the world God is ordering.

God is therefore not merely working within an uncontrolled universe.

He is ordering creation itself.

---

# 21. Genesis 1:13 and divine faithfulness

God's work on Day Three reaches its intended completion.

Nothing is left unresolved within the stated creative purpose of that day.

This demonstrates the faithfulness of God's creative Word.

---

# 22. Genesis 1:13 and the effectiveness of God's Word

The previous verses demonstrate:

God says:

**“Let the earth sprout vegetation.”**

The earth responds.

The vegetation appears.

God declares it good.

The day ends.

Thus God's Word is effective from command through completion.

---

# 23. Genesis 1:13 and divine providence

The completed day demonstrates God's orderly governance of creation.

The world is not developing independently of its Creator.

It is being progressively ordered under divine authority.

---

# 24. Genesis 1:13 and creation's progression

The progression can be summarized:

### Day One

**Light**

### Day Two

**Expanse**

### Day Three

**Land, seas, vegetation**

### Day Four

**Lights in the heavens**

### Day Five

**Sea creatures and birds**

### Day Six

**Land animals and humanity**

The third day therefore stands at the center of the first half of the creation account.

---

# 25. Day Three and the structure of Genesis 1

Day Three is particularly significant because it contains two creative acts.

The first establishes the environment.

The second introduces vegetation.

This makes Day Three a bridge between:

**the preparation of the world**

and

**the filling of the world.**

---

# 26. The importance of repetition

Genesis intentionally repeats:

**“And there was evening and there was morning…”**

The repetition gives the creation account rhythm and structure.

It also emphasizes the completion of God's work.

---

# 27. The third day and divine order

The phrase:

**“the third day”**

reinforces the orderly progression of God's work.

There is:

**Day One**

**Day Two**

**Day Three**

and eventually:

**Day Six.**

The narrative is carefully structured.

---

# 28. Genesis 1:13 and biblical chronology

The verse contributes to the chronological framework of Genesis 1.

The author presents the creation events in sequence.

Day Three follows Day Two.

Day Four follows Day Three.

The creation narrative therefore has a clear temporal progression.

---

# 29. The theological importance of completion

The completion formula demonstrates that God's work has definite stages.

God does not merely initiate creation.

He brings each stage to its intended completion.

The third day reaches its appointed conclusion.

---

# 30. Genesis 1:13 and God's wisdom

The structured progression of creation reveals wisdom.

The environment is prepared before it is filled.

Land appears before land animals.

Vegetation appears before creatures that will consume vegetation.

The created world is progressively prepared.

---

# 31. Vegetation before animals

The third day establishes vegetation before the creation of animals.

This is important within the narrative because the earth is being prepared for life.

Plants will later provide food for animals and humanity.

Thus the sequence reflects purposeful preparation.

---

# 32. Vegetation before humanity

Humanity will not appear until Day Six.

By then, the earth will already contain:

**land**

**seas**

**vegetation**

**sun**

**moon**

**stars**

**birds**

**sea creatures**

**land animals.**

Humanity enters an already prepared world.

---

# 33. Divine provision precedes human existence

Genesis 1 therefore portrays God's provision as preceding humanity's existence.

Before humanity can cultivate or eat from the earth, God has already created the vegetation.

This demonstrates divine generosity and preparation.

---

# 34. The goodness of creation before the fall

Day Three occurs before Genesis 3.

There is no curse yet.

The earth's vegetation is explicitly described as good.

The good creation of Genesis 1 therefore provides the baseline against which the later effects of sin are understood.

---

# 35. The third day and the curse

After the fall, Genesis 3:17–18 describes the ground as cursed.

The ground will produce:

**thorns**

and

**thistles.**

This later condition should be understood against the backdrop of Genesis 1:11–12.

Originally, God declared the vegetation good.

The curse introduces frustration into the relationship between humanity and the ground.

---

# 36. Genesis 1:13 and the doctrine of creation

The verse supports several fundamental creation doctrines:

**God creates intentionally.**

**God orders creation progressively.**

**God establishes time and sequence.**

**God completes His work.**

**God's creation is good.**

---

# 37. Genesis 1:13 and God's sovereignty over history

The creation days establish a pattern of ordered progression.

This provides an early biblical demonstration that history is not meaningless.

God's work proceeds toward defined purposes.

The creation account begins with a world that is unformed and moves toward an ordered and inhabited creation.

---

# 38. Genesis 1:13 and God's purpose

The third day has a definite purpose:

**land is established**

and

**the land becomes productive.**

The day is therefore not merely a passage of time.

It represents a completed stage in God's creative purpose.

---

# 39. Genesis 1:13 and the Word of God

The entire third day can be traced back to God's speech.

God speaks.

Creation responds.

The work is completed.

This provides another demonstration of the authority of God's Word.

---

# 40. Genesis 1:13 and Christological creation

Genesis 1:13 does not explicitly mention Christ.

Nevertheless, the New Testament teaches that creation came into existence through Christ.

John 1:3 states that all things were made through Him.

Colossians 1:16 likewise teaches that all things were created through Him and for Him.

Therefore, the third day belongs within the larger biblical testimony concerning God's work of creation through Christ.

---

# 41. Genesis 1:13 and Christ's sustaining work

Colossians 1:17 teaches:

**“in him all things hold together.”**

The ordered world described in Genesis continues to exist under God's sustaining authority.

The completion of Day Three therefore belongs to the larger biblical doctrine of creation and providence.

---

# 42. Genesis 1:13 and the new creation

The creation account begins with God ordering the world.

The Bible ultimately concludes with God bringing creation to its consummation.

The movement is therefore:

**creation → fall → redemption → restoration.**

The completed third day forms one early stage in this larger biblical storyline.

---

# 43. The literary function of Genesis 1:13

Genesis 1:13 functions primarily as the closing statement for Day Three.

It does not introduce another creative act.

Instead, it marks:

**the end of the third day.**

The verse therefore provides narrative closure.

---

# 44. The rhythm of Genesis 1

Genesis 1:13 contributes to the repeated rhythm:

**God speaks.**

**Something happens.**

**God evaluates.**

**Evening.**

**Morning.**

**Day completed.**

This rhythm continues throughout the chapter.

---

# 45. The significance of the third day in Genesis

The phrase **“third day”** becomes significant in later biblical narratives as well.

However, Genesis 1:13 itself simply identifies the chronological completion of Day Three.

Later biblical uses of the third day should not be imposed upon this verse as though Genesis 1:13 were explicitly predicting them.

Its immediate function is chronological and structural.

---

# 46. Genesis 1:13 and the reliability of God's creative command

Everything commanded on the third day is accomplished.

The waters are gathered.

The land appears.

Vegetation grows.

Trees produce fruit.

The created order responds exactly as God commands.

---

# 47. Creation as ordered worship

Although Genesis 1:13 does not explicitly describe worship, the ordered creation points toward the glory of its Creator.

The world displays:

**order**

**fruitfulness**

**beauty**

**productivity**

**dependence.**

These characteristics ultimately direct attention toward the wisdom and power of God.

---

# 48. Genesis 1:13 and human humility

Humanity does not appear until later.

Therefore humans are not the starting point of creation.

The world already exists by God's command.

Humanity will eventually exercise dominion, but humanity remains a creature within God's creation.

This provides a foundation for humility before the Creator.

---

# 49. The third day as preparation

Day Three prepares the world for what follows.

The earth now has:

**dry land**

**seas**

**vegetation**

This prepared environment will soon receive:

**sun and moon**

**birds**

**sea creatures**

**land animals**

**humanity.**

---

# 50. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:13 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**The third day reaches its appointed completion as evening and morning mark the end of another stage of God's orderly creative work.**

Or more simply:

**God completes the third day of creation according to His established order.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:13

### **“And there was evening”**

→ The third day's period reaches its closing boundary.

### **“And there was morning”**

→ The next temporal boundary arrives.

### **“The third day”**

→ The third stage of God's ordered creative work is completed.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:13 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God governs the progression of creation.
2. **Divine order** — creation proceeds according to an established sequence.
3. **Divine wisdom** — each stage contributes to the preparation of the world.
4. **Effectiveness of God's Word** — God's commands are accomplished.
5. **Completion of divine work** — each creative stage reaches its appointed conclusion.
6. **Created time** — evening and morning form part of the ordered creation.
7. **Creation chronology** — the text identifies a sequence of creation days.
8. **Goodness of creation** — Day Three's creative work has already been declared good.
9. **Divine providence** — the created order operates under God's authority.
10. **Human creatureliness** — humanity enters an already prepared creation.
11. **Divine provision** — vegetation is established before humanity and animals appear.
12. **Pre-fall goodness** — the third day belongs to the original good creation.
13. **Creation and redemption** — the ordered creation provides the foundation for the later biblical story of restoration.
14. **Christological creation** — the New Testament places all creation within Christ's creative work.
15. **Christological sustenance** — all things continue to hold together under Christ.
16. **New creation** — the completed stages of Genesis anticipate the ultimate consummation of God's creative purposes.

---

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:13 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD WORKS ACCORDING TO AN ORDERED PURPOSE

**“The third day…”**

God's creative work unfolds according to a deliberate sequence.

---

## II. GOD COMPLETES WHAT HE BEGINS

**“And there was evening and there was morning…”**

The third day reaches its appointed conclusion.

God's creative work is purposeful and complete.

---

## III. GOD PREPARES CREATION FOR LIFE

Day Three establishes:

**land**

**seas**

**vegetation**

The earth is progressively prepared for the creatures that will inhabit it.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:13 teaches that God brings the third day of creation to its appointed completion within an orderly sequence of evening and morning, demonstrating that His creative work proceeds purposefully, faithfully, and according to His sovereign design.**

The progression from Genesis 1:9 through Genesis 1:13 is:

**God commands**

→ **the waters are gathered**

→ **dry land appears**

→ **God names the land and seas**

→ **God declares the result good**

→ **God commands vegetation**

→ **the earth produces vegetation**

→ **plants yield seed**

→ **trees bear fruit**

→ **God declares the vegetation good**

→ **evening**

→ **morning**

→ **the third day is completed.**

The recurring pattern remains:

**God speaks.**

**Creation responds.**

**God evaluates.**

**God completes.**
