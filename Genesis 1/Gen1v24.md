# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:24

**Genesis 1:24 —** “And God said, ‘Let the earth bring forth living creatures according to their kinds—livestock and creeping things and beasts of the earth according to their kinds.’ And it was so.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים תּוֹצֵא הָאָרֶץ נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה לְמִינָהּ בְּהֵמָה וָרֶמֶשׂ וְחַיְתוֹ־אֶרֶץ לְמִינָהּ וַיְהִי־כֵן׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm tôṣēʾ hāʾāreṣ nepeš ḥayyāh ləmînâ bəhēmâ wāremeś wəḥaytô-ʾereṣ ləmînâ wayəhî-kēn*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And God said, ‘Let the earth bring forth living creatures according to their kind: livestock and creeping things and beasts of the earth according to their kind.’ And it was so.”**

---

# 1. “And God said” — וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים (*wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:24 begins with the familiar expression:

**“And God said…”**

The creation account repeatedly emphasizes God's spoken word.

God speaks, and His command brings about the intended result.

---

# 2. The Word of God and Creation

The phrase connects Genesis 1:24 with the earlier creation commands.

God's creative activity is consistently presented as purposeful and authoritative.

The sequence is:

**God speaks**

→

**God's command is given**

→

**creation responds**

→

**the intended reality appears.**

---

# 3. A New Realm Is Addressed

The fifth day focused on:

**the waters**

and

**the winged creatures.**

Genesis 1:24 now turns to:

**the earth.**

The narrative moves from aquatic and aerial life to terrestrial animal life.

---

# 4. “Let the earth bring forth” — תּוֹצֵא הָאָרֶץ (*tôṣēʾ hāʾāreṣ*)

The expression literally conveys:

**“Let the earth bring forth…”**

The Hebrew verb:

**יָצָא (*yāṣāʾ*)**

means:

**“to go out”**

**“to come out”**

or

**“to emerge.”**

In this context, the earth is commanded to bring forth living creatures.

---

# 5. The Earth Is Not the Ultimate Creator

Although the text says:

**“Let the earth bring forth…”**

God remains the speaker and sovereign Creator.

The earth acts according to God's command.

---

# 6. Divine Sovereignty and Creaturely Means

The verse presents an important relationship:

**God commands**

and

**the earth brings forth.**

The earth functions as an instrument within God's creative ordering.

---

# 7. The Earth Is Prepared for Life

Earlier, God established:

**dry land**

and

**vegetation.**

Now the land is commanded to bring forth animal life.

The earth becomes increasingly populated.

---

# 8. “Living creatures” — נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה (*nepeš ḥayyāh*)

The phrase:

**נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה**

means:

**“living creature”**

or

**“living being.”**

The same expression appeared in Genesis 1:20 concerning aquatic life.

---

# 9. Continuity Between Day Five and Day Six

Genesis deliberately uses similar language for the creatures of the waters and the creatures of the land.

Both are described as:

**living creatures.**

---

# 10. The Hebrew Concept of *Nephesh*

The Hebrew noun:

**נֶפֶשׁ (*nepeš*)**

has a broad range of meaning.

Depending upon context, it can refer to:

**a living being**

**a creature**

**a person**

**life**

or

**the self.**

In Genesis 1:24, the contextual meaning is:

**living creature.**

---

# 11. “According to their kinds” — לְמִינָהּ (*ləmînâ*)

The phrase:

**לְמִינָהּ**

means:

**“according to its kind.”**

Again, Genesis emphasizes ordered diversity.

---

# 12. The Repeated Theme of “Kinds”

The concept of created kinds has already appeared in Genesis 1:21.

It now appears again concerning terrestrial animals.

---

# 13. Diversity Within Order

God creates many types of creatures.

Yet their diversity is not presented as random.

The creatures exist:

**according to their kinds.**

---

# 14. “Livestock” — בְּהֵמָה (*bəhēmāh*)

The first category is:

**בְּהֵמָה (*bəhēmāh*)**

commonly translated:

**“livestock”**

**“cattle”**

or

**“beasts.”**

The term generally refers to larger land animals, especially animals associated with human or domestic settings.

---

# 15. Livestock and Domestic Animals

The category can include animals that are capable of being domesticated.

However, the Hebrew term should not necessarily be restricted only to modern definitions of domesticated livestock.

---

# 16. Large Land Animals

The term broadly identifies a category of terrestrial animals.

It contrasts with the smaller or crawling creatures mentioned next.

---

# 17. “Creeping Things” — וָרֶמֶשׂ (*wāremeś*)

The second category is:

**רֶמֶשׂ (*remeś*)**

which can refer to:

**creeping things**

**small moving creatures**

or

**creatures that move along the ground.**

---

# 18. Movement Along the Ground

The emphasis of the term is associated with movement close to the ground.

It is broader than the modern category of reptiles.

---

# 19. Creeping Things Are Part of Creation

Genesis does not exclude small or seemingly insignificant creatures from God's creative work.

They are included in the ordered creation.

---

# 20. “Beasts of the Earth” — וְחַיְתוֹ־אֶרֶץ (*wəḥaytô-ʾereṣ*)

The third major category is:

**“beasts of the earth.”**

The Hebrew expression is associated with wild animals or animals of the land.

---

# 21. Wild Animals

The phrase distinguishes these creatures from the category of **בְּהֵמָה (*bəhēmāh*)**.

A useful conceptual distinction is:

**livestock / larger animals**

versus

**wild animals of the land.**

---

# 22. The Earth Contains Multiple Animal Categories

Genesis 1:24 therefore identifies three broad categories:

1. **Livestock**
2. **Creeping things**
3. **Beasts of the earth**

---

# 23. These Categories Are Comprehensive Rather Than Modern Taxonomy

The purpose is not to provide a modern zoological classification system.

The categories describe the major forms of terrestrial animal life within the biblical narrative.

---

# 24. The Land Is Now Filled

Earlier, the earth was described in relation to:

**dry land**

and

**vegetation.**

Now the land begins to be populated with animals.

---

# 25. The Correspondence Between Day Three and Day Six

There is a structural correspondence:

### Day Three

God establishes:

**dry land**

**vegetation**

### Day Six

God fills:

**the land with animals**

**humanity**

---

# 26. Forming and Filling

This continues the major pattern of Genesis 1:

**Days One–Three**

→ God forms realms.

**Days Four–Six**

→ God fills those realms.

---

# 27. Day Six Fills the Land

Genesis 1:24 begins the terrestrial filling of the land.

The earth is now commanded to bring forth living creatures.

---

# 28. The Earth Responds to God's Word

The verse concludes:

**“And it was so.”**

This is another confirmation that God's command is accomplished.

---

# 29. “And it was so” — וַיְהִי־כֵן (*wayəhî-kēn*)

The expression:

**וַיְהִי־כֵן**

means:

**“and it was so.”**

This formula emphasizes the effectiveness of God's command.

---

# 30. God's Word Accomplishes Its Purpose

God speaks.

The earth brings forth living creatures.

The result corresponds to God's command.

---

# 31. Divine Authority

The phrase:

**“And it was so”**

demonstrates that creation occurs under God's sovereign authority.

---

# 32. No Resistance to God's Command

The text presents no conflict or resistance.

God commands.

Creation responds.

---

# 33. Creation by Divine Command

Genesis 1:24 therefore continues the repeated biblical pattern:

**God said**

→

**it was so.**

---

# 34. God's Sovereignty Over the Earth

God commands the earth itself to bring forth living creatures.

The land is therefore under God's authority.

---

# 35. The Earth as God's Creation

The earth does not exist independently of God.

It is already His created realm.

He commands it according to His purpose.

---

# 36. Creaturely Dependence

The creatures that emerge from the earth ultimately depend upon God's creative command.

---

# 37. Terrestrial Life Is God's Work

Even though the earth is described as bringing forth the animals, God remains the ultimate source of their existence.

---

# 38. The Earth Is Productive Because God Commands It

The productivity of the earth is not autonomous.

It operates within the order God establishes.

---

# 39. Creation Is Ordered

The phrase:

**“according to their kinds”**

is central.

God creates diversity within recognizable order.

---

# 40. Creation Is Abundant

The earth is commanded to bring forth multiple categories of living creatures.

The result is a richly populated terrestrial world.

---

# 41. Creation Is Purposeful

The different categories of animals occupy the terrestrial realm according to God's design.

---

# 42. Creation Is Structured

The categories demonstrate organization:

**livestock**

**creeping things**

**beasts of the earth.**

---

# 43. The Land and Its Creatures

There is a fitting relationship between:

**earth**

and

**terrestrial creatures.**

The land becomes the environment from which these creatures emerge and upon which they live.

---

# 44. The Earth and Life

The biblical narrative presents the earth as capable of bringing forth life because God commands it to do so.

---

# 45. The Earth Is Not Personified as a Deity

Although the earth is grammatically commanded to bring forth creatures, Genesis does not treat the earth as divine.

It remains part of God's creation.

---

# 46. Creation Is God-Centered

God remains the active authority behind the entire process.

The earth responds to Him.

---

# 47. The Living Earth

The earth becomes a populated realm rather than merely an empty landmass.

---

# 48. The Progression of Genesis 1

The sequence has now developed:

**Light**

→

**Sky and waters**

→

**Dry land**

→

**Vegetation**

→

**Heavenly lights**

→

**Sea creatures and birds**

→

**Land animals**

→

**Humanity.**

---

# 49. The Sixth Day Begins

Genesis 1:24 introduces the first creative work of Day Six.

The land animals are created before humanity.

---

# 50. The Sixth Day Has a Twofold Focus

Day Six will involve:

**terrestrial animals**

and

**humanity.**

---

# 51. Land Animals Prepare the Way for Humanity

The creation of land animals establishes the terrestrial world into which humanity will be placed.

---

# 52. Humanity Is Not Created in Isolation

Humanity will enter a world already populated with land animals, birds, sea creatures, vegetation, and other forms of life.

---

# 53. The Earth Is Prepared for Humanity

Genesis presents the created world as increasingly prepared for humanity's arrival.

---

# 54. The Sequence of Provision

God creates:

**environment**

→

**plants**

→

**animals**

→

**humanity.**

This reveals a purposeful sequence of provision.

---

# 55. Animal Life Precedes Human Life

Land animals appear before humanity.

This establishes humanity's later relationship to them.

---

# 56. Dominion Presupposes Animal Creation

Human dominion over the animals assumes that God has already created the animals.

---

# 57. Human Authority Is Delegated

When humanity receives dominion in Genesis 1:26–28, the animals already belong to God.

Human authority is therefore not absolute ownership in the ultimate sense.

---

# 58. The Goodness of Land Animals

The next verse, Genesis 1:25, explicitly records God's evaluation:

**“God saw that it was good.”**

Thus the terrestrial animals introduced here are part of God's good creation.

---

# 59. Verse 24 and Verse 25

The narrative pattern is:

**Verse 24**

God commands the earth to bring forth animals.

**Verse 25**

God makes the animals and declares them good.

---

# 60. Command and Evaluation

This follows the established pattern of Genesis 1:

**command**

→

**creation**

→

**evaluation.**

---

# 61. The Earth Produces According to God's Command

The phrase:

**“And it was so”**

anticipates the explicit fulfillment statement of verse 25.

---

# 62. Divine Speech and Divine Action

Genesis does not separate God's speech from His creative action.

His command produces the intended result.

---

# 63. God's Word Is Effective

The creation account repeatedly demonstrates that God's word is effective and authoritative.

---

# 64. God's Word and the Created Order

The created order exists according to God's spoken purpose.

---

# 65. Creation Is Not Self-Explaining

Genesis directs attention beyond the creatures to their Creator.

The animals exist because God commands their emergence.

---

# 66. Theological Significance of “It Was So”

The phrase provides a concise theological statement:

**God's command is fulfilled.**

---

# 67. God's Reliability

The fulfillment of God's commands demonstrates the reliability of His word.

---

# 68. Divine Faithfulness in Creation

God says what He intends, and creation comes into conformity with His command.

---

# 69. The Sovereignty of God

Genesis 1:24 therefore contributes to the biblical doctrine that God possesses absolute authority over creation.

---

# 70. The Goodness of the Created Order

The animals are not introduced as enemies of God.

They are part of the creation that God is establishing.

---

# 71. No Dualism

Genesis presents no ultimate conflict between God and an opposing power within the animal kingdom.

---

# 72. Wild Animals Are God's Creatures

Even animals that humans may fear or consider dangerous are part of God's created order.

---

# 73. Small Creatures Are God's Creatures

The category of creeping things reminds the reader that even small creatures are included in God's creation.

---

# 74. Domestic Animals Are God's Creatures

The category of livestock likewise belongs to God's creative work.

---

# 75. All Terrestrial Categories Are Under God

The verse collectively places the terrestrial animal world within God's sovereign creative command.

---

# 76. The Diversity of Land Animals

The verse anticipates the enormous diversity of terrestrial animal life.

---

# 77. The Richness of Creation

God does not create only one kind of land animal.

The earth is populated with many forms of living creatures.

---

# 78. The Wisdom of Classification

The categories help communicate that this diversity possesses order and structure.

---

# 79. The Phrase “According to Their Kinds”

This phrase prevents the reader from viewing the diversity of creatures as mere chaos.

God establishes kinds within His creation.

---

# 80. Created Order and Modern Biology

The biblical concept of **“kind”** should not be automatically equated with modern biological concepts such as:

**species**

**genus**

or

**family.**

Genesis is using its own descriptive framework.

---

# 81. The Primary Concern of the Text

The theological emphasis is that:

**God created the land animals**

and

**He created them according to an ordered design.**

---

# 82. God's Wisdom in Creation

The differentiation of animals into categories demonstrates purposeful organization.

---

# 83. God's Power in Creation

The earth is populated by living creatures because God commands it.

---

# 84. God's Goodness in Creation

The following verse confirms that God sees the terrestrial creatures as good.

---

# 85. God's Providence

The created order provides environments in which terrestrial creatures can live.

---

# 86. The Relationship Between Earth and Animals

The earth is presented as the realm from which terrestrial animal life emerges.

---

# 87. The Land Becomes a Living Realm

Dry land is no longer simply geographical space.

It becomes inhabited by living creatures.

---

# 88. The Earth Moves Toward Fullness

Genesis continues the movement from:

**emptiness**

toward

**fullness.**

---

# 89. The Filling of the Earth

The land is now beginning to be filled with terrestrial life.

---

# 90. The Pattern of Creation

The overall pattern is:

**God forms**

→

**God fills**

→

**God blesses**

→

**creation flourishes.**

---

# 91. The Sixth Day and the Climax of Creation

The creation of land animals prepares for the creation of humanity.

---

# 92. Humanity's Unique Position

Humanity will be distinguished not by being the only living creature but by being made uniquely in God's image.

---

# 93. Shared Creatureliness

Humans will share with animals the reality of being living creatures.

Yet humanity will possess a unique image-bearing status.

---

# 94. Creaturely Dependence

Both humans and animals ultimately depend upon God for life.

---

# 95. God Gives Life

The source of life remains God.

The earth is the commanded instrument through which terrestrial creatures emerge.

---

# 96. Creation and Providence

The verse therefore points toward God's continuing providential relationship with the living world.

---

# 97. Stewardship Implications

Because terrestrial animals are God's creatures, later human dominion should be understood as responsibility exercised under God's authority.

---

# 98. Theological Summary

Genesis 1:24 introduces the terrestrial animal creation of Day Six.

God commands the earth to bring forth living creatures according to their kinds, identifying broad categories of land animals.

The earth responds according to God's command:

**“And it was so.”**

The verse therefore emphasizes:

**God's sovereignty**

**the diversity of terrestrial life**

**the order of creation**

**the productivity of the earth**

and

**the effectiveness of God's word.**

---

# 99. The Central Theological Proposition

If Genesis 1:24 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God commanded the earth to bring forth diverse forms of terrestrial animal life according to their kinds, and His command was immediately fulfilled.**

Or more simply:

**God sovereignly commanded the earth to produce ordered and diverse terrestrial life, and it happened according to His word.**

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:24

### **“And God said”**

→ Creation begins with God's authoritative command.

### **“Let the earth bring forth”**

→ The earth is commanded to produce terrestrial living creatures.

### **“Living creatures”**

→ The creatures belong to the category of living beings.

### **“According to their kinds”**

→ Terrestrial life possesses ordered diversity.

### **“Livestock”**

→ Larger or domestic-associated land animals.

### **“Creeping things”**

→ Creatures that move close to or along the ground.

### **“Beasts of the earth”**

→ Wild terrestrial animals.

### **“According to their kinds”**

→ The categories exist within God's ordered creation.

### **“And it was so”**

→ God's command is fulfilled.

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:24 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God is the Creator of terrestrial life** — land animals ultimately originate from God's creative command.
2. **God's word is authoritative** — His command produces the intended result.
3. **Divine sovereignty** — God rules over the earth and its creatures.
4. **The earth is God's creation** — the earth responds to God's command.
5. **Creaturely dependence** — terrestrial life depends upon God's creative ordering.
6. **Diversity of life** — the earth contains many kinds of creatures.
7. **Order within diversity** — creatures exist according to their kinds.
8. **The goodness of animal life** — the following verse declares the animals good.
9. **The productivity of creation** — the earth is capable of bringing forth life according to God's command.
10. **The forming-and-filling pattern** — Day Six begins filling the land established earlier.
11. **Divine wisdom** — terrestrial creatures are ordered within appropriate categories.
12. **Divine providence** — God establishes the conditions for terrestrial life.
13. **The Creator-creature distinction** — the earth and animals remain creatures rather than divine powers.
14. **Rejection of naturalistic autonomy** — the earth's productivity operates under God's sovereign command.
15. **Human stewardship** — humanity's later dominion concerns animals that already belong to God.
16. **The preparation of creation** — the land is populated before humanity appears.
17. **The progression of creation** — Day Six moves from land animals toward humanity.
18. **The unity of creation** — aquatic, aerial, and terrestrial creatures belong to the same created order.
19. **Divine faithfulness** — “and it was so” demonstrates the fulfillment of God's word.
20. **God-centered creation** — the ultimate focus remains on God as the sovereign Creator.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:24 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD COMMANDS THE EARTH

**“And God said, ‘Let the earth bring forth…’”**

The earth functions under God's sovereign authority.

---

## II. GOD CREATES ORDERED DIVERSITY

**“According to their kinds…”**

The terrestrial world is filled with many forms of life, yet God's creation possesses order.

---

## III. GOD'S WORD ACCOMPLISHES HIS PURPOSE

**“And it was so.”**

God speaks, and creation responds.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:24 teaches that God sovereignly commanded the earth to bring forth diverse terrestrial living creatures according to their kinds, and the earth responded exactly according to His word.**

The progression from Genesis 1:20 through Genesis 1:24 is:

**Genesis 1:20**

God commands:

**waters → swarm**

**birds → fly**

**Genesis 1:21**

God creates:

**sea creatures**

**winged creatures**

God declares:

**“it was good.”**

**Genesis 1:22**

God blesses:

**“Be fruitful.”**

**“Multiply.”**

**“Fill the waters.”**

**“Let birds multiply.”**

**Genesis 1:23**

The fifth day concludes:

**“There was evening.”**

**“There was morning.”**

**“The fifth day.”**

**Genesis 1:24**

The sixth day begins:

**God speaks.**

**The earth brings forth.**

**Living creatures appear.**

**They are ordered according to their kinds.**

**“And it was so.”**

The pattern is:

**God speaks.**

**Creation responds.**

**Life appears.**

**Creation is ordered.**

**God's purpose is accomplished.**
