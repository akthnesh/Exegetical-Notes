# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:22

**Genesis 1:22 —** “And God blessed them, saying, ‘Be fruitful and multiply and fill the waters in the seas, and let birds multiply on the earth.’”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיְבָרֶךְ אֹתָם אֱלֹהִים לֵאמֹר פְּרוּ וּרְבוּ וּמִלְאוּ אֶת־הַמַּיִם בַּיַּמִּים וְהָעוֹף יִרֶב בָּאָרֶץ׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayəḇāreḵ ʾōṯām ʾĕlōhîm lēʾmōr pərû ûrəḇû ûmilʾû ʾeṯ-hammayim bayyamîm wəhāʿôp̄ yireḇ bāʾāreṣ*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And God blessed them, saying, ‘Be fruitful and multiply and fill the waters in the seas, and let the winged creatures multiply on the earth.’”**

---

# 1. “And God blessed them” — וַיְבָרֶךְ אֹתָם אֱלֹהִים (*wayəḇāreḵ ʾōṯām ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:22 introduces an important development in the creation account:

**“And God blessed them.”**

The Hebrew verb:

**בָּרַךְ (*bāraḵ*)**

means:

**“to bless.”**

The object **“them”** refers to the living creatures God created in verse 21.

---

# 2. Creation is followed by blessing

The sequence is significant:

**Genesis 1:20 — God commands.**

**Genesis 1:21 — God creates.**

**Genesis 1:22 — God blesses.**

The creatures are not merely brought into existence.

God pronounces blessing upon them.

---

# 3. The first explicit blessing in Genesis

Genesis 1:22 contains the first explicit occurrence of God blessing something in the biblical narrative.

This is significant for understanding the biblical concept of blessing.

---

# 4. Blessing comes from God

The creatures do not bless themselves.

God blesses them.

The source of their fruitfulness is therefore ultimately God.

---

# 5. “Saying” — לֵאמֹר (*lēʾmōr*)

God's blessing is expressed through speech:

**“saying…”**

Once again, God's word accomplishes His purpose.

The pattern of divine speech continues.

---

# 6. The blessing is a command

The blessing contains commands concerning:

**fruitfulness**

**multiplication**

and

**filling their appointed realm.**

God's blessing is therefore active and productive.

---

# 7. “Be fruitful” — פְּרוּ (*pərû*)

The Hebrew verb:

**פָּרָה (*pārâ*)**

means:

**“to be fruitful”**

**“to bear fruit”**

or

**“to become fruitful.”**

The creatures are commanded to reproduce abundantly.

---

# 8. Fruitfulness

Fruitfulness refers to the capacity to produce offspring and increase in number.

God's blessing includes the continuation of life.

---

# 9. “And multiply” — וּרְבוּ (*ûrəḇû*)

The verb:

**רָבָה (*rāḇâ*)**

means:

**“to become many”**

**“to increase”**

or

**“to multiply.”**

The creatures are commanded not merely to reproduce but to increase abundantly.

---

# 10. Fruitfulness and multiplication

The two commands are closely related:

**“Be fruitful”**

→ produce offspring.

**“Multiply”**

→ increase in number.

Together they describe population growth and flourishing.

---

# 11. God's blessing produces abundance

The command to multiply corresponds naturally with the command in verse 20:

**“Let the waters swarm…”**

Verse 20 introduces abundance.

Verse 22 gives the blessing and command that sustain that abundance.

---

# 12. “Fill the waters” — וּמִלְאוּ אֶת־הַמַּיִם (*ûmilʾû ʾeṯ-hammayim*)

The creatures are commanded:

**“fill the waters.”**

The Hebrew verb:

**מָלֵא (*mālēʾ*)**

means:

**“to fill”**

**“to be full”**

or

**“to fill up.”**

---

# 13. The waters are to become full of life

The waters were already populated in verse 21.

Now God commands the creatures to multiply until the waters are filled with life.

---

# 14. Filling the created realm

The command corresponds to the larger structure of Genesis 1.

God first establishes the realm.

Then He fills it.

The waters are filled by the creatures God created.

---

# 15. “In the seas” — בַּיַּמִּים (*bayyamîm*)

The Hebrew noun:

**יָם (*yām*)**

means:

**“sea”**

or

**“large body of water.”**

The plural:

**יַּמִּים (*yammîm*)**

refers to:

**“seas.”**

---

# 16. The seas belong to God

The seas are not independent realms outside God's authority.

They are part of His creation and are populated according to His command.

---

# 17. Aquatic life is blessed

The blessing specifically concerns the creatures inhabiting the waters.

They are commanded to multiply and fill the seas.

---

# 18. “And let birds multiply” — וְהָעוֹף יִרֶב (*wəhāʿôp̄ yireḇ*)

The second group addressed is:

**the birds / flying creatures.**

The Hebrew noun:

**עוֹף (*ʿôp̄*)**

can refer to birds or flying creatures generally.

---

# 19. The birds are also blessed

The blessing is not restricted to aquatic life.

The winged creatures also receive a command to multiply.

---

# 20. Multiplication on the earth

The text says:

**“let birds multiply on the earth.”**

The birds occupy the aerial realm but reproduce upon the earth.

---

# 21. The earth becomes filled with life

The fifth day therefore contributes to the increasing fullness of the earth.

The waters and skies become populated with living creatures.

---

# 22. The relationship between creation and reproduction

God creates the creatures in verse 21.

God then blesses them with reproductive fruitfulness in verse 22.

Creation and reproduction are therefore distinguished.

God creates the original life.

The creatures subsequently reproduce according to the blessing God gives.

---

# 23. God is the source of reproductive capacity

The ability of living creatures to multiply is presented as part of God's blessing.

---

# 24. Reproduction is good

Genesis places reproduction within God's original good creation.

Fruitfulness is not presented negatively.

It is explicitly connected with divine blessing.

---

# 25. The goodness of multiplication

The multiplication of living creatures belongs to God's good purpose for creation.

The increasing abundance of life is a blessing.

---

# 26. God's blessing is fruitful

The blessing produces practical consequences:

**fruitfulness**

**multiplication**

**filling**

These are not abstract concepts.

They describe the flourishing of life.

---

# 27. The blessing of life

The creatures are blessed with the capacity to continue and expand their populations.

God's blessing therefore includes the continuation of life across generations.

---

# 28. The first command concerning multiplication

Genesis 1:22 is the first explicit divine command concerning multiplication in the Bible.

The principle will later be applied to humanity.

---

# 29. The parallel with humanity

The same language will appear in Genesis 1:28 when God blesses humanity:

**“Be fruitful and multiply.”**

This creates a significant parallel between animal life and human life.

---

# 30. Animal blessing precedes human blessing

God first blesses the creatures of the fifth day.

He later blesses humanity.

The blessing of fruitfulness is therefore already established within creation before humanity receives it.

---

# 31. The blessing of humanity

Genesis 1:28 will contain:

**“And God blessed them.”**

The phrase intentionally echoes Genesis 1:22.

---

# 32. Shared blessing, different vocation

Animals and humanity both receive blessing involving fruitfulness and multiplication.

However, humanity will additionally receive the distinctive command to exercise dominion.

---

# 33. Fruitfulness is not uniquely human

The ability to reproduce belongs to the broader created order.

Humanity shares this aspect of creaturely life.

---

# 34. Humanity's uniqueness is elsewhere

Human uniqueness is not based merely upon reproduction.

Genesis 1:26–28 identifies humanity as uniquely made in God's image.

---

# 35. The blessing of creation

God's blessing demonstrates that His purpose for creation includes flourishing.

The living world is intended to increase and fill the environments God has prepared.

---

# 36. Creation is designed for fullness

The command:

**“fill the waters”**

shows that God's created realms are intended to become abundantly inhabited.

---

# 37. The filling pattern continues

The larger structure is:

**Day Two → waters and expanse established**

**Day Five → waters and expanse filled**

Genesis 1:22 continues that filling pattern through multiplication.

---

# 38. Forming and filling

The creation account can therefore be understood in terms of:

**forming**

and

**filling.**

God forms the environments.

God fills them with creatures.

God blesses those creatures so they may continue filling them.

---

# 39. God's provision for continuation

God provides not only the initial creation of life but also the means by which life continues.

---

# 40. Divine providence

The reproductive capacity of creatures is part of God's providential ordering of creation.

---

# 41. Generational continuity

The command to multiply establishes a world in which life continues across generations.

---

# 42. God's blessing extends through generations

The creatures' offspring will continue the life God established.

Thus the blessing has an ongoing effect.

---

# 43. The blessing and biological processes

Genesis does not explain the biological mechanisms of reproduction.

Its emphasis is theological:

**God blesses creatures with fruitfulness and multiplication.**

---

# 44. Divine sovereignty and creaturely activity

God blesses.

The creatures reproduce.

This demonstrates both divine sovereignty and creaturely participation.

---

# 45. God commands; creatures act

The pattern is:

**God blesses and commands.**

**Creatures reproduce.**

The creatures genuinely act, but their capacity to do so exists within God's created order.

---

# 46. God's sovereignty does not eliminate creaturely activity

The command to multiply assumes that creatures will actively reproduce.

Divine sovereignty and creaturely activity operate together.

---

# 47. God's blessing is not passive

The blessing contains a purpose:

**be fruitful**

**multiply**

**fill.**

It is a blessing that produces flourishing.

---

# 48. Abundance rather than scarcity

Genesis presents the original created order as moving toward abundance.

The waters are to be filled.

The earth is to become populated with birds.

---

# 49. God's generosity

The abundance of life reflects God's generosity in creation.

He creates a world rich in living things.

---

# 50. God's delight in life

God's declaration that animal life is good in verse 21, followed by blessing in verse 22, demonstrates positive divine affirmation of living creation.

---

# 51. Creation is blessed before the fall

Genesis 1:22 occurs before Genesis 3.

The blessing belongs to the original creation order.

---

# 52. The blessing precedes sin

This is significant for biblical theology.

Fruitfulness and multiplication are originally associated with God's good creation, not with humanity's fallen condition.

---

# 53. The goodness of reproduction

The reproductive order is part of God's original design.

---

# 54. The blessing of natural life

Genesis 1:22 affirms the goodness of natural processes within God's created order.

---

# 55. Life has an outward orientation

The command to multiply means that living creatures do not exist merely for individual survival.

They participate in the continuation of life.

---

# 56. Creation is generative

God creates a world capable of producing further generations of life.

---

# 57. God's creative economy

God establishes a pattern in which His original creative work generates continuing creaturely life.

---

# 58. The seas and the earth

The verse mentions two domains:

**the seas**

and

**the earth.**

The blessing therefore extends across multiple realms of creation.

---

# 59. Aquatic and aerial creatures

The fifth-day creatures are divided broadly into:

**aquatic creatures**

and

**winged creatures.**

Both receive divine blessing.

---

# 60. The seas are filled by multiplication

The creatures of the waters are commanded to fill their environment.

---

# 61. Birds multiply on the earth

The birds likewise increase in number.

Their multiplication contributes to the filling of the earth.

---

# 62. God's command corresponds to habitat

The blessing is specifically suited to each group:

**aquatic creatures → fill the waters**

**birds → multiply on the earth**

---

# 63. Divine wisdom in the blessing

God's commands correspond to the created nature and habitat of each creature.

---

# 64. The order of creation continues

The fifth day has:

**creation**

then

**blessing**

then

**multiplication.**

The narrative maintains its orderly progression.

---

# 65. The blessing of fruitfulness anticipates humanity

Genesis 1:22 prepares readers for the similar language of Genesis 1:28.

When humanity receives the command:

**“Be fruitful and multiply,”**

it is receiving a command already given to the animal creation.

---

# 66. The distinction of humanity

Although humanity shares the command to multiply, humans also receive a unique vocation:

**to fill the earth**

**subdue it**

and

**have dominion.**

---

# 67. Dominion is added to fruitfulness

For animals:

**be fruitful**

**multiply**

**fill.**

For humanity:

**be fruitful**

**multiply**

**fill the earth**

**subdue**

**have dominion.**

This highlights humanity's distinctive role.

---

# 68. The blessing of life and the mission of humanity

The multiplication of creatures contributes to the fullness of the world into which humanity will be placed.

---

# 69. Humanity enters a flourishing creation

By the time humanity is created, the world is increasingly populated with life.

---

# 70. God's preparation of the world

Genesis presents God as preparing a world suitable for human habitation.

The seas and skies are already filled with life before humanity appears.

---

# 71. The goodness of a populated creation

The populated world is part of the good creation God is making.

---

# 72. Divine blessing and ecological abundance

The command to fill the seas points toward an abundant ecosystem.

The created world is intended to sustain extensive life.

---

# 73. Theological basis for stewardship

Because multiplication and life are presented as divine blessings, humanity's later responsibility toward animals should involve protection rather than needless destruction.

---

# 74. Animals belong to God's good creation

They are not disposable objects.

They are creatures God created and blessed.

---

# 75. God's care for creatures

The blessing demonstrates that God has a purpose for the continuation of animal life.

---

# 76. The Creator's generosity toward non-human creatures

God blesses animals before humanity is created.

This demonstrates that God's concern for creation is not exclusively anthropocentric.

---

# 77. Creation has value before human use

The animals are blessed and declared good before any human being exists to use them.

---

# 78. Creaturely value

Their value ultimately comes from God's creative purpose rather than merely from their usefulness to humanity.

---

# 79. The blessing and divine goodness

God's blessing reveals His generosity toward His creatures.

---

# 80. The blessing and divine sovereignty

God determines the terms of creaturely flourishing.

---

# 81. The blessing and divine providence

God establishes the means by which life continues from generation to generation.

---

# 82. The blessing and divine wisdom

Different creatures are commanded to flourish within appropriate environments.

---

# 83. The blessing and divine purpose

The created world is moving toward fullness according to God's design.

---

# 84. Creation is dynamic

The world God creates is not static.

It contains processes of:

**growth**

**multiplication**

**generation**

and

**filling.**

---

# 85. God's creation has movement toward fullness

Genesis 1 portrays creation as progressively moving toward greater fullness and habitation.

---

# 86. The blessing is communal

The command to multiply necessarily concerns populations rather than isolated individuals.

---

# 87. Life reproduces life

The blessing creates continuity between generations.

---

# 88. God's design includes generations

The command to multiply implies an ordered succession of living generations.

---

# 89. The blessing is future-oriented

Genesis 1:22 looks beyond the creatures currently existing toward their future offspring.

---

# 90. God's blessing extends beyond the immediate moment

The command establishes a continuing pattern of flourishing.

---

# 91. The abundance of the sea

The sea is to become increasingly full of life.

This anticipates the remarkable abundance of aquatic creation described throughout Scripture.

---

# 92. The abundance of birds

The birds likewise increase across the earth.

The aerial realm becomes increasingly populated.

---

# 93. God's creation is fruitful

Fruitfulness is embedded into the structure of God's created order.

---

# 94. Blessing and responsibility

The blessing ultimately reminds humanity that life belongs to God's purposes.

Human stewardship should respect the Creator's blessing upon living creatures.

---

# 95. The relationship to Psalm 104

Psalm 104 later celebrates God's provision for animals and the abundance of life within creation.

Genesis 1:22 provides foundational language for understanding creaturely flourishing as part of God's providence.

---

# 96. The relationship to Psalm 147

Psalm 147 celebrates God's provision for His creation.

The biblical picture remains one in which God sustains the living world He created.

---

# 97. The relationship to Acts 17

Acts 17:25 teaches that God gives:

**“life and breath and everything.”**

Genesis 1 provides the foundational creation account behind this understanding.

---

# 98. The relationship to Matthew 6

Jesus teaches that God cares for the creatures of creation.

This reflects the broader biblical understanding that God remains sovereignly involved with His created world.

---

# 99. The relationship to Matthew 10

Jesus teaches that even sparrows are known within God's providence.

Genesis 1:22 provides the foundational truth that these creatures exist within God's created and blessed order.

---

# 100. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:22 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God blessed the creatures He had created, granting them fruitfulness and multiplication so that they would fill the waters and increase throughout the earth.**

Or more simply:

**God blessed His living creatures with the capacity to multiply and fill the realms He had prepared for them.**

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:22

### **“And God blessed them”**

→ God graciously bestows blessing upon the living creatures He created.

### **“Be fruitful”**

→ The creatures are commanded to reproduce.

### **“And multiply”**

→ Their populations are to increase.

### **“And fill the waters in the seas”**

→ Aquatic life is to populate the seas abundantly.

### **“And let birds multiply on the earth”**

→ Flying creatures are likewise commanded to increase throughout the earth.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:22 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **Divine blessing** — God blesses His creatures.
2. **God as the source of fruitfulness** — reproductive capacity exists within God's blessing.
3. **Multiplication of life** — God commands living creatures to increase.
4. **Goodness of reproduction** — fruitfulness belongs to God's original good creation.
5. **Abundance** — the seas are intended to become filled with life.
6. **Divine providence** — God establishes the continuation of life through generations.
7. **Creaturely activity** — creatures genuinely reproduce within God's sovereign ordering.
8. **Divine sovereignty** — God determines the purpose and pattern of creaturely flourishing.
9. **Ordered creation** — different creatures flourish within their appropriate environments.
10. **Goodness of animal life** — the creatures are both created and blessed by God.
11. **Value of non-human creation** — animals possess creaturely value before humanity appears.
12. **Creation care** — God's blessing provides a theological foundation for responsible stewardship.
13. **Generational continuity** — life is designed to continue across generations.
14. **Fruitfulness as divine purpose** — God's creation is designed for flourishing and increase.
15. **Preparation for humanity** — the blessing of animal life precedes humanity's creation.
16. **Humanity's shared creatureliness** — humans will share the command to be fruitful and multiply.
17. **Humanity's unique vocation** — unlike animals, humanity will additionally receive dominion.
18. **Divine generosity** — God provides abundance rather than merely bare existence.
19. **God's providential care** — the continued flourishing of creation depends upon God's ordering.
20. **God's glory** — the abundance and diversity of life display the generosity and wisdom of the Creator.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:22 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD BLESSES WHAT HE CREATES

**“And God blessed them…”**

The creatures of the sea and sky are not merely created; they receive God's blessing.

---

## II. GOD DESIGNS CREATION FOR FRUITFULNESS

**“Be fruitful and multiply…”**

The continuation and increase of life are part of God's original good design.

---

## III. GOD INTENDS HIS CREATION TO FLOURISH

**“Fill the waters…”**

**“Let birds multiply…”**

God's blessing moves creation toward abundance and fullness.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:22 teaches that God blessed the living creatures He created, commanding them to be fruitful and multiply so that the seas and earth would become abundantly filled with life.**

The progression from Genesis 1:20 through Genesis 1:22 is:

**Genesis 1:20**

God commands:

**waters → swarm**

**birds → fly**

**Genesis 1:21**

God creates:

**sea creatures**

**living aquatic creatures**

**winged creatures**

God declares:

**“it was good.”**

**Genesis 1:22**

God blesses:

**“Be fruitful.”**

**“Multiply.”**

**“Fill the waters.”**

**“Let birds multiply.”**

The pattern is:

**God commands.**

**God creates.**

**God evaluates.**

**God blesses.**

**God gives fruitfulness.**

**God moves creation toward abundance.**
