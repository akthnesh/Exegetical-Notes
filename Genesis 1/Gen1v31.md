# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:31

**Genesis 1:31 —** God sees everything He has made, and behold, it is very good. Evening and morning mark the sixth day.

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים אֶת־כָּל־אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה וְהִנֵּה־טוֹב מְאֹד וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר יוֹם הַשִּׁשִּׁי׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm ʾeṯ-kol-ʾăšer ʿāśāh wəhinnēh-ṭôḇ məʾōḏ wayəhî-ʿereḇ wayəhî-ḇōqer yôm haššiššî*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And God saw all that He had made, and behold, it was very good. And there was evening, and there was morning—the sixth day.”**

---

# 1. “And God Saw” — וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים (*wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm*)

The verse begins with:

**“And God saw…”**

The Hebrew verb:

**רָאָה (*rāʾâ*)**

means:

**“to see”**

**“to look at”**

or

**“to perceive.”**

God evaluates the completed work of creation.

---

# 2. Divine Evaluation

The phrase is more than a statement that God visually observed creation.

Within the context, God's seeing introduces His evaluation of what He has made.

---

# 3. “All That He Had Made”

The Hebrew expression:

**אֶת־כָּל־אֲשֶׁר עָשָׂה**

means:

**“all that He had made.”**

The verb:

**עָשָׂה (*ʿāśâ*)**

means:

**“to make”**

**“to do”**

or

**“to accomplish.”**

---

# 4. The Scope Is Comprehensive

Earlier evaluations repeatedly concerned individual stages of creation.

Genesis 1:31 now evaluates:

**“all that He had made.”**

The assessment encompasses the completed creation.

---

# 5. Creation as a Whole

God does not merely evaluate isolated parts.

He evaluates the whole created order.

---

# 6. The Final Evaluation

This verse functions as the climactic evaluation of the six days of creation.

The preceding verses have progressively established the created order.

Now God evaluates the whole.

---

# 7. “And Behold” — וְהִנֵּה (*wəhinnēh*)

The expression:

**הִנֵּה (*hinnēh*)**

means:

**“behold”**

**“look”**

or

**“indeed.”**

It draws special attention to the evaluation that follows.

---

# 8. “Very Good” — טוֹב מְאֹד (*ṭôḇ məʾōḏ*)

The central declaration is:

**טוֹב מְאֹד**

meaning:

**“very good.”**

The word:

**טוֹב (*ṭôḇ*)**

means:

**“good.”**

The word:

**מְאֹד (*məʾōḏ*)**

means:

**“very”**

**“exceedingly”**

or

**“greatly.”**

Together:

**“very good.”**

---

# 9. The Earlier “Good”

Throughout Genesis 1, God repeatedly evaluates His creation as:

**“good.”**

Genesis 1:31 intensifies this evaluation.

---

# 10. From “Good” to “Very Good”

The progression is significant.

Individual aspects of creation are described as:

**good.**

The completed creation is described as:

**very good.**

---

# 11. The Climax of God's Evaluation

The phrase **“very good”** represents the climax of the repeated goodness declarations throughout Genesis 1.

---

# 12. Creation Is Fundamentally Good

Genesis 1 establishes the fundamental goodness of God's created order.

Creation originates from God and receives His positive evaluation.

---

# 13. The Creator's Judgment

The standard of evaluation belongs to God.

God declares creation good.

---

# 14. God's Goodness and Creation

The goodness of creation ultimately reflects the wisdom and goodness of its Creator.

---

# 15. Creation Is Not Evil

Genesis 1 provides no basis for viewing the material creation as inherently evil.

God Himself declares the completed creation:

**“very good.”**

---

# 16. Matter Is Good

The physical world belongs to God's good creation.

The earth, vegetation, animals, and humanity all belong to the created order God evaluates.

---

# 17. Humanity Is Included

The evaluation occurs immediately after humanity's creation.

Humanity is therefore included within:

**“all that He had made.”**

---

# 18. Human Nature in Creation

Genesis 1:31 means that humanity, as originally created by God, belongs to the good created order.

---

# 19. Humanity's Original Goodness

The text describes humanity before the Fall.

The later entrance of sin should not be projected backward into the original evaluation.

---

# 20. The Fall Has Not Yet Occurred

Genesis 3 has not yet happened.

The declaration:

**“very good”**

therefore describes the creation before human rebellion.

---

# 21. Sin Is Not Part of the Original Creation

The narrative has not yet introduced sin, death, or the curse.

The original created order is evaluated as very good.

---

# 22. “Very Good” Does Not Mean God Himself

Creation is good, but creation is not God.

Genesis maintains the distinction between:

**Creator**

and

**creation.**

---

# 23. Creator-Creation Distinction

God evaluates creation as good because He is its Creator.

Creation remains dependent upon Him.

---

# 24. Creation Is Not Divine

The goodness of creation does not make creation worthy of worship.

Worship belongs to the Creator.

---

# 25. The Goodness of the Natural World

Genesis gives a theological foundation for recognizing the goodness of the natural world.

---

# 26. The Earth Is Good

The earth is part of God's good creation.

---

# 27. Animals Are Good

The living creatures created by God belong to the good created order.

---

# 28. Vegetation Is Good

The vegetation provided for humanity and animals is part of the good creation.

---

# 29. Humanity Is Good as Created

Humanity's original created condition is included in God's declaration of goodness.

---

# 30. God's Evaluation Is Comprehensive

The phrase:

**“all that He had made”**

prevents the evaluation from being restricted to one category.

---

# 31. The Whole Creation

The complete created order is included:

**heavens**

**earth**

**seas**

**vegetation**

**sun**

**moon**

**stars**

**animals**

**humanity.**

---

# 32. Order and Goodness

The goodness of creation is connected to the ordered structure God established throughout Genesis 1.

---

# 33. God's Wisdom in Creation

Creation demonstrates order, distinction, fruitfulness, provision, and purpose.

God evaluates the completed result as very good.

---

# 34. Creation Is Functional

The various parts of creation have assigned purposes.

The completed system is evaluated as very good.

---

# 35. Creation and Flourishing

The creation account presents an environment capable of sustaining abundant life.

---

# 36. The Goodness of Life

Life itself is presented positively throughout Genesis 1.

God creates living creatures and blesses them.

---

# 37. Fruitfulness Is Good

God blesses the living creatures and commands humanity to multiply.

The resulting flourishing belongs within the good creation.

---

# 38. Human Dominion Is Good in Its Original Form

The dominion given to humanity in Genesis 1:28 belongs to the creation God evaluates as very good.

---

# 39. Dominion Before the Fall

Human authority over creation therefore precedes sin.

The problem with sinful domination arises later, not from the original mandate itself.

---

# 40. Work Before the Fall

The human vocation to cultivate and govern creation belongs to God's original good design.

---

# 41. The Goodness of Human Vocation

Human beings are created with purpose.

Their creational calling is part of the very good creation.

---

# 42. The Goodness of Human Community

Humanity is created male and female.

Their existence together belongs within the good created order.

---

# 43. Male and Female

Genesis 1:27 explicitly describes humanity as:

**male and female.**

Genesis 1:31 places that creation within the overall declaration:

**“very good.”**

---

# 44. The Goodness of Created Distinctions

Genesis 1 repeatedly establishes distinctions:

**light and darkness**

**waters above and below**

**sea and dry land**

**day and night**

**kinds of living creatures**

**male and female.**

The creation account presents these distinctions within God's good order.

---

# 45. Distinction Does Not Mean Defect

The existence of distinctions within creation does not imply inequality in value.

God declares the completed order very good.

---

# 46. Creation and Order

Genesis 1 portrays God as bringing order to the created world.

The completed order receives His approval.

---

# 47. “All That He Had Made”

The phrase points backward across the entire chapter.

Every stage has contributed to the final result.

---

# 48. The Six Days Reach Their Goal

The six-day creation sequence reaches its climactic evaluation here.

---

# 49. The Seventh Day Is Approaching

The next chapter division begins with the seventh day.

Genesis 1:31 therefore serves as the conclusion to the six-day creation work.

---

# 50. The Sixth Day

The verse ends:

**“the sixth day.”**

The Hebrew:

**יוֹם הַשִּׁשִּׁי (*yôm haššiššî*)**

means:

**“the sixth day.”**

---

# 51. “Day” — יוֹם (*yôm*)

The word:

**יוֹם (*yôm*)**

means:

**“day.”**

Genesis 1 has repeatedly used the day-and-night framework to structure the creation account.

---

# 52. Evening and Morning

The verse repeats the formula:

**“there was evening, and there was morning.”**

This formula has appeared throughout the creation account.

---

# 53. Creation-Day Structure

The repeated pattern is:

**evening**

→

**morning**

→

**day.**

---

# 54. The Sixth Day Completes the Pattern

The sixth day contains the creation of humanity and the final provision described in the preceding verses.

---

# 55. Humanity as the Climax of the Six Days

Humanity is created toward the conclusion of the creation sequence.

Genesis 1:31 then evaluates the completed creation.

---

# 56. The Sixth Day Is Not the End of the Story

The seventh day follows.

Creation is complete, but God's relationship to creation continues.

---

# 57. Preparation for Genesis 2

Genesis 2:1–3 will describe the completion of the heavens and earth and God's seventh-day rest.

---

# 58. Creation and Sabbath

Genesis 1:31 prepares for the transition from:

**creation**

to

**rest.**

---

# 59. God's Completed Work

The phrase:

**“all that He had made”**

indicates the completion of the creation work described in the six days.

---

# 60. Divine Satisfaction

God's declaration of “very good” communicates the complete suitability of creation according to His purpose.

---

# 61. “Very Good” Is God's Verdict

The goodness of creation is not merely humanity's opinion.

It is God's own evaluation.

---

# 62. Objective Goodness

Within the theological framework of Genesis, creation's goodness is grounded in God's declaration.

---

# 63. God's Authority to Define Good

The Creator has the authority to evaluate His creation.

This becomes important later when humanity attempts to determine good and evil independently of God.

---

# 64. Genesis 1 and Genesis 3

There is a striking theological contrast:

**Genesis 1**

God evaluates creation as **very good.**

**Genesis 3**

humanity disobeys God's command and experiences the consequences of sin.

---

# 65. God's Definition of Good

Genesis 1 establishes God's evaluation before humanity's rebellion.

---

# 66. Creation and Morality

The declaration of goodness is theological and comprehensive.

It does not mean every later human action within creation is automatically good.

---

# 67. Created Goodness and Human Sin

Creation itself is good, while human sin later distorts the use of creation.

---

# 68. Distortion Rather Than Original Defect

The Fall introduces corruption into an originally good creation.

---

# 69. Redemption Presupposes Creation's Goodness

The biblical story of redemption ultimately assumes the value of God's creation.

God's redemptive purposes do not require abandoning creation.

---

# 70. New Creation

The biblical storyline eventually moves toward restoration and renewal of creation.

Genesis 1 therefore establishes the foundational goodness of the created order.

---

# 71. The Goodness of the Material World

This has significant theological implications.

Christian theology should not treat the physical world as inherently evil merely because it is material.

---

# 72. Body and Creation

Human bodily existence belongs to God's original good creation.

---

# 73. Food and Creation

The food provision in Genesis 1:29 belongs to the creation God declares very good.

---

# 74. Animal Life and Creation

The animal world belongs to the same good creation.

---

# 75. Work and Creation

Human labor and dominion belong to the original vocation within the good creation.

---

# 76. Human Culture and Creation

The development of human culture can be understood as an extension of humanity's original creational calling.

---

# 77. Creation and Stewardship

Because creation is good and belongs to God, humanity should treat it responsibly.

---

# 78. Dominion and Care

Human dominion should reflect God's own evaluation of creation as good.

---

# 79. Creation Is Valuable Because God Made It

The value of creation ultimately derives from God as Creator.

---

# 80. God's Ownership

The Creator has rightful authority over what He has made.

---

# 81. Humanity's Stewardship

Human beings exercise delegated authority within God's creation.

---

# 82. God's Final Evaluation

The verse brings together the themes of Genesis 1:

**God creates**

**God orders**

**God separates**

**God fills**

**God blesses**

**God provides**

**God evaluates.**

---

# 83. The Pattern of Genesis 1

The chapter repeatedly demonstrates:

**divine command**

→

**creative accomplishment**

→

**divine evaluation.**

Genesis 1:31 provides the climactic evaluation.

---

# 84. God's Word and Creation

The creation exists because God speaks and acts.

---

# 85. God's Wisdom and Creation

The created order is structured and purposeful.

---

# 86. God's Goodness and Creation

The completed creation is declared:

**“very good.”**

---

# 87. Creation Is Complete

The sixth day concludes the creation work described in Genesis 1.

---

# 88. The Final “Good”

The movement from repeated **“good”** declarations to **“very good”** signals completion and climax.

---

# 89. “Very Good” and Completion

The phrase does not merely repeat previous evaluations.

It gathers them together into a comprehensive final verdict.

---

# 90. The Creator's Approval

God approves the completed work of His creation.

---

# 91. God's Work Is Worthy of His Approval

The verse portrays the Creator as satisfied with the completed order.

---

# 92. The Goodness of God's Purposes

The creation fulfills the purposes established by God's commands.

---

# 93. Creation Is Purposeful

The repeated descriptions of creation's functions show that each part serves a purpose within God's order.

---

# 94. Humanity's Place in the Whole

Humanity is important, but humanity is not the Creator.

Humans occupy a unique position within God's creation.

---

# 95. Human Dominion Within a Good Creation

The authority given to humanity must therefore be exercised within the boundaries of God's good order.

---

# 96. Creation and Worship

The proper response to God's good creation is not worship of creation but worship of the Creator.

---

# 97. Gratitude

The goodness of creation provides reason for gratitude.

---

# 98. Hope

The declaration of original goodness also provides the foundation for understanding the biblical hope of restoration.

---

# 99. The Central Theological Proposition

If Genesis 1:31 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God looked upon the entire creation He had made, including humanity, and declared it very good, bringing the six-day creation account to its climactic conclusion.**

Or more simply:

**God's completed creation was very good, perfectly reflecting His purposeful and ordered creative work.**

---

### **“God saw”**

→ God evaluates His completed work.

### **“All that He had made”**

→ The evaluation encompasses the whole creation.

### **“Behold”**

→ The text draws attention to God's verdict.

### **“Very good”**

→ The completed creation receives God's highest positive evaluation in the chapter.

### **“There was evening”**

→ The established creation-day pattern continues.

### **“There was morning”**

→ The sixth day reaches its completion.

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:31 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God is the Creator** — everything in the created order ultimately originates from Him.
2. **God evaluates creation as good** — creation's goodness is grounded in God's own verdict.
3. **The completed creation is very good** — the final evaluation intensifies the earlier declarations of goodness.
4. **Material creation is good** — the physical world is not inherently evil.
5. **Humanity belongs to God's good creation** — humanity is included within “all that He had made.”
6. **Humanity's original condition was good** — the declaration occurs before the Fall.
7. **Sin is not part of the original creation order** — the Fall has not yet occurred.
8. **Creation is distinct from God** — creation is good without being divine.
9. **God has authority to define and evaluate goodness** — the Creator gives the final verdict on His creation.
10. **Creation is ordered** — the six-day account demonstrates purposeful structure.
11. **Creation is purposeful** — the various parts of creation function within God's design.
12. **Human dominion belongs to the good creation** — humanity's original rule over creation predates sin.
13. **Human vocation belongs to the good creation** — fruitfulness, cultivation, and dominion are established before the Fall.
14. **Male and female belong to the good creation** — their created distinction appears within the context of God's very good creation.
15. **Human embodiment is good** — physical human existence belongs to God's original design.
16. **Animal life is good** — living creatures belong to the creation God approves.
17. **Vegetation is good** — God's provision of plants and food belongs to the good created order.
18. **Creation is worthy of responsible stewardship** — humanity's dominion operates within God's good creation.
19. **Humanity remains creaturely** — being God's image-bearer does not make humanity divine.
20. **God's word accomplishes His purposes** — the completed creation corresponds to His commands.
21. **The creation account has a defined sequence** — evening and morning mark the completion of the sixth day.
22. **The six-day creation account reaches its climax** — Genesis 1:31 provides the comprehensive final evaluation.
23. **The seventh day follows the completed work** — Genesis 1:31 prepares for the Sabbath pattern of Genesis 2:1–3.
24. **Creation and redemption belong to a larger biblical storyline** — the original goodness of creation provides the foundation for understanding the later effects of sin and the hope of restoration.
25. **God's creative work is worthy of praise** — His wisdom, power, goodness, and providential care are displayed throughout creation.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

## I. GOD SAW ALL THAT HE HAD MADE

**“God saw all that He had made…”**

God evaluates the completed work of creation as a whole.

---

## II. GOD DECLARED CREATION VERY GOOD

**“Behold, it was very good.”**

Creation is fundamentally good because it comes from the wise and purposeful work of its Creator.

---

## III. HUMANITY BELONGS TO THIS GOOD CREATION

Human beings, created in God's image as male and female, are included within God's comprehensive declaration of goodness.

---

## IV. THE SIXTH DAY WAS COMPLETE

**“There was evening, and there was morning—the sixth day.”**

The six-day creation sequence reaches its conclusion and prepares for the seventh-day rest.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:31 teaches that God evaluated the entire creation He had made—including humanity—and declared it very good, bringing the six-day creation account to its climactic completion.**

The progression from Genesis 1:27 to Genesis 1:31 is:

**Genesis 1:27**

God creates humanity:

**in His image**

**male and female.**

**Genesis 1:28**

God:

**blesses humanity**

and gives humanity its creation mandate.

**Genesis 1:29**

God provides:

**food for humanity.**

**Genesis 1:30**

God provides:

**food for the animals.**

**Genesis 1:31**

God looks upon:

**all that He had made**

and declares:

**“very good.”**

The pattern is:

**God creates.**

**God orders.**

**God blesses.**

**God provides.**

**God evaluates.**

**God declares the completed creation very good.**

**The sixth day concludes.**
