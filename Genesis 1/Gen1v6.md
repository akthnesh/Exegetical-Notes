# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:6

**Genesis 1:6 —** “And God said, ‘Let there be an expanse in the midst of the waters, and let it separate the waters from the waters.’”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים יְהִי רָקִיעַ בְּתוֹךְ הַמָּיִם וִיהִי מַבְדִּיל בֵּין מַיִם לָמָיִם**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm yəhî rāqîaʿ bəṯôḵ ham-māyim wîhî mavdîl bên mayim lāmāyim*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And God said, ‘Let there be an expanse in the midst of the waters, and let it be separating between waters and waters.’”**

---

# 1. “And God said” — וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים (*wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:6 begins with the same important formula introduced in Genesis 1:3:

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים**

*Wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm*

**“And God said…”**

This repeated expression emphasizes the authority of God's Word in the creation account.

In Genesis 1:3:

**God said → light appeared.**

In Genesis 1:6:

**God said → an expanse is established.**

The creation narrative repeatedly presents God's speech as the means by which He orders His creation.

The pattern is therefore becoming unmistakable:

**God speaks → God commands → creation responds.**

---

# 2. “Let there be” — יְהִי (*yəhî*)

The divine command begins:

**יְהִי**

*yəhî*

**“Let there be.”**

The verb comes from **היה (*h-y-h*)**, meaning **“to be,” “to become,” or “to come to pass.”**

The same verb appeared in Genesis 1:3:

**“Let there be light.”**

The repetition connects the second day directly to the first.

God continues to bring His creation into ordered existence through His sovereign Word.

---

# 3. “An expanse” — רָקִיעַ (*rāqîaʿ*)

The central noun in Genesis 1:6 is:

**רָקִיעַ (*rāqîaʿ*)**

Traditionally translated:

**“firmament”**

and in the ESV:

**“expanse.”**

The word is related to the Hebrew root **רקע (*r-q-ʿ*)**, associated with the idea of spreading out, stretching out, or extending something.

In Genesis 1, the **rāqîaʿ** is the expanse that God establishes within the waters and later associates with the heavens.

The term therefore describes part of the ordered structure God establishes in creation.

---

# 4. The “firmament” or “expanse”

Older English translations commonly use the word:

**“firmament.”**

Modern translations often use:

**“expanse.”**

The important exegetical point is that Genesis 1 presents the **rāqîaʿ** as something established by God to perform a separating function.

Verse 6 itself defines its immediate purpose:

**“Let it separate the waters from the waters.”**

Therefore, regardless of the translation chosen, the central function in this verse is **separation**.

God establishes an expanse that distinguishes one body of waters from another.

---

# 5. “In the midst of the waters” — בְּתוֹךְ הַמָּיִם (*bəṯôḵ ham-māyim*)

The next phrase is:

**בְּתוֹךְ הַמָּיִם**

*bəṯôḵ ham-māyim*

**“in the midst of the waters.”**

The noun:

**מַיִם (*mayim*)**

means:

**“water.”**

Hebrew normally uses this word in a plural form.

The phrase indicates that the expanse is established **within** the waters.

This continues the description of the waters introduced in Genesis 1:2.

The creation narrative is therefore progressing from the watery, unstructured condition toward a more ordered environment.

---

# 6. The waters of Genesis 1

Water has been present since Genesis 1:2:

**“And the Spirit of God was hovering over the face of the waters.”**

Genesis 1:6 now returns to the waters.

The sequence is significant:

**Genesis 1:2 — waters**

**Genesis 1:6 — expanse established among the waters**

**Genesis 1:7 — waters divided**

**Genesis 1:9 — waters gathered**

The waters therefore become an important part of God's ordering activity during creation.

God does not leave the waters undifferentiated.

He establishes boundaries and divisions within the created order.

---

# 7. “And let it separate” — וִיהִי מַבְדִּיל (*wîhî mavdîl*)

The purpose of the expanse is expressed with:

**מַבְדִּיל (*mavdîl*)**

meaning:

**“separating”** or **“dividing.”**

This comes from the same root **בדל (*b-d-l*)** encountered in Genesis 1:4.

Genesis 1:4:

**God separated the light from the darkness.**

Genesis 1:6:

**God establishes an expanse to separate the waters from the waters.**

The repetition of this separation language demonstrates a major structural theme of Genesis 1:

**God creates by establishing distinctions.**

---

# 8. Separation as a major creation theme

Genesis 1 repeatedly describes God establishing boundaries:

**Light ↔ Darkness**

**Waters ↔ Waters**

**Sea ↔ Dry Land**

The world becomes ordered through distinctions established by God.

This means that creation is not presented merely as the production of material objects.

God also establishes:

- boundaries
- relationships
- functions
- domains
- rhythms
- distinctions

The created world is ordered because the Creator gives it structure.

---

# 9. “Between waters and waters” — בֵּין מַיִם לָמָיִם (*bên mayim lāmāyim*)

The verse concludes with:

**בֵּין מַיִם לָמָיִם**

*bên mayim lāmāyim*

**“between waters and waters.”**

The repetition of **מַיִם (*mayim*)**, “waters,” emphasizes the division.

There are waters on both sides of the separation.

The expanse functions as the boundary or separating space between them.

Genesis 1:6 therefore prepares for the explanation given in verse 7, where the waters are explicitly distinguished.

---

# 10. Genesis 1:6 and Genesis 1:2

Genesis 1:6 should be read in light of Genesis 1:2.

Genesis 1:2 describes:

**darkness**

**the deep**

**waters**

Genesis 1:6 begins God's further ordering of the waters.

The progression is:

**Genesis 1:2**

Waters are present in an unstructured creation.

**Genesis 1:6**

God commands an expanse within the waters.

**Genesis 1:7**

God establishes the separation.

**Genesis 1:9**

God gathers the waters so that dry land appears.

Thus Genesis 1 presents God progressively ordering the world.

---

# 11. Genesis 1:6 and the second day

Genesis 1:6 introduces the work associated with the **second day**.

Unlike the first day, where God creates light and establishes the Day/Night distinction, the second day focuses upon the ordering of the waters.

The emphasis is not simply on creating another physical object.

It is on establishing an ordered environment.

The expanse provides a distinction between waters.

This prepares the world for the subsequent appearance of dry land and the habitation of living creatures.

---

# 12. The heavens and the expanse

Later in the chapter, Genesis 1:8 says:

**“And God called the expanse Heaven.”**

Therefore the **rāqîaʿ** introduced in verse 6 receives the name **Heaven**.

This is important for interpreting verse 6.

The expanse is not an independent divine realm.

It is part of God's created order.

God establishes it.

God names it.

God governs it.

---

# 13. Genesis 1:6 and ancient cosmology

Genesis 1 describes the world using the conceptual and linguistic framework of the ancient Near Eastern world.

The text speaks of:

- waters
- an expanse
- waters above
- waters below
- heavens
- dry land

The purpose of Genesis is not to provide a modern scientific model of atmospheric or planetary physics.

Its primary concern is theological and literary:

**God is the sovereign Creator who establishes order in the heavens and earth.**

The ancient cosmological language communicates the structure of the created world as understood within the narrative.

---

# 14. The theological importance of the expanse

The most important theological point in verse 6 is not the physical mechanics of the expanse.

It is the authority of God over the waters.

The waters do not establish their own boundaries.

God does.

The expanse exists because God commands it.

Therefore:

**God creates order where there was previously an undifferentiated watery environment.**

The verse continues the central message of Genesis 1:

**Creation is subject to the sovereign Word of God.**

---

# 15. God as the sovereign ruler over the waters

In the ancient world, bodies of water could be associated with chaos or hostile divine powers.

Genesis presents a radically different picture.

The waters are not God's equal.

They do not challenge Him.

God simply speaks.

He commands the expanse.

He divides the waters.

He establishes their boundaries.

The waters therefore remain completely subordinate to the Creator.

This reinforces the Bible's uncompromising presentation of God's sovereignty.

---

# 16. Genesis 1:6 and the absence of divine conflict

Nothing in the verse suggests a cosmic battle.

There is no struggle between God and another deity.

There is no rival god controlling the waters.

There is no divine warfare required to establish order.

God's command is sufficient.

This is one of the distinctive theological features of Genesis 1.

The Creator's authority is effortless and absolute.

---

# 17. God's Word establishes boundaries

Genesis 1:6 also reveals that God's Word establishes boundaries.

God does not merely create things individually.

He determines where things belong.

The waters are given distinct locations.

The expanse occupies its appointed place.

Later, the sea and dry land will likewise receive their boundaries.

This contributes to a biblical theology of divine order.

God is the One who determines the proper boundaries of creation.

---

# 18. Genesis 1:6 and God's wisdom

The ordering of the waters prepares the earth for life.

The creation narrative proceeds toward the appearance of:

- vegetation
- animals
- birds
- sea creatures
- land animals
- humanity

The establishment of the expanse is therefore part of a larger preparation of the world.

God is not creating randomly.

He is preparing an ordered environment in which life will flourish.

The creation account therefore portrays divine wisdom as well as divine power.

---

# 19. Genesis 1:6 and God's providence

The structure established in Genesis 1 continues throughout the biblical understanding of the world.

The heavens and waters remain under God's sovereign governance.

The ordered creation becomes the setting in which God's providential purposes unfold.

Genesis therefore establishes the foundational truth that the world is not independent of God.

The created order exists under His continuing authority.

---

# 20. Genesis 1:6 and the Creator–creature distinction

The expanse is created.

The waters are created.

The heavens are created.

God alone is the Creator.

This distinction is essential.

The biblical worldview does not identify God with nature.

God is not merely the universe.

The universe is God's creation.

Genesis 1:6 therefore contributes to a foundational biblical doctrine:

**The Creator is distinct from His creation.**

---

# 21. Genesis 1:6 and God's transcendence

Because God establishes the expanse and the waters, He is presented as transcending the created order.

He is not trapped inside creation.

He commands creation from outside and above it.

His authority is not derived from nature.

Nature derives its order from Him.

This becomes a foundational biblical understanding of God's transcendence.

---

# 22. Genesis 1:6 and God's immanence

At the same time, God is actively involved in the ordering of creation.

He speaks.

He establishes.

He separates.

He names.

Thus the God of Genesis is both:

**transcendent**

and

**actively involved with His creation.**

He is not part of creation, yet He is intimately active within the creation process.

---

# 23. Genesis 1:6 and the biblical doctrine of creation

Genesis 1:6 contributes to a broader doctrine of creation in several ways.

It demonstrates that:

**God is the source of creation.**

**God governs creation.**

**God orders creation.**

**God establishes boundaries within creation.**

**God gives creation its structure and function.**

Creation therefore exists under divine authority from beginning to end.

---

# 24. Genesis 1:6 and later Scripture

The imagery of heavens and waters continues throughout Scripture.

The Psalms repeatedly celebrate God's sovereignty over the seas and heavens.

For example, Psalm 104 reflects upon God's ordering of the waters and the earth.

The prophets likewise use creation imagery to communicate God's power.

In the New Testament, creation language becomes associated with Christ.

John 1:3 declares that all things were made through the Word.

Colossians 1:16 states that all things were created through Christ and for Christ.

Thus the creation order established in Genesis ultimately belongs within the New Testament's Christ-centered understanding of creation.

---

# 25. Genesis 1:6 and Christ

Genesis 1:6 does not explicitly mention Jesus Christ.

However, the New Testament reveals that the Son participated in creation.

John 1:3:

**“All things were made through him.”**

Colossians 1:16:

**“All things were created through him and for him.”**

Therefore, Christians reading Genesis within the completed canon can understand the creation work described here as belonging to the activity of the triune God.

The verse itself remains focused on God's sovereign command.

The fuller Trinitarian understanding comes through the progressive revelation of Scripture.

---

# 26. Genesis 1:6 and the Trinity

Genesis 1:2 already introduced:

**the Spirit of God**

Genesis 1:3–6 repeatedly introduces:

**God speaking.**

John 1 later reveals:

**the eternal Word through whom all things were made.**

The complete biblical picture therefore gives Christians a Trinitarian framework for understanding creation.

The Father is Creator.

The Son is Creator.

The Spirit is active in creation.

Genesis 1:6 contributes to this doctrine by presenting God's sovereign creative command.

---

# 27. The literary pattern of Genesis 1

Genesis 1:6 also continues the literary structure established earlier:

**God said.**

**God commanded.**

**Creation was ordered.**

The same structure will recur throughout the chapter.

This repeated formula reinforces the authority and effectiveness of God's Word.

The chapter is therefore not merely describing *what* exists.

It repeatedly explains *who* brought it into existence:

**God.**

---

# 28. The movement from chaos to order

A helpful way to understand Genesis 1 is to observe its movement from an initially unformed condition toward an ordered and inhabited world.

Genesis 1:2:

**formlessness**

**emptiness**

**darkness**

**waters**

Genesis 1:3–5:

**light**

**Day**

**Night**

Genesis 1:6–8:

**expanse**

**heavens**

**waters separated**

Genesis 1:9–13:

**dry land**

**seas**

**vegetation**

God is progressively establishing a world capable of being inhabited.

Genesis 1:6 is therefore one important step in this movement.

---

# 29. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:6 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God sovereignly establishes an expanse within the waters to separate them, demonstrating that the structure, boundaries, and order of creation are determined by His authoritative Word.**

Or more simply:

**God brings order to the waters by His sovereign command.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:6

### **“And God said”**

→ God's Word remains the instrument of divine ordering.

→ God exercises absolute authority over creation.

### **“Let there be an expanse”**

→ God establishes a structured division within creation.

→ The expanse belongs to the created order.

### **“In the midst of the waters”**

→ The waters introduced in Genesis 1:2 are now further ordered.

→ Creation is being progressively structured.

### **“And let it separate”**

→ Separation is a recurring theme in God's creative work.

→ God establishes boundaries within creation.

### **“The waters from the waters”**

→ The waters are distinguished into separate domains.

→ God governs the waters rather than struggling against them.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:6 establishes or contributes to at least these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God exercises absolute authority over creation.
2. **Authority of God's Word** — God's command accomplishes His purpose.
3. **Creation by divine command** — the ordered world exists according to God's Word.
4. **Divine order** — God establishes structure within creation.
5. **Divine separation** — God creates distinctions and boundaries.
6. **Creator–creature distinction** — the expanse and waters are created realities, not divine beings.
7. **God's transcendence** — God is Lord over the created order.
8. **God's immanence** — God actively works within creation.
9. **God's wisdom** — the created environment is progressively prepared for life.
10. **God's sovereignty over the waters** — the waters remain subject to divine authority.
11. **Anti-dualism** — there is no rival divine power controlling the waters.
12. **Biblical cosmology** — the narrative describes an ordered heaven-and-earth structure using ancient Near Eastern terminology.
13. **Trinitarian creation** — the broader canon identifies Father, Son, and Spirit with God's creative work.
14. **Christological creation** — the New Testament identifies Christ as the divine Word through whom all things were made.
15. **Providence** — the ordered creation remains under God's sovereign governance.

---

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:6 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD SPEAKS

**“And God said…”**

God's Word remains the foundation of creation.

He does not need to struggle to establish His purposes.

He speaks, and creation responds.

---

## II. GOD ESTABLISHES ORDER

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

God continues transforming the initially unstructured world into an ordered creation.

He establishes structure where there was previously no defined separation.

---

## III. GOD ESTABLISHES BOUNDARIES

**“Let it separate the waters from the waters.”**

God determines the boundaries of His creation.

The waters remain under His authority.

The created order exists according to His sovereign wisdom.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:6 teaches that God sovereignly orders creation by establishing an expanse between the waters, demonstrating that the structure, boundaries, and functions of the created world are established by His authoritative Word.**

The movement from Genesis 1:2 through Genesis 1:6 is therefore remarkable:

**Genesis 1:2**

Darkness.

Waters.

An unformed and empty world.

**Genesis 1:3**

God speaks:

**“Let there be light.”**

**Genesis 1:4**

God separates:

**light from darkness.**

**Genesis 1:5**

God names:

**Day and Night.**

**Genesis 1:6**

God speaks again:

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

And God begins another act of separation:

**waters from waters.**

The Creator is progressively transforming the unformed creation into an ordered world.

**God speaks.**

**God separates.**

**God orders.**

**And creation obeys.**
