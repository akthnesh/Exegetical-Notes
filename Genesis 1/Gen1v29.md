# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:29

**Genesis 1:29 —** God gives humanity every seed-bearing plant and every tree bearing seed as food.

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים הִנֵּה נָתַתִּי לָכֶם אֶת־כָּל־עֵשֶׂב זֹרֵעַ זֶרַע אֲשֶׁר עַל־פְּנֵי כָל־הָאָרֶץ וְאֶת־כָּל־הָעֵץ אֲשֶׁר־בּוֹ פְרִי־עֵץ זֹרֵעַ זָרַע לָכֶם יִהְיֶה לְאָכְלָה׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm hinnēh nāṯattî lāḵem ʾeṯ-kol-ʿēśeḇ zōrēaʿ zeraʿ ʾăšer ʿal-pənê kol-hāʾāreṣ wəʾeṯ-kol-hāʿēṣ ʾăšer-bô pərî-ʿēṣ zōrēaʿ zāraʿ lāḵem yihyeh ləʾoḵlāh*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And God said, ‘Behold, I have given to you every plant yielding seed that is upon the face of all the earth, and every tree in which is the fruit of a tree yielding seed; it shall be for you for food.’”**

---

# 1. “And God Said” — וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים (*wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:29 begins with another divine declaration.

The repeated expression:

**“And God said”**

reminds us that the provision described in this verse comes directly from God.

Humanity's food supply is presented as God's provision.

---

# 2. Divine Speech and Divine Provision

Genesis 1 repeatedly connects God's speech with the ordering and supplying of creation.

Here, God's speech identifies the provision given to humanity.

---

# 3. “Behold” — הִנֵּה (*hinnēh*)

God says:

**“Behold…”**

The Hebrew expression **הִנֵּה** draws attention to what follows.

It can function as:

**“look”**

**“behold”**

or

**“see.”**

God directs humanity's attention toward His provision.

---

# 4. “I Have Given” — נָתַתִּי (*nāṯattî*)

The Hebrew verb:

**נָתַן (*nāṯan*)**

means:

**“to give.”**

The form **נָתַתִּי** means:

**“I have given.”**

God Himself is the giver.

---

# 5. Food Is Given by God

The text therefore presents humanity's food as a divine gift.

Humanity does not independently establish the source of its provision.

God provides.

---

# 6. “To You” — לָכֶם (*lāḵem*)

The plural pronoun means:

**“to you.”**

The provision is addressed to humanity collectively.

This continues the plural language of verse 28.

---

# 7. Connection With Genesis 1:28

Genesis 1:28 describes humanity's commission.

Genesis 1:29 describes God's provision for humanity.

The sequence is:

**God blesses → God commissions → God provides.**

---

# 8. “Every Plant” — כָּל־עֵשֶׂב (*kol-ʿēśeḇ*)

The phrase means:

**“every plant”**

or

**“every herb.”**

The word **עֵשֶׂב (*ʿēśeḇ*)** refers broadly to vegetation or plants.

---

# 9. “Yielding Seed” — זֹרֵעַ זֶרַע (*zōrēaʿ zeraʿ*)

The expression emphasizes plants that produce seed.

The noun:

**זֶרַע (*zeraʿ*)**

means:

**“seed.”**

The concept of seed has already appeared repeatedly in Genesis 1.

---

# 10. Seed-Bearing Creation

Genesis 1 repeatedly emphasizes reproduction according to God's created order.

Plants produce seed.

Animals reproduce according to their kinds.

Humanity is commanded to multiply.

---

# 11. Reproductive Continuity

The seed-bearing language points toward the continuing provision and reproduction built into God's created order.

---

# 12. “Upon the Face of All the Earth”

The provision encompasses vegetation throughout the earth.

God's provision is described broadly rather than locally.

---

# 13. The Earth as a Provision

The earth is not merely the sphere of humanity's dominion.

It is also the source of food that God provides.

---

# 14. Dominion and Provision

Genesis 1:28 gives humanity authority over creation.

Genesis 1:29 immediately shows that creation also provides for humanity.

This creates a relationship of:

**responsibility**

and

**provision.**

---

# 15. “Every Tree”

The second major category is:

**“every tree.”**

The Hebrew:

**כָּל־הָעֵץ (*kol-hāʿēṣ*)**

means:

**“every tree.”**

---

# 16. Fruit-Bearing Trees

The text specifies trees containing:

**“the fruit of a tree yielding seed.”**

Trees provide fruit containing seed.

---

# 17. Fruit as Food

God explicitly identifies the vegetation and fruit as food.

The Hebrew phrase:

**לָכֶם יִהְיֶה לְאָכְלָה (*lāḵem yihyeh ləʾoḵlāh*)**

means:

**“it shall be for you for food.”**

---

# 18. Food Is Part of God's Good Creation

Food is therefore incorporated into God's original provision for humanity.

The creation account does not present material provision as inherently evil.

---

# 19. Creation Is Generous

The repeated:

**“every”**

emphasizes the abundance of God's provision.

Humanity receives a broad range of plant-based food.

---

# 20. Divine Generosity

God's provision is not described as minimal.

The language communicates generous provision.

---

# 21. Humanity Depends Upon Creation

Although humanity exercises dominion over creation, humanity also depends upon the created world for nourishment.

---

# 22. Dependence and Dominion

Humanity's dominion does not mean independence.

Humans remain dependent upon God and His provision.

---

# 23. God Is the Ultimate Provider

The immediate source of food is vegetation.

The ultimate source of the provision is God.

---

# 24. Provision and Providence

Genesis 1:29 provides an early picture of divine providence.

God creates an ordered world capable of sustaining the creatures He has made.

---

# 25. The Creator Knows the Needs of His Creatures

God does not merely create humanity.

He provides what humanity requires for life.

---

# 26. Food Before the Fall

This provision occurs before Genesis 3.

Therefore food, eating, cultivation, and material creation belong to God's original good design.

---

# 27. Material Creation Is Good

Genesis does not teach that physical creation is inherently inferior or evil.

God provides physical food for physical human beings within a good creation.

---

# 28. Humanity's Bodily Existence

Human beings are embodied creatures.

They require physical nourishment.

God's provision acknowledges this bodily reality.

---

# 29. The Goodness of the Body

The provision of food demonstrates that bodily existence is part of God's good creation.

---

# 30. God's Provision Is Ordered

The food God provides corresponds to the structure of creation:

**plants produce seed**

**trees produce fruit**

**humanity receives food.**

---

# 31. Creation Is Interdependent

Genesis 1 presents an ordered relationship among different parts of creation.

Vegetation provides food for humanity.

Later in the chapter, animals are also given food.

---

# 32. Food and Life

Food sustains biological life.

God's provision therefore supports the continuation of human life and fulfillment of the creation mandate.

---

# 33. Food and Fruitfulness

Humanity has been commanded to:

**be fruitful**

and

**multiply.**

Food provision supports humanity's ability to fulfill this command.

---

# 34. Food and Dominion

Healthy and sustained humanity is equipped to carry out the dominion mandate.

---

# 35. The Creation Mandate and Provision

Genesis 1:28 and 1:29 should therefore be read together.

God gives:

**a mission**

and

**the resources necessary for that mission.**

---

# 36. God's Commission Is Not Without Provision

The Creator who commands humanity also provides for humanity.

---

# 37. “I Have Given”

The language emphasizes completed divine generosity:

**“I have given.”**

God's provision accompanies His command.

---

# 38. Provision as Gift

Humanity receives food rather than creating the fundamental biological system by which food exists.

---

# 39. Humanity Cultivates What God Provides

The later biblical story will develop agriculture and cultivation.

But Genesis 1:29 establishes that the underlying provision originates with God.

---

# 40. Human Labor and Divine Provision

Human beings may work to cultivate food.

Yet the ability of the earth to produce food remains grounded in God's creative ordering.

---

# 41. The Seed Principle

The recurring concept of seed is significant throughout Genesis 1.

God creates living systems capable of continued reproduction.

---

# 42. Reproduction and Sustainability

The seed-bearing pattern allows creation to continue producing nourishment.

---

# 43. Created Order

The verse reinforces the orderly nature of creation.

God does not create a chaotic world.

He creates a world capable of sustaining life.

---

# 44. The Face of the Earth

The phrase:

**“face of all the earth”**

emphasizes the comprehensive extent of God's provision.

---

# 45. Humanity's Global Provision

As humanity fills the earth, the created world contains provision throughout its domain.

---

# 46. Abundance and Gratitude

Because food is presented as God's gift, the proper human response is ultimately gratitude.

---

# 47. Food and Worship

Food itself is not the object of worship.

The Creator who provides the food is.

---

# 48. The Giver Above the Gift

Genesis directs attention toward God as the giver rather than merely toward the created provision.

---

# 49. Creation Is Not Autonomous

The created world functions according to God's design.

Its provision ultimately depends upon the Creator.

---

# 50. God Owns the Creation

Because God creates and provides, humanity's use of creation remains accountable to God.

---

# 51. Dominion With Gratitude

Human dominion should therefore be exercised with gratitude rather than entitlement.

---

# 52. Dominion With Restraint

Human beings may use creation for legitimate purposes, but they remain responsible to the Creator.

---

# 53. Food and Stewardship

The provision of food also introduces the stewardship dimension of humanity's relationship with the earth.

---

# 54. Stewardship of Agricultural Resources

The earth's vegetation is a divine provision.

Humanity is therefore responsible for using and cultivating it wisely.

---

# 55. The Goodness of Agriculture

Agricultural production can be understood as part of the broader human vocation established in Genesis 1:28.

---

# 56. Food and Human Culture

Human societies inevitably develop systems of:

**farming**

**harvesting**

**storage**

**food preparation**

**trade**

and

**distribution.**

These later cultural developments operate within the original provision framework of Genesis 1.

---

# 57. God's Provision and Human Work

The fact that God provides food does not eliminate human labor.

Rather, human labor participates in the cultivation and distribution of what God has provided.

---

# 58. The Divine-Human Pattern

A recurring biblical pattern begins here:

**God provides**

→

**humanity receives**

→

**humanity cultivates and uses**

→

**humanity remains accountable to God.**

---

# 59. The Absence of Death in the Provision

The food specified in this verse comes from vegetation and fruit.

The verse does not describe humanity receiving animals as food.

---

# 60. The Initial Food Provision

Genesis 1:29 therefore specifically identifies plant life and fruit as humanity's food provision at this stage of the narrative.

---

# 61. Genesis 9:3

Later, after the Flood, God explicitly expands the human food provision to include animals.

Genesis 1:29 should therefore be understood within the progression of the biblical narrative.

---

# 62. Creation Order and Later Permission

Genesis 1 presents the initial provision.

Later Scripture records additional divine permissions concerning food.

---

# 63. No Need to Force Later Conditions Into Genesis 1:29

The verse should first be understood according to its own historical and literary context.

It describes God's original provision for humanity.

---

# 64. Humanity Receives Food Before Sin

This is important theologically.

Food is not a result of the Fall.

Hunger and toil are later affected by the curse, but eating itself belongs to the original creation.

---

# 65. Eating Is Good

The provision of food demonstrates that eating and bodily nourishment are part of God's good design.

---

# 66. Creation and Enjoyment

God provides creation not merely for bare survival but as an abundant environment containing fruit and vegetation.

---

# 67. The Garden Context

Genesis 2 will provide a more detailed account of humanity's relationship with trees and food in Eden.

Genesis 1 gives the broad creation framework.

---

# 68. God's Generosity in Eden

Genesis 2 will further demonstrate God's generosity by describing trees that are pleasant to the sight and good for food.

---

# 69. Provision Before Prohibition

The later prohibition concerning the tree of the knowledge of good and evil occurs within an environment already filled with God's generous provision.

---

# 70. God's Commands Are Not Arbitrary

The later prohibition must be understood against the background of God's extensive generosity.

---

# 71. The Pattern of Giving

Genesis 1:29 contributes to an important biblical pattern:

**God gives before humanity acts.**

---

# 72. Grace and Creation

God's giving here is creational generosity.

Humanity first receives God's gifts before carrying out its assigned work.

---

# 73. Gift Before Task

The sequence is significant:

**blessing**

→

**commission**

→

**provision.**

---

# 74. Human Dependence

Human beings cannot fulfill the creation mandate independently of God's provision.

---

# 75. Theological Anthropology

Genesis 1:29 contributes to biblical anthropology by showing that humans are:

**dependent creatures**

**bodily creatures**

**working creatures**

**receiving creatures.**

---

# 76. Humanity Is Not Self-Sufficient

The verse contradicts the idea of humanity as completely autonomous.

Human life depends upon God's provision.

---

# 77. Creaturely Dependence

Dependence is not a defect.

It is part of being created.

---

# 78. God as Provider

God's provision demonstrates His care for the creatures He has made.

---

# 79. Providence Begins at Creation

The verse gives an early picture of God's continuing care through the structures He established in creation.

---

# 80. Creation and Sustenance

God not only creates life.

He provides the means by which life is sustained.

---

# 81. Theological Significance of Seed

The seed-bearing pattern also emphasizes continuity.

God's creation contains mechanisms through which provision continues from generation to generation.

---

# 82. Provision for Future Generations

Seed-bearing plants and trees can continue producing after the initial generation.

The provision therefore has a forward-looking dimension.

---

# 83. Abundance for Humanity

The broad language of:

**“every plant”**

and

**“every tree”**

communicates extensive provision.

---

# 84. God's Economy of Creation

Creation operates through an ordered network of provision.

Plants produce.

Trees produce.

Humanity receives.

---

# 85. The Human Calling Within Creation

Humanity is therefore neither detached from creation nor merely another passive participant.

Humans receive from creation while being commissioned to exercise dominion over it.

---

# 86. Authority and Dependence

This produces a necessary balance:

**humanity has authority**

but

**humanity remains dependent.**

---

# 87. Stewardship Rather Than Ownership

Because humanity receives God's provision, the earth should be viewed as entrusted rather than absolutely possessed.

---

# 88. The Earth Belongs to God

The later biblical witness makes this principle explicit, but Genesis 1 already establishes God as the Creator and provider.

---

# 89. Gratitude for Food

The theological response to food is gratitude toward the Creator.

---

# 90. Food and Human Flourishing

God's provision supports the flourishing of human life and the fulfillment of humanity's calling.

---

# 91. Provision and Mission

Humanity is given food so that humanity can live and fulfill its God-given vocation.

---

# 92. God's Provision Is Purposeful

The food provision is not disconnected from God's broader purpose for humanity.

---

# 93. Creation Is Ordered Toward Life

The verse reinforces the broader Genesis 1 theme of creation being ordered toward life and flourishing.

---

# 94. Divine Generosity

The verse presents God as generous.

He does not merely create humanity and abandon it.

He provides.

---

# 95. Divine Care

God's care is demonstrated through the provision of food.

---

# 96. Human Gratitude and Stewardship

The appropriate response is both:

**gratitude**

and

**responsible use.**

---

# 97. The Central Theological Proposition

If Genesis 1:29 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God generously provided humanity with seed-bearing plants and fruit-bearing trees as food, demonstrating His care and sustaining provision within the good order of creation.**

Or more simply:

**God created and provided abundant vegetation and fruit as food for humanity, showing that human life depends upon His generous provision.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:29

### **“God said”**

→ The provision comes through divine declaration.

### **“Behold”**

→ God directs humanity's attention to His provision.

### **“I have given”**

→ Food is presented as God's gift.

### **“To you”**

→ The provision is given to humanity.

### **“Every plant yielding seed”**

→ Seed-bearing vegetation is included.

### **“Every tree”**

→ Fruit-bearing trees are included.

### **“For food”**

→ God identifies the purpose of this provision as human nourishment.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:29 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God is the provider of human food** — humanity's nourishment ultimately comes from God.
2. **Creation is good** — physical creation is suitable for sustaining human life.
3. **Material creation is not inherently evil** — God gives physical food to embodied human beings.
4. **Humanity is dependent upon God** — human beings require God's provision for life.
5. **Food is part of the original creation** — eating and nourishment precede the Fall.
6. **Vegetation is part of God's provision** — plants and fruit are explicitly given as food.
7. **God's provision is abundant** — the repeated language of “every” emphasizes generosity.
8. **God's provision is purposeful** — food supports human life and the fulfillment of humanity's vocation.
9. **Creation is ordered** — seed-bearing plants and fruit-bearing trees reproduce according to God's design.
10. **Seed provides continuity** — God's creation contains mechanisms for continued reproduction and provision.
11. **Humanity receives before it works** — God's gift precedes human cultivation.
12. **Dominion does not mean independence** — humanity exercises authority while remaining dependent upon God's provision.
13. **Human stewardship follows from divine provision** — humanity should responsibly use what God provides.
14. **Agriculture has a creational foundation** — cultivation belongs within humanity's broader creation vocation.
15. **Human labor is compatible with divine providence** — humans may cultivate what God has created and provided.
16. **Human beings are embodied creatures** — physical nourishment is part of God's design for humanity.
17. **Human flourishing is connected to provision** — food sustains humanity's ability to fulfill its vocation.
18. **God cares for His creatures** — His provision demonstrates divine generosity and care.
19. **The initial human food provision is plant-based** — Genesis 1:29 specifically identifies plants and fruit as humanity's food at this stage.
20. **Later biblical developments should be read progressively** — later passages expand or modify provisions without changing what Genesis 1:29 itself says.
21. **Food should produce gratitude** — God's role as giver establishes the proper theological response to His provision.
22. **Humanity should not treat creation as autonomous** — the created world remains dependent upon its Creator.
23. **The Creator remains the ultimate owner and Lord** — human use of creation remains subordinate to God.
24. **Divine blessing includes provision** — God's blessing in verse 28 is followed by tangible provision in verse 29.
25. **God equips humanity for its mission** — the Creator who commissions humanity also provides for humanity's needs.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

## I. GOD PROVIDES FOR HUMANITY

**“I have given to you…”**

God does not merely commission humanity; He provides for humanity.

---

## II. GOD PROVIDES ABUNDANTLY

**“Every plant…”**

**“Every tree…”**

The language emphasizes the breadth of God's provision.

---

## III. GOD PROVIDES THROUGH HIS CREATION

Seed-bearing plants and fruit-bearing trees become instruments through which God sustains human life.

---

## IV. GOD PROVIDES FOR HUMAN FLOURISHING

**“It shall be for you for food.”**

God's provision sustains humanity as it fulfills the vocation given in the preceding verse.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:29 teaches that God generously provided humanity with seed-bearing plants and fruit-bearing trees as food, demonstrating His abundant care and sustaining provision within His good creation.**

The progression from Genesis 1:27 to Genesis 1:29 is:

**Genesis 1:27**

God creates humanity:

**“in His image.”**

Humanity is:

**male and female.**

**Genesis 1:28**

God:

**blesses humanity**

and commissions humanity to:

**be fruitful**

**multiply**

**fill the earth**

**subdue it**

**rule over creation.**

**Genesis 1:29**

God then provides:

**seed-bearing plants**

**fruit-bearing trees**

**food for humanity.**

The pattern is:

**God creates.**

**God blesses.**

**God commissions.**

**God provides.**

**Humanity receives God's provision and fulfills God's calling.**
