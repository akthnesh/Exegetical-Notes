# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:8

**Genesis 1:8 —** “And God called the expanse Heaven. And there was evening and there was morning, the second day.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיִּקְרָא אֱלֹהִים לָרָקִיעַ שָׁמָיִם וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר יוֹם שֵׁנִי**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyiqrāʾ ʾĕlōhîm lārāqîaʿ šāmayim wayəhî-ʿerev wayəhî-vōqer yôm šēnî*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And God called the expanse Heavens. And there was evening, and there was morning, the second day.”**

---

# 1. “And God called” — וַיִּקְרָא אֱלֹהִים (*wayyiqrāʾ ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:8 begins with:

**וַיִּקְרָא אֱלֹהִים**

*Wayyiqrāʾ ʾĕlōhîm*

**“And God called…”**

This is the same naming formula used in Genesis 1:5.

There God called:

**the light → Day**

**the darkness → Night**

Here God gives a name to the expanse established in verses 6–7.

The repeated naming formula emphasizes God's authority over the created order.

God does not merely establish the expanse.

He also gives it its designation.

---

# 2. “The expanse” — לָרָקִיעַ (*lārāqîaʿ*)

The object being named is:

**רָקִיעַ (*rāqîaʿ*)**

**“expanse”**

The word was introduced in Genesis 1:6.

In verse 7, God made the expanse and separated the waters.

Verse 8 now identifies what God calls this expanse.

The sequence is:

**Genesis 1:6 — God commands the expanse.**

**Genesis 1:7 — God makes and establishes the expanse.**

**Genesis 1:8 — God names the expanse.**

This continues the pattern of divine ordering:

**command → accomplishment → designation.**

---

# 3. “Heaven” — שָׁמָיִם (*šāmayim*)

God calls the expanse:

**שָׁמָיִם (*šāmayim*)**

Traditionally:

**“Heaven”**

or

**“the heavens.”**

The Hebrew form **שָׁמַיִם (*šāmayim*)** is morphologically plural in form and is commonly translated according to context as **“heaven,” “heavens,”** or **“sky.”**

Here it functions as the name God gives to the expanse.

The ESV renders it:

**“Heaven.”**

Thus Genesis 1:8 establishes the relationship:

**Expanse → Heaven**

---

# 4. The significance of naming Heaven

The act of naming is important.

God has authority over the heavens because He created them.

The heavens are not divine beings.

They are not independent powers.

They are part of God's creation.

This is consistent with the broader biblical declaration:

**“In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.”**

Genesis 1:8 therefore reinforces the opening statement of the chapter.

The heavens belong to the Creator.

---

# 5. Heaven is created, not Creator

This distinction is foundational.

The ancient world frequently associated heavenly bodies and heavenly realms with deity.

Genesis presents a radically different worldview.

The heavens are not God.

God created and named them.

The created heavens therefore remain subordinate to the Creator.

This principle becomes especially important later in Genesis 1 when the sun, moon, and stars appear as created objects within the heavens rather than as divine beings.

---

# 6. Genesis 1:8 and Genesis 1:1

Genesis 1:1 says:

**“In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth.”**

Genesis 1:8 now gives the name **Heaven** to the expanse established during the second day.

The chapter is therefore progressively explaining the ordered structure of the heavens and earth introduced in verse 1.

Verse 1 gives the comprehensive statement.

Verses 2 onward progressively describe how God orders the creation.

---

# 7. The relationship between Heaven and Earth

Genesis 1:8 establishes **Heaven** as one major domain within God's ordered creation.

Later Genesis 1 will describe the earth and its inhabitants.

The chapter therefore develops a broad heaven-earth structure:

**Heaven above**

**Earth below**

Both belong to God.

Neither is autonomous.

Neither exists independently of the Creator.

This provides the foundation for the biblical understanding of the created cosmos.

---

# 8. “And there was evening” — וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב (*wayəhî-ʿerev*)

The second half of the verse begins:

**וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב**

*Wayəhî-ʿerev*

**“And there was evening.”**

The word **עֶרֶב (*ʿerev*)** means:

**“evening.”**

This repeats the temporal formula established in Genesis 1:5.

The creation narrative continues to mark the completion of each creation day through:

**evening**

and

**morning.**

---

# 9. “And there was morning” — וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר (*wayəhî-vōqer*)

The next phrase is:

**וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר**

*Wayəhî-vōqer*

**“And there was morning.”**

The noun **בֹּקֶר (*bōqer*)** means:

**“morning.”**

Together, the two phrases form the recurring creation-day formula:

**“And there was evening and there was morning…”**

This formula appeared in Genesis 1:5 and will continue throughout the creation account.

---

# 10. “The second day” — יוֹם שֵׁנִי (*yôm šēnî*)

The verse concludes:

**יוֹם שֵׁנִי**

*yôm šēnî*

**“the second day.”**

Here the ordinal form **שֵׁנִי (*šēnî*)** means:

**“second.”**

The first day in Genesis 1:5 was described as:

**יוֹם אֶחָד**

*“one day.”*

Now Genesis explicitly identifies this as:

**“the second day.”**

The creation sequence is therefore advancing numerically.

---

# 11. The second day

Genesis 1:8 concludes the work associated with the second day.

The sequence can be summarized:

**Genesis 1:6**

God commands an expanse.

**Genesis 1:7**

God makes the expanse and separates the waters.

**Genesis 1:8**

God names the expanse Heaven.

Then:

**evening → morning → second day.**

The creation account therefore presents a clearly ordered sequence.

---

# 12. The recurring creation formula

Genesis 1:8 reinforces a literary pattern that becomes increasingly clear:

**God speaks.**

**God accomplishes His command.**

**God establishes order.**

**God names.**

**Evening comes.**

**Morning comes.**

**The day is completed.**

This repeated structure gives Genesis 1 its highly organized literary character.

---

# 13. Genesis 1:8 and God's authority to name

God's naming of Heaven continues the theme introduced in Genesis 1:5.

God names:

**Light → Day**

**Darkness → Night**

**Expanse → Heaven**

Naming communicates divine authority.

The Creator defines the created order.

The heavens therefore receive their identity from God.

They do not determine their own ultimate purpose or identity.

---

# 14. Heaven belongs to God

Because God created and named the heavens, Scripture can later speak of:

**“the heavens”**

as belonging to Him.

Psalm 115:16 states:

**“The heavens are the LORD's heavens, but the earth he has given to the children of man.”**

Genesis provides the foundation for this concept.

The heavens belong to God because He made them.

---

# 15. The heavens declare God's glory

The biblical theology of creation later develops into the testimony of the heavens themselves.

Psalm 19:1 declares:

**“The heavens declare the glory of God.”**

The heavens are not objects of worship.

They are witnesses to the glory of their Creator.

Genesis 1:8 establishes the foundational distinction:

**God → Creator**

**Heaven → Creation**

---

# 16. Genesis 1:8 and the rejection of astral worship

The naming of Heaven prepares for what happens in Genesis 1:14–18.

God will place lights in the expanse:

- greater light
- lesser light
- stars

The text deliberately portrays them as created objects.

They are not presented as gods.

This is especially significant in an ancient context in which heavenly bodies were widely associated with divine powers.

Genesis places all heavenly phenomena under the authority of the one Creator.

---

# 17. Heaven is not identical with God's essence

The word **“Heaven”** can later refer to the heavenly realm associated with God's presence.

However, Genesis 1:8 is specifically naming a created expanse.

Therefore, the heaven created and named here should not simply be equated with God's own being.

God is not a created object.

The heavens are.

This preserves the Creator–creature distinction.

---

# 18. Genesis 1:8 and divine transcendence

God's naming of Heaven demonstrates that He is sovereign over it.

Heaven does not contain or limit God.

Rather, Heaven belongs to the realm God created.

This reinforces God's transcendence.

God is greater than the created heavens.

The heavens depend upon Him.

He does not depend upon them.

---

# 19. Genesis 1:8 and divine immanence

Although God transcends creation, Scripture also reveals Him as actively present and working within creation.

The creation account demonstrates this from the beginning.

God speaks.

God makes.

God separates.

God names.

He actively establishes the structure of the world.

Thus God's transcendence does not mean His absence.

He is sovereignly active within the creation He has made.

---

# 20. Genesis 1:8 and God's wisdom

The naming of the expanse as Heaven is another step toward a fully ordered creation.

The narrative is progressively organizing:

**light and darkness**

**day and night**

**waters above and below**

**heaven and earth**

The creation is being given structure.

This demonstrates God's wisdom in preparing the world for the life that will later inhabit it.

---

# 21. Genesis 1:8 and the order of creation

The creation account is not random.

God establishes categories and domains.

The expanse has a function.

The expanse receives a name.

The waters have designated locations.

The temporal cycle is established.

The created world is therefore portrayed as a coherent order.

Everything exists according to God's sovereign purpose.

---

# 22. Genesis 1:8 and time

The conclusion of the verse again includes:

**evening**

**morning**

**second day.**

This reinforces the structured nature of the creation week.

God's creative activity is described through a sequence of ordered days.

The passage therefore emphasizes not only *what* God creates but also the ordered progression by which the creation account unfolds.

---

# 23. Genesis 1:8 and the second-day structure

The second day differs somewhat from the first.

On the first day, Genesis explicitly records:

**“And God saw that the light was good.”**

Genesis 1:8 does not contain an equivalent statement saying:

**“God saw that it was good.”**

This omission has attracted interpretive discussion.

The text itself does not explain the omission.

Therefore, interpretations should be distinguished from what the verse explicitly states.

What the text clearly records is:

**God established the expanse.**

**God separated the waters.**

**God named the expanse Heaven.**

**The second day concluded.**

---

# 24. Why does Genesis 1:8 not say “God saw that it was good”?

The absence of the phrase is notable because the goodness formula appears elsewhere in Genesis 1.

Some interpreters have proposed various explanations.

One possibility is that the second day's work is incomplete until the waters are gathered on the third day.

Another possibility is that the literary structure simply does not require the formula at every stage.

The text itself does not explicitly provide an explanation.

Therefore, the safest exegetical approach is to acknowledge the omission without building a doctrine upon a speculative explanation.

---

# 25. The second day prepares for the third

Genesis 1:8 leaves the waters still present above and below the expanse.

Genesis 1:9 will continue God's ordering work:

**“Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place, and let the dry land appear.”**

Thus the second day prepares for the next stage of creation.

The narrative continues progressively:

**Day 1 → light**

**Day 2 → heaven/expanse**

**Day 3 → dry land and vegetation**

The creation account is moving toward an increasingly habitable world.

---

# 26. Genesis 1:8 and the ordered cosmos

The verse contributes to the biblical picture of a structured cosmos.

There is:

**Heaven**

**Earth**

**waters**

**land**

**light**

**darkness**

All of these belong within God's created order.

Nothing exists outside His sovereignty.

Nothing competes with Him.

---

# 27. Genesis 1:8 and monotheism

The verse provides another indirect rejection of polytheism.

The heavens are named by one God.

The waters are ordered by one God.

The earth will be ordered by the same God.

The lights in the heavens will later be created by the same God.

Genesis presents one sovereign Creator over the whole cosmos.

---

# 28. Genesis 1:8 and God's Word

The expanse exists because God spoke.

Although verse 8 emphasizes naming rather than the original command, the whole context makes clear that the expanse is the result of God's Word.

God says:

**“Let there be an expanse.”**

God makes it.

God names it.

Thus God's Word stands behind the structure of the heavens.

---

# 29. Genesis 1:8 and the New Testament

The New Testament develops the creation theology introduced in Genesis.

John 1:3 teaches:

**“All things were made through him.”**

Colossians 1:16 says that all things were created through Christ and for Christ.

Hebrews 1:10 also applies creation language to the Son.

Therefore, Christian theology reads Genesis 1 within the fuller revelation of God as Father, Son, and Spirit.

Genesis reveals the sovereign Creator.

The New Testament reveals the Son's participation in that creative work.

---

# 30. Genesis 1:8 and Christological fulfillment

Genesis 1:8 concerns the created heavens.

The New Testament reveals Christ as the One through whom creation came into being.

Therefore, the heavens named in Genesis ultimately belong to Christ as Creator as well.

Colossians 1:16 teaches that:

**“all things were created through him and for him.”**

The heavens therefore exist not merely as an independent cosmic structure but ultimately for God's glory and, in the New Testament's Christological presentation, for Christ.

---

# 31. Genesis 1:8 and the new creation

The Bible begins with the creation of the heavens and earth.

It ends with the promise of:

**“a new heaven and a new earth.”**

The creation theme therefore extends throughout Scripture.

Genesis 1 establishes the original ordered creation.

Revelation describes the consummation of God's redemptive purposes.

The God who orders the heavens in Genesis remains sovereign over the final renewal of creation.

---

# 32. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:8 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God sovereignly names the expanse Heaven and brings the second creation day to completion, demonstrating His authority over the heavens and His orderly governance of creation.**

Or more simply:

**God establishes and names the heavens as part of His ordered creation.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:8

### **“And God called the expanse”**

→ God has authority to name what He has created.

→ The expanse established in verses 6–7 receives its designation.

### **“Heaven”**

→ The heavens belong to the created order.

→ They are not divine beings.

→ They exist under God's sovereign authority.

### **“And there was evening”**

→ The creation sequence continues according to an ordered temporal rhythm.

### **“And there was morning”**

→ The second creation day reaches its completion.

### **“The second day”**

→ Genesis advances the creation sequence.

→ The ordered creation continues toward its preparation for habitation.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:8 establishes or contributes to at least these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God has authority over the heavens.
2. **Divine naming** — God determines the designation of His creation.
3. **Creator–creature distinction** — Heaven is created; God is the Creator.
4. **Monotheism** — the heavens are under the authority of one sovereign God.
5. **Rejection of astral divinity** — heavenly realities are part of creation rather than rival gods.
6. **Divine order** — God structures creation into distinct domains.
7. **God's transcendence** — God is greater than the heavens He created.
8. **God's immanence** — God actively orders and governs His creation.
9. **Divine wisdom** — the creation is progressively prepared for habitation.
10. **Divine authority over time** — the creation week proceeds according to an ordered sequence.
11. **Effectiveness of God's Word** — the expanse exists because of God's command.
12. **Providence** — the created heavens remain under God's sovereign governance.
13. **Biblical cosmology** — the text describes Heaven as the name given to the expanse within the ancient creation framework.
14. **Trinitarian creation** — the broader canon identifies Father, Son, and Spirit with the work of creation.
15. **Christological creation** — the New Testament identifies Christ as the One through whom all things were created.
16. **New creation** — the ordered heavens of Genesis anticipate the Bible's ultimate hope of renewed creation.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:8 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD NAMES WHAT HE CREATES

**“And God called the expanse Heaven.”**

The heavens receive their identity and designation from their Creator.

God has authority over everything He makes.

---

## II. GOD RULES THE HEAVENS

The expanse is not divine.

It is created.

The heavens belong to the Creator and remain subject to His authority.

---

## III. GOD ORDERS CREATION THROUGH TIME

**“And there was evening and there was morning, the second day.”**

The creation account continues according to an ordered sequence.

God's work unfolds according to His sovereign purpose and wisdom.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:8 teaches that God sovereignly names the expanse Heaven and completes the second creation day, demonstrating that the heavens themselves are created realities under His authority and that the entire created order unfolds according to His wise and sovereign design.**

The movement from Genesis 1:2 through Genesis 1:8 is therefore remarkable:

**Genesis 1:2**

Darkness.

Waters.

An unformed and empty world.

**Genesis 1:3**

God speaks:

**“Let there be light.”**

**Genesis 1:4**

God separates:

**light from darkness.**

**Genesis 1:5**

God names:

**Day and Night.**

**Genesis 1:6**

God speaks:

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

**Genesis 1:7**

God accomplishes His command:

**the waters are separated.**

**Genesis 1:8**

God names the expanse:

**Heaven.**

Then:

**evening → morning → the second day.**

The pattern continues:

**God speaks.**

**God acts.**

**God separates.**

**God names.**

**God orders.**

**Creation obeys.**
