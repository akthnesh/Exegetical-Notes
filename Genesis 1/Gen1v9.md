# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:9

**Genesis 1:9 —** “And God said, ‘Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place, and let the dry land appear.’ And it was so.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים יִקָּווּ הַמַּיִם מִתַּחַת הַשָּׁמַיִם אֶל־מָקוֹם אֶחָד וְתֵרָאֶה הַיַּבָּשָׁה וַיְהִי־כֵן**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm yiqqāwû ham-mayim mittaḥat haššāmayim ʾel-māqôm ʾeḥād wəṯērāʾeh hayyabbāšāh wayəhî-kēn*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And God said, ‘Let the waters under the heavens be gathered to one place, and let the dry land appear.’ And it was so.”**

---

# 1. “And God said” — וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים (*wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:9 begins with the familiar creation formula:

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים**

*Wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm*

**“And God said…”**

This is now the third major creative command recorded after the beginning of the chapter.

Genesis repeatedly directs the reader's attention to God's speech as the decisive instrument of creation.

The pattern continues:

**God speaks → God commands → creation responds.**

Genesis 1:3:

**“Let there be light.”**

Genesis 1:6:

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

Genesis 1:9:

**“Let the waters … be gathered together…”**

The sovereign Creator continues bringing order to the world through His Word.

---

# 2. “Let the waters … be gathered together” — יִקָּווּ הַמַּיִם (*yiqqāwû ham-māyim*)

The command begins:

**יִקָּווּ הַמַּיִם**

*Yiqqāwû ham-māyim*

**“Let the waters be gathered together.”**

The verb **יִקָּווּ (*yiqqāwû*)** comes from the root **קוה (*q-w-h*)**, in this context expressing the idea of being gathered or collected.

The waters that previously covered the earth are now commanded to move into an appointed place.

This is another major act of divine ordering.

God is not creating the waters here.

He is **gathering and assigning them a defined location.**

---

# 3. “The waters” — הַמַּיִם (*ham-māyim*)

The waters have been present since Genesis 1:2.

They have already been divided by the expanse in Genesis 1:6–7.

Now God gives further structure to the waters beneath the heavens.

The sequence is:

**Genesis 1:2**

Waters are present.

**Genesis 1:6–7**

The waters are divided by the expanse.

**Genesis 1:9**

The waters beneath the heavens are gathered into one place.

The creation narrative therefore continues progressively ordering what was initially described as an unformed and empty environment.

---

# 4. “Under the heavens” — מִתַּחַת הַשָּׁמַיִם (*mittaḥat haššāmayim*)

The phrase:

**מִתַּחַת הַשָּׁמַיִם**

means:

**“under the heavens.”**

The heavens were named in Genesis 1:8.

Now the waters beneath those heavens are addressed.

This establishes another major distinction within the creation account:

**Heavens above**

**Waters below**

The narrative is progressively defining the structure of the world.

---

# 5. “Into one place” — אֶל־מָקוֹם אֶחָד (*ʾel-māqôm ʾeḥād*)

God commands the waters to be gathered:

**אֶל־מָקוֹם אֶחָד**

*ʾel-māqôm ʾeḥād*

**“into one place.”**

The noun:

**מָקוֹם (*māqôm*)**

means:

**“place.”**

The adjective:

**אֶחָד (*ʾeḥād*)**

means:

**“one.”**

God therefore assigns the waters a defined place.

The emphasis is again on divine ordering.

The waters do not determine their own boundaries.

God determines them.

---

# 6. “One place” and divine order

The expression **“one place”** does not necessarily mean that there was literally only one geographic body of water in the modern geographical sense.

The immediate literary purpose is to contrast the gathered waters with the dry land that will appear.

God commands the waters to be collected into an identifiable domain so that dry land can become visible.

The theological emphasis is therefore on **God's establishment of boundaries and order.**

---

# 7. “And let the dry land appear” — וְתֵרָאֶה הַיַּבָּשָׁה (*wəṯērāʾeh hayyabbāšāh*)

The second half of the command says:

**וְתֵרָאֶה הַיַּבָּשָׁה**

*wəṯērāʾeh hayyabbāšāh*

**“And let the dry land appear.”**

The noun:

**יַבָּשָׁה (*yabbāšāh*)**

means:

**“dry land”** or **“dry ground.”**

The verb:

**תֵרָאֶה (*ṯērāʾeh*)**

comes from **ראה (*r-ʾ-h*)**, meaning **“to see”** or **“to appear/become visible.”**

The command therefore does not necessarily describe God creating land from nothing at this precise moment.

Rather, it describes the dry land becoming visible as the waters are gathered away.

---

# 8. Dry land appears

The relationship between the two commands is important:

**Waters gathered → dry land appears.**

God establishes one domain for the waters.

As a result, another domain becomes visible:

**dry land.**

This is another example of God's ordering activity through separation and boundaries.

The land is distinguished from the waters.

---

# 9. The emergence of dry land

Genesis 1:9 portrays dry land as becoming visible.

This recalls the earlier condition described in Genesis 1:2.

At the beginning:

**darkness**

**waters**

**the deep**

Now:

**waters are gathered**

**dry land appears**

The creation is progressively moving from an unstructured watery environment toward an ordered world.

---

# 10. Genesis 1:9 and the theme of separation

Separation has been one of the major themes of Genesis 1.

Genesis 1:4:

**Light separated from darkness.**

Genesis 1:7:

**Waters above separated from waters below.**

Genesis 1:9:

**Waters distinguished from dry land.**

God establishes meaningful distinctions within creation.

The created world is ordered through boundaries.

---

# 11. “And it was so” — וַיְהִי־כֵן (*wayəhî-kēn*)

The verse concludes:

**וַיְהִי־כֵן**

*Wayəhî-kēn*

**“And it was so.”**

This short statement is extremely important.

God commands.

Creation obeys.

The divine Word accomplishes exactly what God intends.

The phrase therefore functions as a divine confirmation of the effectiveness of God's command.

---

# 12. The authority of God's Word

Genesis repeatedly demonstrates that God's speech is effective.

He does not need to struggle with creation.

He does not need assistance from another deity.

He speaks.

The created order responds.

Genesis 1:9 therefore reinforces a central biblical doctrine:

**God's Word is sovereign and effective.**

What He commands comes to pass.

---

# 13. Genesis 1:9 and divine sovereignty

The waters are commanded to move.

The dry land is commanded to appear.

Both occur according to God's Word.

This demonstrates that God has authority over:

- waters
- land
- boundaries
- geography
- the structure of the created world

Nothing in creation possesses independent authority over God's purposes.

---

# 14. God establishes boundaries

The command of Genesis 1:9 establishes a major geographical distinction:

**water**

and

**dry land.**

Later Scripture repeatedly appeals to God's establishment of the boundaries of the sea and land.

For example, Psalm 95:5 declares:

**“The sea is his, for he made it, and his hands formed the dry land.”**

The Psalm is directly consistent with the theological foundation established here.

The sea and land belong to God because He made and ordered them.

---

# 15. The sea is not divine

Genesis presents the waters as created and controlled by God.

The sea is not an independent divine force.

God assigns it its place.

This continues Genesis's rejection of ancient pagan conceptions in which cosmic waters could represent divine powers opposed to creation.

The biblical Creator does not battle the sea.

He commands it.

---

# 16. The Creator and the sea

This theme becomes prominent in later biblical poetry.

The Psalms repeatedly celebrate God's authority over the seas.

The sea belongs to God because He created it.

The waves and waters remain subject to His command.

Genesis 1:9 provides the foundational creation account behind this theology.

---

# 17. “Dry land” and the habitability of creation

The appearance of dry land is crucial for the development of the creation narrative.

Dry land provides the environment in which God will establish vegetation.

Genesis 1:11 will introduce plant life.

Later the land will become the environment for animals and humanity.

Thus Genesis 1:9 represents a major transition.

The world is becoming increasingly suitable for habitation.

---

# 18. Genesis 1:9 and the third day

Genesis 1:9 introduces the work of the third day.

The second day concluded with the establishment of the expanse and the separation of the waters.

The third day begins with the gathering of the waters and the appearance of dry land.

Then the same day will continue with vegetation.

The progression is:

**Day 1 → Light**

**Day 2 → Heaven/Expanse**

**Day 3 → Land and Vegetation**

The creation account is moving toward a fully ordered and inhabited world.

---

# 19. Genesis 1:9 and creation as preparation

The third day begins to transform the creation from a largely uninhabited environment into a world capable of sustaining life.

The dry land is necessary for vegetation.

Vegetation will provide food.

Later, the land will support animals and humanity.

Genesis therefore presents God's creative work as purposeful preparation.

He establishes the environment before populating it.

---

# 20. Divine wisdom in the ordering of creation

Genesis 1:9 demonstrates not merely God's power but His wisdom.

God establishes:

**water where water belongs**

and

**land where land belongs.**

The creation is being structured in a way that permits subsequent life to flourish.

The order of creation therefore reflects divine intentionality.

---

# 21. Genesis 1:9 and the goodness of the created world

The following verse records:

**“And God saw that it was good.”**

The dry land and seas are therefore part of the good created order.

The physical world is not inherently evil.

Matter is not evil.

Land is not evil.

Water is not evil.

Creation belongs to God and is evaluated according to His purposes.

---

# 22. Genesis 1:9 and biblical theology of land

Land becomes an increasingly important theme throughout Scripture.

The biblical story will later involve:

- Eden
- the land promised to Abraham
- Israel's inheritance
- exile from the land
- restoration
- the renewed creation

Genesis 1:9 establishes the most basic theological reality:

**God is the Creator and owner of the land.**

Human beings later receive land as a gift and stewardship responsibility.

---

# 23. Genesis 1:9 and human stewardship

Although humanity has not yet appeared, Genesis 1:9 establishes the world that humanity will eventually inhabit.

The land does not belong to humanity independently.

It belongs first to God.

Human dominion, introduced later in Genesis 1:26–28, is therefore delegated authority.

Human beings are stewards of a world that God created and ordered.

---

# 24. Genesis 1:9 and divine ownership

The biblical pattern is:

**God creates → God owns → God orders → God delegates.**

The sea belongs to God.

The dry land belongs to God.

The heavens belong to God.

Human beings therefore live within a world that is fundamentally God's possession.

---

# 25. Genesis 1:9 and God's sovereignty over chaos

The waters that initially covered the scene are progressively brought under divine order.

Genesis does not portray God as struggling to overcome an independent cosmic force.

Instead:

**God speaks.**

**The waters gather.**

**The land appears.**

The transition from watery unstructured conditions to visible dry land demonstrates the supremacy of God's command.

---

# 26. Genesis 1:9 and ancient Near Eastern creation accounts

Ancient Near Eastern traditions often contained stories involving gods, waters, cosmic conflict, and the formation of the world.

Genesis shares certain broad ancient cosmological categories, including waters, heavens, and dry land.

But Genesis presents them within a radically different theological framework.

There is one Creator.

There is no divine conflict.

The waters are not gods.

The sea does not defeat or threaten God.

God simply commands.

The result:

**“And it was so.”**

---

# 27. Genesis 1:9 and monotheism

The verse contributes to the Bible's consistent monotheistic worldview.

One God controls:

**the heavens**

**the waters**

**the land**

The created world is not divided among competing divine rulers.

Everything belongs to one sovereign Creator.

---

# 28. Genesis 1:9 and the Creator–creature distinction

The waters are created.

The land is created.

The heavens are created.

God alone is Creator.

This distinction prevents nature from becoming an object of ultimate worship.

Creation can reveal God's glory, but creation itself is not God.

---

# 29. Genesis 1:9 and God's transcendence

God exercises authority over the entire earth.

He establishes the location of the waters.

He brings dry land into visibility.

His authority therefore extends over the physical structure of the world.

God is not contained by creation.

He is its sovereign Creator.

---

# 30. Genesis 1:9 and God's immanence

At the same time, God actively works within creation.

He speaks into the created order.

He establishes physical boundaries.

He brings about visible results.

Thus God's transcendence does not mean distance or inactivity.

The Creator actively governs His creation.

---

# 31. Genesis 1:9 and the Word of God

The final phrase:

**“And it was so.”**

is one of the most powerful recurring expressions in Genesis 1.

God's command is not merely a proposal.

It is effective.

His Word accomplishes His purpose.

This becomes an important foundation for the biblical understanding of God's revelation.

---

# 32. Genesis 1:9 and Christological creation

Genesis 1:9 does not explicitly identify Christ.

However, the New Testament teaches that all creation came through the Son.

John 1:3:

**“All things were made through him.”**

Colossians 1:16:

**“All things were created through him and for him.”**

Thus the land and seas established in Genesis ultimately belong within the New Testament's Christ-centered theology of creation.

---

# 33. Genesis 1:9 and the new creation

The biblical story begins with God ordering the earth.

It ultimately moves toward the renewal of creation.

Revelation speaks of:

**“a new heaven and a new earth.”**

The God who establishes dry land in Genesis remains the God who will consummate His purposes for creation.

Creation and new creation are therefore connected by the sovereignty and faithfulness of the same Creator.

---

# 34. Genesis 1:9 and God's faithfulness

The recurring order established in creation becomes the foundation for later biblical reflections on God's faithfulness.

The sea has boundaries.

The land has boundaries.

The created order operates according to God's established design.

Scripture can therefore appeal to creation as evidence of God's continuing faithfulness.

---

# 35. Literary function within Genesis 1

Genesis 1:9 is important because it marks a transition.

The first two days primarily establish:

**light/darkness**

and

**heaven/waters.**

The third day begins establishing:

**land/seas**

and then:

**vegetation.**

The creation account is moving from the establishment of cosmic spaces toward the filling of those spaces with life.

---

# 36. The pattern of Genesis 1

The chapter can be observed as a movement from forming to filling.

### Days of forming

**Day 1**

Light and darkness.

**Day 2**

Heavens and waters.

**Day 3**

Sea and dry land, followed by vegetation.

### Days of filling

**Day 4**

Sun, moon, and stars.

**Day 5**

Sea creatures and birds.

**Day 6**

Land animals and humanity.

Genesis 1:9 begins the third-day work of forming the land environment that will later be filled with living creatures.

---

# 37. Genesis 1:9 and God's purposeful sequence

The sequence of creation emphasizes divine purpose.

God does not populate an unordered world.

He first establishes the domains.

Then He fills them.

The heavens are established before the heavenly lights are described.

The seas are established before sea creatures appear.

The land is established before land animals and humanity appear.

Genesis therefore presents a deliberate sequence of preparation and filling.

---

# 38. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:9 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God sovereignly gathers the waters beneath the heavens and causes dry land to appear, establishing the boundaries of sea and land by His authoritative Word and preparing the earth for life.**

Or more simply:

**God orders the waters and establishes the dry land according to His sovereign command.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:9

### **“And God said”**

→ God's Word continues to govern creation.

→ Divine command remains the instrument of God's creative ordering.

### **“Let the waters … be gathered together”**

→ God assigns the waters a definite place.

→ The waters remain under divine authority.

### **“Into one place”**

→ God establishes boundaries within the created world.

→ Creation is being progressively ordered.

### **“And let the dry land appear”**

→ Dry land becomes visible as the waters are gathered.

→ The world is being prepared for habitation.

### **“And it was so”**

→ God's command is accomplished.

→ Creation responds to the Creator's sovereign Word.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:9 establishes or contributes to at least these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God commands and governs the waters and land.
2. **Effectiveness of God's Word** — God's command produces the intended result.
3. **Divine order** — God establishes the geographical structure of creation.
4. **Divine separation** — water and dry land are distinguished.
5. **Divine boundaries** — God determines the proper domain of the waters.
6. **Creator–creature distinction** — sea and land are created realities, not divine beings.
7. **God's sovereignty over the sea** — the waters are subject to God's authority.
8. **God's sovereignty over the land** — dry land exists according to God's command.
9. **Divine wisdom** — the world is progressively prepared for habitation.
10. **Goodness of creation** — the physical world belongs to God's good created order.
11. **Monotheism** — there is no rival deity controlling the sea or land.
12. **Rejection of cosmic dualism** — the waters do not represent a power equal to God.
13. **Human stewardship** — the land later entrusted to humanity ultimately belongs to its Creator.
14. **Divine providence** — the created order remains under God's governance.
15. **Christological creation** — the New Testament identifies Christ as the One through whom all things were created.
16. **New creation** — the original ordering of earth anticipates the Bible's ultimate renewal of creation.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:9 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD GATHERS THE WATERS

**“Let the waters under the heavens be gathered together into one place…”**

God establishes boundaries within creation.

The waters are not beyond His authority.

---

## II. GOD REVEALS THE DRY LAND

**“And let the dry land appear.”**

God prepares the earth as a place capable of sustaining life.

The created world is being progressively ordered for habitation.

---

## III. GOD'S WORD ACCOMPLISHES HIS PURPOSE

**“And it was so.”**

God speaks.

Creation responds.

His command does not fail.

His Word accomplishes exactly what He purposes.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:9 teaches that God sovereignly gathers the waters and causes dry land to appear, establishing the boundaries of sea and land by His authoritative Word and preparing the earth as an ordered environment for life.**

The movement from Genesis 1:2 through Genesis 1:9 is therefore remarkable:

**Genesis 1:2**

Darkness.

Waters.

An unformed and empty world.

**Genesis 1:3**

God speaks:

**“Let there be light.”**

**Genesis 1:4**

God separates:

**light from darkness.**

**Genesis 1:5**

God names:

**Day and Night.**

**Genesis 1:6**

God speaks:

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

**Genesis 1:7**

God establishes:

**the waters above and below the expanse.**

**Genesis 1:8**

God names the expanse:

**Heaven.**

**Genesis 1:9**

God speaks again:

**“Let the waters … be gathered together…”**

And:

**“let the dry land appear.”**

Then the decisive confirmation:

**“And it was so.”**

The pattern continues:

**God speaks.**

**God separates.**

**God gathers.**

**God establishes boundaries.**

**Creation obeys.**
