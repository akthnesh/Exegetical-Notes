# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:19

**Genesis 1:19 —** “And there was evening and there was morning, the fourth day.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר י֥וֹם רְבִיעִֽי׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayhî-ʿereḇ wayhî-ḇōqer yôm rəḇîʿî*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And there was evening, and there was morning, the fourth day.”**

---

# 1. “And there was evening” — וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב (*wayhî-ʿereḇ*)

The verse begins:

**וַֽיְהִי־עֶ֥רֶב**

*Wayhî-ʿereḇ*

**“And there was evening.”**

The Hebrew verb **הָיָה (*hāyâ*)** means:

**“to be”**

**“to become”**

**“to happen”**

or

**“to come to pass.”**

Here it introduces the completion of the day's cycle.

---

# 2. “Evening”

The Hebrew word:

**עֶרֶב (*ʿereḇ*)**

means:

**“evening”**

and refers to the transition toward darkness and the close of the daylight period.

The expression has already appeared in the creation account.

---

# 3. The recurring creation formula

Genesis repeatedly uses the pattern:

**“And there was evening and there was morning…”**

This occurs at the conclusion of the successive creation days.

Genesis 1:19 therefore follows the established literary pattern.

---

# 4. Evening marks the close of the fourth day's work

The fourth day involved God's work of establishing the heavenly lights.

God:

**made the lights**

**placed them in the expanse**

**gave them their functions**

**established their relationship to day and night**

and declared the arrangement:

**good.**

Verse 19 brings this day's work to its conclusion.

---

# 5. “And there was morning” — וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר (*wayhî-ḇōqer*)

The next phrase is:

**וַֽיְהִי־בֹ֖קֶר**

*Wayhî-ḇōqer*

**“And there was morning.”**

The Hebrew word:

**בֹּקֶר (*bōqer*)**

means:

**“morning”**

or

**“dawn.”**

It refers to the arrival of daylight following the night.

---

# 6. Evening and morning

The two terms appear together:

**evening**

and

**morning.**

Together they describe the completion of a full day within the creation narrative.

---

# 7. “The fourth day” — יוֹם רְבִיעִי (*yôm rəḇîʿî*)

The final phrase is:

**יוֹם רְבִיעִי**

*yôm rəḇîʿî*

**“the fourth day.”**

The noun:

**יוֹם (*yôm*)**

means:

**“day.”**

The adjective:

**רְבִיעִי (*rəḇîʿî*)**

means:

**“fourth.”**

The verse therefore explicitly identifies the completed period as:

**the fourth day.**

---

# 8. The fourth day

The fourth day corresponds to the fourth major creative work described in Genesis 1.

On this day God establishes the heavenly lights.

The sequence is:

**Day One → light**

**Day Two → expanse**

**Day Three → land, seas, vegetation**

**Day Four → heavenly lights**

---

# 9. The completion formula

Genesis 1:19 concludes with the familiar creation-day formula.

This demonstrates that the fourth day is presented as a completed unit within the sequence of creation.

---

# 10. The fourth day follows God's declaration of goodness

Verse 18 ended with:

**“And God saw that it was good.”**

Verse 19 now concludes:

**“And there was evening and there was morning, the fourth day.”**

The sequence is therefore:

**God establishes**

→

**God evaluates**

→

**the day concludes.**

---

# 11. Evening and morning as a literary boundary

The phrase provides a clear boundary between one creation day and the next.

It marks the transition from:

**Day Four**

to

**Day Five.**

---

# 12. The repeated formula creates structure

The repeated expression:

**“there was evening and there was morning”**

provides the creation narrative with a recognizable rhythm.

Each day has:

**a beginning**

**divine activity**

**completion**

**and conclusion.**

---

# 13. Creation is presented sequentially

Genesis 1 does not describe the creative acts as an undifferentiated event.

The narrative presents a sequence of days.

Verse 19 confirms the completion of the fourth unit.

---

# 14. The progression of the creation week

The creation account moves progressively:

**Day One**

Light and darkness.

**Day Two**

The expanse.

**Day Three**

Dry land, seas, and vegetation.

**Day Four**

Sun, moon, and stars.

**Day Five**

Sea creatures and birds.

**Day Six**

Land animals and humanity.

**Day Seven**

God's rest.

Genesis 1:19 marks the completion of the fourth stage.

---

# 15. Day Four corresponds to Day One

There is an important structural relationship between the first and fourth days.

### Day One

God establishes:

**light and darkness**

### Day Four

God establishes:

**the heavenly lights that govern day and night.**

The fourth day therefore corresponds closely with the first day.

---

# 16. The pattern of forming and filling

Genesis 1 has a recognizable structural pattern.

The first three days establish realms.

The following days fill those realms.

### Day One

Light/darkness.

### Day Four

Heavenly lights.

### Day Two

Sky and waters.

### Day Five

Birds and sea creatures.

### Day Three

Land and vegetation.

### Day Six

Land animals and humanity.

Verse 19 marks the completion of the first half of this pattern.

---

# 17. Day Four as a counterpart to Day One

Day One introduces the distinction between light and darkness.

Day Four introduces the heavenly bodies associated with those temporal realms.

The narrative therefore moves from:

**light**

to

**lights.**

---

# 18. The distinction between light and light-bearers

Genesis 1 contains an important distinction.

On Day One:

**light appears.**

On Day Four:

**heavenly lights are appointed.**

The existence of light is therefore presented before the heavenly bodies that function as visible light-bearers.

---

# 19. God is the source of creation's order

The fact that light exists before the heavenly lights emphasizes that the created order does not depend upon the heavenly bodies as ultimate sources.

God Himself establishes the order.

---

# 20. The heavenly lights are appointed servants

The sun, moon, and stars have assigned functions.

They serve the created order.

Verse 19 concludes the day in which this arrangement was established.

---

# 21. The fourth day and time

The heavenly lights were created to serve as markers of:

**seasons**

**days**

**years**

and

**appointed times.**

Verse 19 places these functions within the continuing rhythm of the creation week.

---

# 22. Time is ordered

The repeated day formula demonstrates that creation unfolds according to an ordered temporal sequence.

The fourth day has a defined beginning and completion.

---

# 23. The creation week has rhythm

Genesis 1 presents a rhythm:

**evening**

→

**morning**

→

**day**

The repeated rhythm emphasizes order and progression.

---

# 24. God's work has completion

God's work on Day Four is brought to a conclusion.

This pattern anticipates the completion of the entire creation week.

---

# 25. The phrase “there was”

The repeated phrase:

**“there was”**

emphasizes that the events described actually occurred within the narrative's framework.

The day concludes as God has ordered it.

---

# 26. The fourth day is identified explicitly

Genesis does not leave the reader to infer which day has ended.

It explicitly says:

**“the fourth day.”**

This establishes the sequence clearly.

---

# 27. The significance of numbering the days

The numbering:

**first**

**second**

**third**

**fourth**

creates a sequential framework.

Genesis presents the creation week as ordered rather than random.

---

# 28. The fourth day follows three completed days

The phrase connects Day Four with the preceding days.

The creation narrative is cumulative.

Each stage builds upon the previous work.

---

# 29. Creation as preparation

By the end of Day Four, the basic environmental order is increasingly established.

The world is being prepared for the creatures that will inhabit it.

---

# 30. Day Four prepares for Day Five

The next day will introduce:

**birds**

and

**sea creatures.**

These creatures inhabit realms already established and ordered by God.

---

# 31. Day Four prepares for Day Six

The heavenly lights also form part of the ordered environment in which land animals and humanity will live.

---

# 32. The creation week moves toward humanity

The narrative continues toward the creation of humanity.

The heavenly lights are established before humanity appears.

Human beings therefore enter an already functioning creation.

---

# 33. Humanity receives rather than creates the order

The creation narrative emphasizes that the fundamental structure of the world precedes humanity.

Humanity does not establish:

**day**

**night**

**seasons**

or

**years.**

These belong to God's prior work.

---

# 34. The goodness of the fourth day

Verse 18 has already declared the result:

**“it was good.”**

Verse 19 closes the day without introducing any failure or disorder.

---

# 35. No conflict in creation

The fourth day is presented as a successful fulfillment of God's purposes.

There is no conflict between God and the heavenly lights.

They perform the roles He assigns.

---

# 36. No struggle among gods

The passage presents no cosmic battle.

One God commands.

The heavenly lights are created.

They fulfill their functions.

The day ends.

This is a strongly ordered monotheistic presentation.

---

# 37. The Creator remains sovereign

The heavenly lights may “rule” day and night in an appointed sense.

But God remains the One who establishes their authority.

The fourth day demonstrates God's sovereignty over the entire heavenly order.

---

# 38. The creation formula emphasizes God's control

The repeated structure of Genesis 1 shows that each stage occurs under divine direction.

The fourth day is no exception.

---

# 39. Evening and morning demonstrate regularity

The repeated cycle demonstrates the regularity of God's created order.

Day Four follows the same basic pattern as the preceding days.

---

# 40. The order is stable

God's creation is characterized by meaningful sequence and stability.

The heavenly lights contribute to this order.

---

# 41. The fourth day and divine faithfulness

The continuing regularity of day and night becomes an enduring feature of creation.

This provides a foundation for later biblical reflections upon God's faithfulness.

---

# 42. The heavens and earthly life

The heavenly lights are established for the benefit and ordering of the earth.

The completion of Day Four therefore represents another stage in God's preparation of the world for life.

---

# 43. The relationship between heaven and earth

The heavenly realm and earthly realm are distinct.

Yet the heavenly lights affect the earth through:

**light**

**day**

**night**

and

**temporal cycles.**

---

# 44. God's provision for earthly life

The creation account presents the earth as receiving what it needs according to God's design.

The heavenly lights contribute to that provision.

---

# 45. The fourth day and God's wisdom

The orderly sequence of creation demonstrates wisdom.

God establishes:

**light**

then

**light-bearers**

and organizes the rhythms of the world.

---

# 46. The fourth day and God's goodness

The result of God's work is explicitly called:

**good.**

The physical world is therefore affirmed rather than rejected.

---

# 47. The goodness of time

Time itself is part of the created order.

The numbered days demonstrate that creation unfolds within an ordered temporal framework.

---

# 48. The goodness of rhythm

Evening and morning establish a recurring rhythm.

This rhythm belongs to the good structure of creation.

---

# 49. The fourth day and Sabbath theology

The six-day creation sequence eventually leads to the seventh day.

The numbered days establish the framework for the Sabbath pattern.

The completion of Day Four therefore contributes to the developing creation-week structure.

---

# 50. Creation and worship

The ordered heavens should produce worship of God.

The heavenly lights are magnificent, but their existence points toward the One who created them.

---

# 51. The heavens display God's glory

Later Scripture describes the heavens as declaring God's glory.

Genesis 1 establishes the creation foundation for that theological theme.

---

# 52. Psalm 19

Psalm 19 presents the heavens as proclaiming the glory of God.

The heavenly lights therefore become part of the visible testimony of God's creative power.

---

# 53. Psalm 8

Psalm 8 reflects upon the moon and stars as God's handiwork.

The proper response is wonder and humility before God.

---

# 54. The heavenly lights are not divine

The fourth day reinforces that the sun, moon, and stars are part of creation.

They are not the Creator.

---

# 55. The fourth day and idolatry

Because the heavenly bodies are creatures, they must not be worshiped as gods.

Their function is to serve God's order.

---

# 56. The fourth day and astrology

The heavenly bodies mark and govern created rhythms.

They are not presented as independent forces determining human destiny.

---

# 57. God's authority over celestial bodies

The heavenly lights exist according to God's appointment.

Their authority is therefore subordinate.

---

# 58. The Creator-creature distinction

Genesis maintains a clear distinction:

**God → Creator**

**heavenly lights → creatures**

This distinction is fundamental to biblical theology.

---

# 59. The fourth day and divine providence

The established day-night rhythm continues to provide an ordered environment for earthly life.

This becomes part of God's ongoing providential care.

---

# 60. The fourth day and stability

The regular cycles of creation provide a stable framework for:

**life**

**work**

**rest**

**agriculture**

and

**human society.**

---

# 61. The fourth day and human dependence

Humanity depends upon a world whose basic rhythms were established before humanity existed.

This reinforces creaturely dependence upon God.

---

# 62. The fourth day and stewardship

Because humanity enters an already ordered world, human dominion is stewardship rather than autonomous ownership.

---

# 63. The fourth day and humility

The enormous heavenly bodies are ultimately under God's authority.

This provides a reason for human humility.

---

# 64. The fourth day and wonder

The vastness and order of the heavens should produce awe.

Yet the biblical emphasis directs that awe toward God.

---

# 65. Christological perspective

Genesis 1:19 does not explicitly mention Christ.

However, the New Testament teaches that creation came through the Son.

The fourth day's heavenly order therefore belongs to the created realm ultimately made through Christ.

---

# 66. John 1:3

John teaches:

**“All things were made through him.”**

The heavenly lights and the temporal order they serve belong to the “all things” of creation.

---

# 67. Colossians 1:16

Colossians teaches that all things were created:

**“in heaven and on earth.”**

The fourth day therefore falls within the comprehensive scope of Christ's creative work.

---

# 68. Christ as Lord of creation

The heavenly lights are creatures.

Christ is presented in the New Testament as Lord over creation.

Their existence therefore ultimately points toward His authority.

---

# 69. Creation and new creation

The creation week establishes the first ordered world.

The biblical storyline eventually points toward the renewal of creation.

The fourth day therefore belongs to the larger biblical story of creation, fall, redemption, and consummation.

---

# 70. The final light

The Bible ultimately points beyond created lights to God's own glory as the supreme source of light.

Created illumination is therefore not the final reality.

God Himself is.

---

# 71. The fourth day as a completed divine work

Genesis 1:19 can therefore be viewed as a completion statement.

The fourth day's assigned work has been accomplished.

---

# 72. God's purposes are fulfilled

The heavenly lights have been:

**created**

**placed**

**appointed**

and

**ordered.**

The fourth day concludes with their successful establishment.

---

# 73. The pattern of divine completion

The repeated creation-day formula demonstrates that God's work progresses toward completion.

Day Four is one completed stage in that larger work.

---

# 74. The creation week is cumulative

Each day contributes to the whole.

The heavenly lights depend upon the expanse established earlier.

They then serve the earth prepared during the previous days.

---

# 75. God's creation is interconnected

The various elements of creation are not isolated.

They function together within God's design.

The fourth day's lights serve the realms established previously.

---

# 76. The fourth day and order

The central theme of the verse is orderly completion.

Evening.

Morning.

Fourth day.

The language establishes sequence and rhythm.

---

# 77. The fourth day and divine faithfulness

The recurring pattern demonstrates the reliability of God's established order.

The day ends as the previous days ended.

---

# 78. The importance of “fourth”

The explicit designation:

**“the fourth day”**

continues the numerical progression of the creation week.

The narrative is carefully structured.

---

# 79. The fourth day and literary symmetry

The fourth day corresponds to the first day.

The first day establishes the distinction between light and darkness.

The fourth day establishes the heavenly lights that govern those realms.

This literary symmetry strengthens the structure of Genesis 1.

---

# 80. From light to lights

A useful summary of the first and fourth days is:

**Day One: light**

**Day Four: lights**

The first establishes the phenomenon of light.

The fourth establishes the created bodies that govern the visible rhythms associated with light and darkness.

---

# 81. The fourth day and preparation

God's work continues toward filling the world with living creatures.

The fourth day prepares the temporal and luminous environment in which later creatures will live.

---

# 82. The fourth day and God's intentionality

Nothing in the verse suggests randomness.

The fourth day has:

**a specific sequence**

**a specific purpose**

**a specific completion.**

---

# 83. The fourth day and divine order

The numbered day belongs to an ordered week.

The evening and morning formula provides the temporal boundaries.

God's creation unfolds according to His design.

---

# 84. The fourth day and the created calendar

The existence of numbered days demonstrates that creation has an ordered temporal structure.

The heavenly lights later help humanity observe this structure.

---

# 85. The fourth day and human history

Human history will unfold within this created framework of:

**days**

**months**

**seasons**

and

**years.**

---

# 86. The fourth day and God's sovereignty over time

God establishes the rhythms within which created history unfolds.

Time itself does not operate independently of His creative order.

---

# 87. The fourth day and divine purpose

The day closes after God's assigned purposes for the heavenly lights have been established.

Creation functions according to divine intention.

---

# 88. The fourth day and divine goodness

The preceding verse explicitly declares the result good.

The fourth day therefore ends within the positive evaluation of God's creation.

---

# 89. The fourth day and absence of disorder

There is no indication of failure.

The lights fulfill their assigned role.

The day concludes.

---

# 90. The fourth day and God's authority

The entire fourth-day account demonstrates divine authority over:

**the heavens**

**the lights**

**the day**

**the night**

**light**

**darkness**

and

**time.**

---

# 91. The fourth day and worshipful reflection

The proper response to this passage is not merely curiosity about astronomy.

It is recognition of the Creator's wisdom, authority, and goodness.

---

# 92. The fourth day and creation theology

Genesis 1:19 reinforces the doctrine that God created an ordered universe in which every stage has a defined place.

---

# 93. The fourth day and providence

The order established during creation continues to serve the earth.

This provides the foundation for understanding God's providential governance of creation.

---

# 94. The fourth day and divine wisdom

The repeated structure of Genesis 1 demonstrates deliberate organization.

The fourth day fits into the larger pattern rather than appearing as an isolated event.

---

# 95. The fourth day and biblical anthropology

Humanity is created after the world has already been ordered.

Humans therefore begin as recipients of God's creation rather than creators of its fundamental order.

---

# 96. The fourth day and human vocation

Human dominion will operate within God's prior ordering of creation.

---

# 97. The fourth day and stewardship

Humans are called to exercise dominion responsibly within the good world God created.

The heavenly order itself demonstrates that creation functions according to appointed purposes.

---

# 98. The fourth day and God's glory

The heavenly lights reveal something of God's wisdom and power.

They are signs of the Creator's greatness.

---

# 99. The fourth day and biblical hope

The ordered creation established here becomes the foundation for the biblical story that ultimately culminates in renewed creation and the fullness of God's presence.

---

# 100. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:19 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God completed the fourth day's work of ordering the heavenly lights, and the creation week continued according to the established rhythm of evening and morning.**

Or more simply:

**The fourth day ended according to God's ordered rhythm of evening and morning.**

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:19

### **“And there was evening”**

→ The daylight period reaches its close.

### **“And there was morning”**

→ The night passes and a new daylight period begins.

### **“The fourth day”**

→ The fourth stage of God's creation week is explicitly completed.

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:19 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God establishes the temporal order of creation.
2. **Ordered creation** — creation proceeds according to a defined sequence.
3. **Creation in time** — the creation account unfolds through identifiable days.
4. **Divine purpose** — the fourth day accomplishes its assigned purpose.
5. **Divine goodness** — the work completed on the fourth day has already been declared good.
6. **Temporal rhythm** — evening and morning establish a recurring pattern.
7. **Day-night order** — the distinction between day and night belongs to God's design.
8. **Divine providence** — the ordered heavenly system serves the earth.
9. **Human dependence** — humanity enters a world already structured by God.
10. **Creator-creature distinction** — the heavenly lights remain created objects.
11. **Rejection of celestial worship** — the heavenly bodies are servants within creation.
12. **Rejection of astrology** — the heavenly bodies do not possess autonomous sovereignty over human destiny.
13. **Divine wisdom** — the structure of the creation week demonstrates purposeful design.
14. **Divine faithfulness** — the recurring rhythms of creation display stability.
15. **Sabbath theology** — the numbered creation days establish the framework leading toward the seventh-day rest.
16. **Christological creation** — the New Testament teaches that all things were created through Christ.
17. **Christological lordship** — Christ is Lord over the heavenly and earthly realms.
18. **God's glory** — the ordered heavens reveal the wisdom and power of their Creator.
19. **New creation** — the original ordered creation points forward to the final renewal of all things.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:19 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD COMPLETES HIS WORK IN AN ORDERED RHYTHM

**“And there was evening and there was morning…”**

God's creative work proceeds according to an established rhythm.

---

## II. GOD'S CREATION PROGRESSES ACCORDING TO HIS DESIGN

**“The fourth day.”**

The fourth day is a defined stage within God's ordered creation week.

---

## III. GOD'S GOOD CREATION MOVES TOWARD COMPLETION

The fourth day closes successfully, preparing the way for the next stages of creation.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:19 teaches that the fourth day of creation was completed according to God's established rhythm of evening and morning, demonstrating the orderly, purposeful, and progressive character of God's creative work.**

The progression from Genesis 1:14 through Genesis 1:19 is:

**Genesis 1:14**

God commands the heavenly lights:

**“Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens.”**

Their purposes include:

**separating day from night**

**marking seasons**

**marking days**

**marking years**

**Genesis 1:15**

Their purpose:

**“to give light upon the earth.”**

And:

**“it was so.”**

**Genesis 1:16**

God makes:

**the greater light**

**the lesser light**

**the stars**

**Genesis 1:17**

God places them:

**“in the expanse of the heavens”**

to:

**“give light on the earth.”**

**Genesis 1:18**

God assigns their functions:

**greater light → rules the day**

**lesser light → rules the night**

and they participate in:

**separating light from darkness.**

God evaluates the result:

**“it was good.”**

**Genesis 1:19**

The fourth day concludes:

**“And there was evening and there was morning, the fourth day.”**

The pattern is:

**God commands.**

**God creates.**

**God places.**

**God appoints.**

**Creation functions according to His design.**

**God declares it good.**

**The day concludes.**
