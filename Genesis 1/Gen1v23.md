# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:23

**Genesis 1:23 —** “And there was evening and there was morning, the fifth day.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר יוֹם חֲמִישִׁי׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayəhî-ʿereḇ wayəhî-ḇōqer yôm ḥămîšî*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And there was evening, and there was morning, the fifth day.”**

---

# 1. “And there was evening” — וַיְהִי־עֶרֶב (*wayəhî-ʿereḇ*)

Genesis 1:23 begins with the familiar creation-day formula:

**“And there was evening…”**

The Hebrew verb:

**הָיָה (*hāyâ*)**

means:

**“to be”**

**“to become”**

or

**“to come to pass.”**

The noun:

**עֶרֶב (*ʿereḇ*)**

means:

**“evening.”**

---

# 2. Evening marks the completion of the day

The appearance of evening marks the conclusion of the activity of the fifth day.

The narrative has moved through:

**command**

→

**creation**

→

**blessing**

→

**fruitfulness**

→

**completion.**

---

# 3. “And there was morning” — וַיְהִי־בֹקֶר (*wayəhî-ḇōqer*)

The formula continues:

**“and there was morning.”**

The Hebrew noun:

**בֹּקֶר (*bōqer*)**

means:

**“morning.”**

The evening and morning together frame the fifth day.

---

# 4. Evening and morning

The repeated expression:

**“evening and morning”**

appears throughout Genesis 1.

It provides a recognizable boundary for each creation day.

---

# 5. “The fifth day” — יוֹם חֲמִישִׁי (*yôm ḥămîšî*)

The Hebrew phrase literally means:

**“day fifth.”**

The noun:

**יוֹם (*yôm*)**

means:

**“day.”**

The adjective:

**חֲמִישִׁי (*ḥămîšî*)**

means:

**“fifth.”**

---

# 6. The fifth day is complete

Genesis 1:23 formally concludes Day Five.

The day began with God's command concerning the waters and birds.

It included:

**the creation of aquatic life**

**the creation of winged creatures**

**divine blessing**

**fruitfulness**

and

**multiplication.**

---

# 7. The fifth day is a complete unit

Genesis presents the fifth day as a defined period within the creation account.

It begins and ends within the narrative's repeated day formula.

---

# 8. Day Five corresponds to Day Two

There is a deliberate structural relationship between the creation days.

### Day Two

God establishes:

**the waters**

**the expanse**

### Day Five

God fills:

**the waters with living creatures**

**the sky with winged creatures**

This is part of the larger forming-and-filling structure of Genesis 1.

---

# 9. The forming-and-filling pattern

Genesis 1 can be observed in two broad movements.

### Forming

**Day One → light and darkness**

**Day Two → waters and expanse**

**Day Three → dry land and vegetation**

### Filling

**Day Four → heavenly lights**

**Day Five → sea creatures and birds**

**Day Six → land animals and humanity**

Genesis 1:23 closes the second major filling movement.

---

# 10. Day Five and abundance

Day Five is characterized by abundant life.

Verse 20 introduced swarming waters.

Verse 21 described God's creation of the creatures.

Verse 22 recorded God's blessing.

Verse 23 closes the day.

---

# 11. The sequence of Day Five

The narrative sequence can therefore be summarized:

**God speaks**

→

**God creates**

→

**God blesses**

→

**creatures multiply**

→

**the day ends.**

---

# 12. God's work is orderly

The repeated day formula demonstrates that the creation account is presented in an ordered sequence.

God's creative work is not described as chaotic or accidental.

---

# 13. The creation account has structure

The repetition of:

**“And there was evening and there was morning…”**

provides literary structure to Genesis 1.

---

# 14. Repetition emphasizes completion

The formula repeatedly signals that a particular stage of God's creative work has reached completion.

---

# 15. Day Five and God's blessing

The fifth day is especially notable because it contains the first explicit divine blessing in Genesis.

God blesses the creatures and commands them to multiply.

---

# 16. Blessing before completion

The order is significant:

**creation**

→

**evaluation**

→

**blessing**

→

**completion.**

The day closes after God's blessing has been pronounced.

---

# 17. God's blessing produces flourishing

The fifth day does not merely introduce living creatures.

It introduces a world capable of continuing to flourish through multiplication.

---

# 18. God's creative work is fruitful

The creatures created on Day Five are blessed with the capacity to reproduce.

The fifth day therefore emphasizes both:

**creation**

and

**continuation.**

---

# 19. Life continues beyond the initial creation

Genesis 1:22 shows that God established reproductive processes through which creatures would multiply.

Verse 23 confirms the completion of this stage.

---

# 20. The fifth day anticipates the sixth day

The narrative now moves toward the creation of land animals and humanity.

Day Five prepares the reader for the climax of the creation week.

---

# 21. The movement toward humanity

The creation narrative progresses toward the creation of humanity on Day Six.

The fifth day is therefore part of the preparation for that climax.

---

# 22. The fifth day is not the final day

Although the seas and skies have been filled, the land still awaits its creatures and humanity.

The creation account continues.

---

# 23. Creation unfolds progressively

The narrative moves from:

**light**

to

**separation**

to

**land and vegetation**

to

**heavenly lights**

to

**animal life**

and finally

**humanity.**

---

# 24. Day Five as a major transition

Day Five marks the transition from the creation of non-living environments and vegetation toward the widespread creation of animal life.

---

# 25. The first animal life in Genesis 1

Day Five introduces the first animals specifically described in the creation sequence:

**sea creatures**

and

**winged creatures.**

---

# 26. The goodness of animal life

Verse 21 declared the creatures:

**good.**

Verse 22 blessed them.

Verse 23 concludes the day.

Thus the fifth day presents animal life positively from beginning to end.

---

# 27. Creation is good and ordered

The completion formula reinforces the orderly progression of the good creation.

---

# 28. God's sovereignty over time

Just as God governs the realms of creation, He governs the sequence and boundaries of the days.

---

# 29. God establishes rhythms

The repeated pattern of:

**evening**

and

**morning**

establishes a rhythm within creation.

---

# 30. The rhythm of creation

Creation is presented as moving through ordered cycles:

**evening**

→

**morning**

→

**day.**

---

# 31. Time belongs to God

Genesis does not present time as an independent force controlling God.

God establishes the ordered sequence within which His creative work unfolds.

---

# 32. The fifth day has a beginning and an end

Like the previous creation days, Day Five is bounded by the narrative's temporal formula.

---

# 33. “Day” — יוֹם (*yôm*)

The Hebrew noun **יוֹם** has a range of meanings depending on context.

It can refer to:

**a day**

**daylight**

or

**a period of time.**

In Genesis 1, the repeated references to evening and morning provide an important contextual framework for understanding the creation days.

---

# 34. Evening and morning as temporal markers

The combination of:

**evening**

and

**morning**

functions as a temporal marker within the narrative.

---

# 35. The fifth day is numbered

Unlike some poetic references to time, Genesis explicitly numbers this day:

**“the fifth day.”**

---

# 36. Numbered creation days

Genesis 1 identifies successive days as:

**first**

**second**

**third**

**fourth**

**fifth**

**sixth**

This gives the creation account a clear sequence.

---

# 37. Day Five and the Sabbath structure

The six days of creation lead toward the seventh-day rest described in Genesis 2:1–3.

The fifth day is therefore part of the larger six-plus-one pattern.

---

# 38. Creation and covenantal rhythm

Later biblical teaching concerning Sabbath rest is grounded in the creation pattern.

The ordered rhythm of work and rest begins in God's own creative activity.

---

# 39. God's work has purposeful progression

The creation account does not depict God as endlessly producing unrelated things.

Each day contributes to the developing order of creation.

---

# 40. Day Five fills previously established realms

The creatures of Day Five correspond to environments established earlier.

This demonstrates purposeful progression.

---

# 41. The waters and expanse

Day Two established:

**waters**

and

**expanse.**

Day Five fills these realms.

---

# 42. The fifth day completes another stage of filling

The seas are populated.

The aerial realm is populated.

The created world becomes increasingly full.

---

# 43. Fullness before humanity

Before humanity appears, God creates an increasingly abundant world.

Humanity is placed into a creation already prepared and populated.

---

# 44. The generosity of God

The abundance of Day Five reflects the generosity of the Creator.

The world is not merely functional; it is richly populated with living things.

---

# 45. The diversity of creation

The creatures of Day Five include numerous kinds.

The day therefore displays remarkable biological diversity within the biblical account.

---

# 46. God's ordered diversity

Verse 21 repeatedly used:

**“according to their kinds.”**

Verse 22 commands:

**“be fruitful and multiply.”**

Verse 23 concludes this stage.

The sequence shows:

**diversity**

plus

**reproduction**

within

**divine order.**

---

# 47. God's blessing continues beyond the verse

The blessing pronounced in verse 22 establishes a pattern that extends beyond the immediate moment.

The creatures are to continue multiplying.

---

# 48. Creation includes future generations

The fifth day therefore points beyond the first generation of creatures toward future generations.

---

# 49. Divine providence and time

The passing of the fifth day reminds the reader that God's creation includes not only things but also ordered time.

---

# 50. God creates within an ordered temporal framework

The creation account portrays God as establishing both:

**spatial order**

and

**temporal order.**

---

# 51. Spatial order

Examples include:

**waters**

**expanse**

**dry land**

**seas.**

---

# 52. Temporal order

Examples include:

**evening**

**morning**

**first day**

**second day**

**fifth day.**

---

# 53. God is Lord over both

Genesis therefore presents God as sovereign over:

**space**

and

**time.**

---

# 54. The fifth day and divine rest

The fifth day moves the creation account closer to the seventh-day Sabbath.

God's work is progressing toward completion.

---

# 55. Creation is moving toward completion

The sequence is now:

**Days One–Three → realms established**

**Days Four–Six → realms populated**

**Day Seven → God rests.**

Day Five occupies the middle of the second triad.

---

# 56. Day Five within the six-day pattern

The second triad can be summarized:

### Day Four

**lights**

### Day Five

**birds and sea creatures**

### Day Six

**land animals and humanity**

This progression leads toward the climax of humanity's creation.

---

# 57. The fifth day is significant for biblical anthropology

Although humanity has not yet been created, the fifth day establishes the animal world over which humanity will later exercise dominion.

---

# 58. Dominion presupposes creation

Human dominion in Genesis 1:26–28 is meaningful because God has already created a world containing living creatures.

---

# 59. Humanity enters an established order

Human beings do not create the world they govern.

They receive responsibility within a world created by God.

---

# 60. Stewardship follows creation

Because God creates and blesses the animals before humanity receives dominion, human authority must be understood as delegated stewardship.

---

# 61. The fifth day and creaturely dignity

The creatures are created by God.

They are declared good.

They are blessed.

They multiply.

These observations demonstrate their significance within God's created order.

---

# 62. Animal life is part of God's purpose

The fifth day demonstrates that God's purpose includes the flourishing of non-human life.

---

# 63. God's work is worthy of evaluation

The phrase:

**“and God saw…”**

from verse 21 is followed by the completion of the day.

God's evaluation stands.

---

# 64. The fifth day is approved

The day ends after the creatures have been created and blessed.

Nothing in the text suggests divine dissatisfaction.

---

# 65. The pattern of goodness

The creation account repeatedly records God's assessment of His work.

This reinforces the goodness of the created order.

---

# 66. The goodness of time

The ordered passage of days is itself part of God's creation structure.

---

# 67. Creation has rhythm

The repeated evening-and-morning formula establishes a rhythm of:

**beginning**

**activity**

**completion.**

---

# 68. God's work is purposeful

The completion of each day indicates movement toward an intended goal.

---

# 69. Day Five demonstrates divine faithfulness

God's command in verse 20 is fulfilled.

The creatures appear.

They are blessed.

The day reaches its appointed completion.

---

# 70. God's word accomplishes His purpose

The fifth day demonstrates again that God's spoken command results in reality.

---

# 71. The command-fulfillment pattern

The structure is:

**God says**

→

**creation occurs**

→

**God evaluates**

→

**God blesses**

→

**the day concludes.**

---

# 72. Creation and blessing belong together

God's creative work is followed by blessing.

This demonstrates His generous purpose toward the creatures He has made.

---

# 73. Blessing and multiplication belong together

God blesses the creatures by commanding them to be fruitful and multiply.

---

# 74. Multiplication and filling belong together

The creatures multiply so that the waters and earth become increasingly full of life.

---

# 75. Fullness and goodness belong together

The abundant creation of life is declared good.

---

# 76. The theological movement

Genesis 1:20–23 can therefore be traced as:

**command**

→

**creation**

→

**goodness**

→

**blessing**

**multiplication**

→

**completion.**

---

# 77. The fifth day points forward

The fifth day prepares the reader for Day Six, when God creates land animals and humanity.

---

# 78. The sixth day will introduce humanity

The next major movement will concern:

**land animals**

and

**human beings.**

---

# 79. The climax is approaching

The creation account is moving toward humanity's creation in God's image.

---

# 80. Yet the animals remain important

The fact that humanity is the climax does not make the earlier creatures insignificant.

They are repeatedly described as God's good creation.

---

# 81. The creation account is God-centered

The repeated subject of the creation account is:

**God.**

and

**evaluates.**

# 82. God-centered interpretation

Genesis 1:23 therefore should not be isolated from the larger God-centered narrative.

The point is not merely that another day passed.

The point is that God's ordered creative work continued according to His purpose.

---

# 83. The sovereignty of the Creator

The fifth day demonstrates God's authority over:

**life**

**habitats**

**reproduction**

and

**time.**

---

# 84. The goodness of creation

The fifth day belongs to the repeated biblical declaration that God's creation is good.

---

# 85. The created order is purposeful

# 86. The creation week has direction

The narrative moves toward:

**fullness**

**humanity**

and ultimately

**Sabbath rest.**

---

# 87. Day Five is part of the divine work

The fifth day should therefore be read as an essential stage in God's comprehensive creation.

---

# 88. The fifth day and worship

The ordered and abundant creation can lead to worship because it reveals God's:

**power**

**wisdom**

**goodness**

and

**sovereignty.**

# 89. The theological significance of “fifth day”

The designation confirms that God's creative work occurs within an ordered progression rather than an undefined sequence.

# 90. The importance of completion

The phrase:

**“And there was evening and there was morning”**

marks the completion of another stage of God's work.

# 91. God's work moves toward completion

The fifth day is complete, but the creation week is not yet complete.

**Day Six**

and

**Day Seven.**

# 92. Creation is not yet finished

# 94. The fifth day as preparation for humanity

# 96. The order of divine provision

God creates:

**environment**

# 97. The fifth day and providence

God's blessing upon the creatures indicates His intention for their continuing existence.

# 98. The fifth day and divine generosity

# 99. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:23 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**The fifth day concludes as God completes the creation and blessing of the creatures of the seas and skies, establishing another ordered stage in His good creation.**

Or more simply:

**The fifth day comes to its appointed completion within God's orderly and purposeful creation.**

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:23

### **“And there was evening”**

→ The fifth day reaches its evening boundary.

### **“And there was morning”**

→ The creation-day cycle is completed.

### **“The fifth day”**

→ The day is explicitly identified within the ordered sequence of creation.

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:23 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God's sovereignty over time** — the creation days occur within God's ordered work.
2. **The orderliness of creation** — creation unfolds according to a structured sequence.
3. **The goodness of creation** — Day Five concludes after the creation of good living creatures.
4. **The importance of divine blessing** — the creatures created on Day Five have been blessed by God.
5. **The continuation of life** — God's creatures are designed for fruitfulness and multiplication.
6. **The forming-and-filling pattern** — Day Five fills realms established earlier.
7. **Divine providence** — God's blessing provides for the continuation of creaturely life.
8. **Creator-creature distinction** — God remains the sovereign Creator over the creatures.
9. **Human stewardship** — humanity will later receive responsibility over an already established animal creation.
10. **The progression of creation** — Day Five prepares for the creation of land animals and humanity.
11. **The six-day creation pattern** — the fifth day belongs to the ordered sequence leading to Sabbath.
12. **The rhythm of time** — evening and morning establish recurring temporal boundaries.
13. **Divine purpose** — creation proceeds toward an intended completion.
14. **Divine wisdom** — established realms are progressively populated.
15. **Divine generosity** — the fifth day produces abundant life.
16. **The goodness of animal life** — the creatures of Day Five have already been declared good.
17. **The goodness of fruitfulness** — multiplication is part of God's original blessing.
18. **God-centered creation** — God's actions remain the central focus of the narrative.
19. **Creation as preparation** — the world is progressively prepared for humanity.
20. **Sabbath theology** — the completion of the fifth day contributes to the six-day work pattern that culminates in divine rest.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:23 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD WORKS IN AN ORDERED CREATION

**“And there was evening and there was morning…”**

God's creative activity unfolds according to an ordered pattern.

---

## II. GOD BRINGS HIS WORK TO APPOINTED COMPLETION

**“The fifth day.”**

Each stage of creation reaches its appointed conclusion.

---

## III. GOD IS MOVING CREATION TOWARD ITS INTENDED GOAL

Day Five ends, but God's work continues toward:

**land animals**

→

**humanity**

→

**Sabbath rest.**

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:23 teaches that the fifth day came to its appointed completion within God's orderly creation, following His creation, blessing, and multiplication of the creatures of the seas and skies.**

The progression from Genesis 1:20 through Genesis 1:23 is:

**Genesis 1:20**

God commands:

**“Let the waters swarm.”**

**“Let birds fly.”**

**Genesis 1:21**

God creates:

**sea creatures**

**winged creatures**

God declares:

**“it was good.”**

**Genesis 1:22**

God blesses:

**“Be fruitful.”**

**“Multiply.”**

**“Fill the waters.”**

**“Let birds multiply.”**

**Genesis 1:23**

The fifth day concludes:

**“There was evening.”**

**“There was morning.”**

**“The fifth day.”**

The pattern is:

**God speaks.**

**God creates.**

**God evaluates.**

**God blesses.**

**Creation flourishes.**

**The day is completed.**
