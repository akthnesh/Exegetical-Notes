# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:26

**Genesis 1:26 —** “Then God said, ‘Let us make man in our image, after our likeness. And let them have dominion over the fish of the sea and over the birds of the heavens and over the livestock and over all the earth and over every creeping thing that creeps on the earth.’”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים נַעֲשֶׂה אָדָם בְּצַלְמֵנוּ כִּדְמוּתֵנוּ וְיִרְדּוּ בִדְגַת הַיָּם וּבְעוֹף הַשָּׁמַיִם וּבַבְּהֵמָה וּבְכָל־הָאָרֶץ וּבְכָל־הָרֶמֶשׂ הָרֹמֵשׂ עַל־הָאָרֶץ׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm naʿăśeh ʾādām bəṣalmēnû kiḏmûṯēnû wəyirdû biḏgaṯ hayyām ûḇəʿôp̄ haššāmayim ûḇabəhēmâ ûḇəḵol-hāʾāreṣ ûḇəḵol-hāremeś hārōmēś ʿal-hāʾāreṣ*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And God said, ‘Let us make humankind in our image, according to our likeness; and let them rule over the fish of the sea, and over the birds of the heavens, and over the livestock, and over all the earth, and over every creeping thing that creeps upon the earth.’”**

---

# 1. “Then God said” — וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים (*wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:26 begins once again with:

**“Then God said…”**

The creation of humanity begins with divine speech.

This continues the repeated pattern of Genesis 1:

**God speaks → God creates → creation comes into being.**

---

# 2. A Major Transition

Genesis 1:26 marks a dramatic transition in the creation narrative.

Previous verses have described:

**light**

**the heavens**

**the seas**

**vegetation**

**the heavenly lights**

**sea creatures**

**birds**

**land animals.**

Now the narrative reaches humanity.

---

# 3. Humanity as the Climax of the Creation Week

The creation of humanity occupies a unique position within Genesis 1.

Human beings are introduced only after the other major realms and creatures have been prepared.

---

# 4. “Let Us Make” — נַעֲשֶׂה (*naʿăśeh*)

The Hebrew verb:

**עָשָׂה (*ʿāśâ*)**

means:

**“to make”**

**“to do”**

or

**“to accomplish.”**

The first-person plural form is:

**נַעֲשֶׂה — “let us make.”**

---

# 5. The Divine Plural

The phrase:

**“Let us make…”**

has generated extensive discussion throughout the history of biblical interpretation.

The immediate text clearly identifies **God** as the speaker.

The plural wording, however, invites theological reflection.

---

# 6. The “Us” of Genesis 1:26

Several major interpretations have been proposed.

These include:

1. A divine-council setting.
2. A plural of deliberation.
3. A majestic or deliberative plural.
4. A Trinitarian reading.

The text itself does not explicitly explain the grammatical reason for the plural.

---

# 7. The Divine Council Interpretation

Some interpreters understand the plural as language spoken in the presence of a heavenly divine council.

On this reading, God announces His intention before His heavenly attendants.

However, the following verse emphasizes that **God Himself creates humanity**.

---

# 8. The Deliberative Interpretation

Another interpretation understands:

**“Let us make…”**

as deliberative language.

The speaker expresses a solemn decision:

**“Let us make…”**

This interpretation emphasizes the deliberate nature of God's creative act.

---

# 9. The Trinitarian Reading

Christian theology has traditionally recognized Genesis 1:26 as compatible with the doctrine of the Trinity.

The plural language can be understood retrospectively in light of God's fuller revelation in Scripture.

However, Genesis 1:26 by itself does not explicitly state the doctrine of the Trinity.

---

# 10. The Importance of Context

Whatever interpretation is adopted concerning **“us,”** the immediate context establishes one essential truth:

**God is the Creator of humanity.**

Genesis 1:27 immediately returns to singular language:

**“So God created man…”**

---

# 11. “Let Us Make Man” — אָדָם (*ʾādām*)

The Hebrew noun:

**אָדָם (*ʾādām*)**

can refer to:

**Adam**

**a human being**

**humankind**

or

**humanity**

depending on context.

Here the context concerns humanity collectively.

---

# 12. “Man” in the ESV

The ESV translates:

**“Let us make man…”**

In this context, “man” refers to humanity rather than merely the male sex.

Verse 27 clarifies this by referring to:

**male and female.**

---

# 13. Humanity as a Collective

The singular term **ʾādām** can function collectively.

The following verses demonstrate that humanity encompasses both:

**male**

and

**female.**

---

# 14. The Creation of Humanity Is Unique

The language surrounding humanity differs significantly from the language used for the animals.

God does not merely say:

**“Let the earth bring forth…”**

Instead:

**“Let us make man in our image…”**

---

# 15. “In Our Image” — בְּצַלְמֵנוּ (*bəṣalmēnû*)

The Hebrew noun:

**צֶלֶם (*ṣelem*)**

means:

**“image”**

**“likeness”**

or

**“representation.”**

The suffix:

**־נוּ (*-nû*)**

means:

**“our.”**

Thus:

**בְּצַלְמֵנוּ**

means:

**“in our image.”**

---

# 16. The Image of God

The phrase:

**“in our image”**

is one of the most significant statements concerning humanity in the entire Bible.

Human beings possess a unique relationship to God within creation.

---

# 17. Humanity Reflects God

The concept of God's image indicates that humanity has been created to represent or reflect God within the created order.

---

# 18. Image Does Not Mean Physical Resemblance

The text should not be understood as teaching that God possesses a human physical body that humanity literally reproduces.

The biblical context points toward a representative and relational significance.

---

# 19. Representation

An image can function as a representation of something or someone.

Humanity is therefore uniquely appointed to represent God's rule within creation.

---

# 20. “After Our Likeness” — כִּדְמוּתֵנוּ (*kiḏmûṯēnû*)

The Hebrew noun:

**דְּמוּת (*dəmûṯ*)**

means:

**“likeness”**

**“resemblance”**

or

**“similarity.”**

Again, the possessive suffix means:

**“our.”**

---

# 21. Image and Likeness

Genesis 1:26 uses two closely related concepts:

**image**

and

**likeness.**

The text therefore strongly emphasizes humanity's unique correspondence to God.

---

# 22. “Image” and “Likeness” Are Closely Related

The two terms should not necessarily be treated as describing two completely separate components of humanity.

Together they emphasize humanity's distinctive status in relation to God.

---

# 23. The Image Is Given by God

Human beings do not achieve God's image through personal accomplishment.

God creates humanity **in** His image.

---

# 24. The Image Is Creational

The image of God belongs to humanity by virtue of God's creative act.

---

# 25. Human Dignity Is God-Given

Human dignity therefore does not ultimately depend upon:

**wealth**

**social status**

**intelligence**

**strength**

**productivity**

or

**social usefulness.**

It originates in God's creative purpose.

---

# 26. The Image Is Given to Humanity

The text does not say that only certain humans are made in God's image.

The following verse applies the statement to humanity as a whole.

---

# 27. Male and Female

Genesis 1:27 explicitly states:

**“male and female he created them.”**

Therefore both male and female equally participate in humanity's image-bearing identity.

---

# 28. Equality of Human Dignity

The creation account provides a foundation for affirming the equal fundamental dignity of men and women.

Both belong to the humanity created in God's image.

---

# 29. The Image and Human Identity

Human identity is therefore fundamentally connected to God's creative purpose.

Human beings are not merely highly developed animals.

They occupy a distinctive role within creation.

---

# 30. Humanity and the Animal Kingdom

Human beings share creatureliness with animals.

Yet humanity is uniquely described as being made:

**“in our image.”**

---

# 31. Humanity's Unique Status

Genesis therefore presents a distinction:

**Animals → living creatures**

**Humanity → image-bearers of God**

---

# 32. Image and Dominion

Genesis 1:26 immediately connects the image of God with:

**dominion.**

This connection is extremely important.

---

# 33. “Let Them Have Dominion” — וְיִרְדּוּ (*wəyirdû*)

The Hebrew verb:

**רָדָה (*rādâ*)**

means:

**“to rule”**

**“to have dominion”**

or

**“to exercise authority.”**

The plural form:

**וְיִרְדּוּ**

means:

**“and let them rule.”**

---

# 34. Humanity Receives Authority

Human beings are appointed to exercise authority over the animal creation.

This authority is delegated by God.

---

# 35. Dominion Is Not Independent Sovereignty

Humanity does not become sovereign in the absolute sense.

God remains the Creator.

Human beings exercise authority because God grants it.

---

# 36. Delegated Dominion

The structure is:

**God → Creator and ultimate King**

**Humanity → delegated ruler**

**Animals → creatures under human stewardship**

---

# 37. Image and Dominion

The connection between image and dominion suggests that humanity represents God's rule within the created world.

---

# 38. The Royal Function of Humanity

Human beings are given a royal or representative role within creation.

They are to exercise authority under God's authority.

---

# 39. “Over the Fish of the Sea”

The first domain is:

**the fish of the sea.**

This corresponds to the creatures created on Day Five.

---

# 40. “Over the Birds of the Heavens”

The second domain is:

**the birds of the heavens.**

Again, these correspond to the creatures created on Day Five.

---

# 41. “Over the Livestock”

The third domain is:

**livestock.**

These belong to the terrestrial creatures created on Day Six.

---

# 42. “Over All the Earth”

The scope then expands:

**“over all the earth.”**

This is broader than merely ruling over animals.

---

# 43. Humanity's Relationship to the Earth

Humanity is given a comprehensive responsibility within the terrestrial creation.

The earth itself falls within humanity's sphere of delegated rule.

---

# 44. “Over Every Creeping Thing”

The final category is:

**“every creeping thing that creeps on the earth.”**

Even the smallest ground-moving creatures fall within the stated scope of human dominion.

---

# 45. Comprehensive Dominion

Genesis 1:26 therefore describes humanity's authority across:

**sea**

**sky**

**livestock**

**earth**

**creeping creatures.**

---

# 46. The Scope of Human Responsibility

The command is comprehensive.

Humanity is not assigned authority over merely one type of creature.

---

# 47. Dominion and Stewardship

Biblical dominion should be understood as responsible rule under God.

The animals already belong to God's good creation.

---

# 48. Dominion Is Not Exploitation

Nothing in Genesis 1:26 gives humanity permission to treat creation as worthless or to destroy it arbitrarily.

Dominion is a delegated responsibility.

---

# 49. The Creator Remains the Owner

Human beings receive authority.

They do not replace God as Creator.

---

# 50. Humanity as God's Representative

The image and dominion concepts together support the idea that humanity represents God's authority within creation.

---

# 51. The Earth as God's Kingdom

The created world remains God's realm.

Humanity is appointed to exercise delegated authority within it.

---

# 52. Human Responsibility

Because authority is delegated, humans are accountable to the One who granted it.

---

# 53. Dominion and Accountability

Human authority therefore carries responsibility.

---

# 54. The Image of God and Ethics

The doctrine of the image of God has major implications for human ethics.

If humanity bears God's image, human beings possess extraordinary dignity.

---

# 55. Human Life Is Sacred in a Unique Sense

The image of God provides the theological foundation for the distinctive value placed upon human life throughout Scripture.

---

# 56. Genesis 9 and the Image of God

After the Flood, Genesis 9:6 grounds the prohibition of murder in humanity's creation:

**“for God made man in his own image.”**

Thus the image doctrine has direct ethical consequences.

---

# 57. The Image Survives the Fall

Genesis 9 demonstrates that the image of God remains significant after humanity's fall into sin.

---

# 58. The Fall Does Not Erase Human Dignity

Human sin does not eliminate the fundamental creational reality that humanity bears God's image.

---

# 59. James 3 and the Image

James 3:9 likewise refers to human beings as those:

**“made in the likeness of God.”**

This provides a New Testament ethical application of the creation doctrine.

---

# 60. Human Treatment of Other Humans

Because people bear God's image, human beings should not curse, dehumanize, or unjustly harm one another.

---

# 61. Human Equality

The image doctrine establishes a profound basis for recognizing the dignity of every human being.

---

# 62. The Image and Human Community

Humanity is created as a community of:

**male**

and

**female.**

The image-bearing vocation is therefore not presented merely as an individual calling.

---

# 63. “Let Them Have Dominion”

Notice the plural:

**“let them have dominion.”**

Humanity's dominion is a shared vocation.

---

# 64. Men and Women Share the Dominion Mandate

Genesis 1:27–28 indicates that both male and female participate in the image-bearing and dominion vocation.

---

# 65. Humanity's Royal Calling

Humanity is appointed to exercise wise authority over the created world.

---

# 66. Dominion and Cultivation

The next verse expands the mandate:

**“Be fruitful and multiply and fill the earth and subdue it.”**

Genesis 1:26 introduces this royal vocation.

---

# 67. Humanity Is Created for Purpose

Humanity is not created merely to exist.

Humanity is created with a mission:

**represent God**

**exercise dominion**

**cultivate creation**

**fill the earth.**

---

# 68. The Image and Vocation

The image of God is connected to humanity's calling.

Humanity is to live as God's representative within creation.

---

# 69. Humanity as Vice-Regent

A useful theological description is:

**vice-regent**

or

**delegated ruler.**

Human beings rule under God rather than instead of God.

---

# 70. Dominion Requires Wisdom

Because humanity rules God's creation, human authority should be exercised according to God's purposes.

---

# 71. Dominion Requires Goodness

God's own evaluation of creation is:

**“good.”**

Human rule should therefore seek the flourishing of God's creation.

---

# 72. Dominion Requires Faithfulness

Delegated authority implies accountability to the one who delegated it.

---

# 73. Humanity Is Not the Ultimate Standard

Human beings are rulers within creation but are themselves creatures.

---

# 74. Creator and Creature

Genesis preserves the fundamental distinction:

**God → Creator**

**Humanity → image-bearing creature**

**Animals → created creatures**

---

# 75. Human Exaltation and Humility

Humanity has extraordinary dignity.

Yet humanity remains dependent upon God.

The image of God should therefore produce both:

**dignity**

and

**humility.**

---

# 76. Humanity's Dependence on God

Humanity does not create itself.

Humanity receives:

**life**

**identity**

**vocation**

and

**authority**

from God.

---

# 77. Human Authority Is Received

Genesis does not portray humanity as taking dominion.

God gives it.

---

# 78. The Gift of Dominion

The phrase:

**“let them have dominion”**

shows that authority is a divine gift and assignment.

---

# 79. Dominion and Responsibility

A gift of authority creates responsibility.

Human beings must exercise dominion faithfully.

---

# 80. Dominion and the Animal Kingdom

The animals are not divine competitors.

They are creatures placed within humanity's sphere of responsibility.

---

# 81. Dominion and the Goodness of Animals

Because God has declared animal life good, humanity's dominion should preserve and cultivate the goodness of creation.

---

# 82. Humanity's Relationship to Nature

Genesis rejects two extremes:

**worship of nature**

and

**contempt for nature.**

Nature is God's creation, not God Himself.

Yet it is also genuinely good and therefore worthy of responsible care.

---

# 83. Creation Is Not Divine

Human beings are made in God's image.

Animals are not described as gods.

The earth is not divine.

God alone is Creator.

---

# 84. The Image Is Unique to Humanity

Genesis gives humanity a special status that is not assigned to the animals in the same way.

---

# 85. Humanity's Unique Relationship With God

The image language establishes an unparalleled relationship between humanity and God within the creation account.

---

# 86. The Image and Representation

Humanity represents God within creation by exercising delegated rule.

---

# 87. The Image and Moral Responsibility

Because humans represent God, their behavior has moral significance.

---

# 88. Human Authority Has Moral Limits

Human beings cannot legitimately exercise dominion contrary to God's purposes.

---

# 89. Dominion Under God's Word

The authority given in Genesis 1:26 exists within the larger command structure of Genesis.

God defines humanity's vocation.

---

# 90. The Image and the Kingdom of God

Humanity's original vocation anticipates the biblical theme of God's kingdom being represented through faithful human rule.

---

# 91. Adam as Representative

The creation of Adam and humanity establishes a representative dimension that becomes important throughout biblical theology.

---

# 92. Humanity's Representative Role

Adam is not merely an isolated individual.

He represents humanity within God's created order.

---

# 93. Corporate Humanity

The phrase:

**“let them have dominion”**

points toward humanity's collective vocation.

---

# 94. Male and Female Together

The image-bearing vocation belongs to both:

**male**

and

**female.**

---

# 95. Humanity Is Relational

The creation account will immediately reveal that humanity exists in differentiated male-and-female form.

This becomes foundational for biblical teaching concerning human relationships, marriage, family, and community.

---

# 96. Human Identity and Sexual Differentiation

Genesis 1:27 identifies two sexes:

**male**

and

**female.**

Both share the divine image.

---

# 97. Equality Without Erasing Distinction

Genesis presents equality of image-bearing dignity alongside the distinction of male and female.

---

# 98. Humanity's Calling Is Shared

The dominion mandate belongs to humanity as a whole.

---

# 99. The Central Theological Proposition

If Genesis 1:26 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God created humanity uniquely in His image and likeness, appointing human beings to exercise delegated dominion over the earth and its living creatures.**

Or more simply:

**God made humanity in His image to represent His rule within creation.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:26

### **“Then God said”**

→ Humanity's creation begins with God's authoritative word.

### **“Let us make”**

→ God deliberately announces the creation of humanity.

### **“Man”**

→ Humanity is brought into existence as a distinct category within creation.

### **“In our image”**

→ Humanity uniquely represents and reflects God.

### **“After our likeness”**

→ Humanity possesses a distinctive correspondence to God.

### **“Let them have dominion”**

→ Humanity receives delegated authority.

### **“Over the fish of the sea”**

→ Dominion extends over aquatic creatures.

### **“Over the birds of the heavens”**

→ Dominion extends over flying creatures.

### **“Over the livestock”**

→ Dominion extends over terrestrial livestock.

### **“Over all the earth”**

→ Humanity's responsibility extends broadly throughout the terrestrial realm.

### **“Over every creeping thing”**

→ Even ground-moving creatures fall within humanity's assigned sphere of authority.

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:26 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God is the Creator of humanity** — human existence originates in God's creative purpose.
2. **Humanity is uniquely made in God's image** — human beings possess a distinctive status within creation.
3. **The image of God is God-given** — it is received through creation rather than earned.
4. **Human dignity is foundational** — human worth originates in God's design.
5. **Male and female equally bear God's image** — the following verse applies the image to both.
6. **Humanity is distinct from animals** — humans are living creatures but uniquely image-bearing.
7. **Humanity has a royal vocation** — humans receive delegated authority over creation.
8. **Human dominion is delegated** — God remains the ultimate sovereign.
9. **Humanity is accountable to God** — delegated authority remains under the Creator.
10. **Creation is good** — the animals over which humanity rules have already been declared good.
11. **Stewardship is part of humanity's vocation** — dominion implies responsible care.
12. **The earth belongs ultimately to God** — human authority does not make humanity the absolute owner.
13. **Humanity represents God's rule** — image and dominion are closely connected.
14. **Human life has unique dignity** — the image provides the foundation for later biblical ethics.
15. **The image has ethical implications** — human beings must treat other image-bearers with dignity.
16. **The image remains significant after the Fall** — later Scripture continues to ground human dignity in it.
17. **Humanity's vocation is corporate** — “let them have dominion” concerns humanity collectively.
18. **Humanity's mission includes the whole created order** — sea, sky, land, and animals fall within its responsibility.
19. **Nature is neither divine nor worthless** — creation belongs to God and is declared good.
20. **Humanity's dignity requires humility** — humans are exalted as God's image-bearers while remaining creatures dependent upon God.
21. **Human authority has moral limits** — humans are rulers under God's authority, not autonomous sovereigns.
22. **Humanity's creation is purposeful** — humans are created with a defined vocation.
23. **The creation account is God-centered** — humanity's identity and authority come entirely from God.
24. **Humanity's royal vocation anticipates biblical kingdom themes** — faithful rule under God becomes a major biblical theme.
25. **The image of God is central to biblical anthropology** — it establishes the foundation for understanding what humanity is.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:26 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD CREATED HUMANITY UNIQUELY

**“Let us make man in our image, after our likeness.”**

Human beings possess a unique status within creation because God made them in His image.

---

## II. GOD GAVE HUMANITY A UNIQUE VOCATION

**“Let them have dominion…”**

Humanity is appointed to represent God's authority within the created world.

---

## III. HUMAN DOMINION IS DELEGATED DOMINION

**“Over the fish… birds… livestock… all the earth… every creeping thing.”**

Human authority extends broadly, but it remains under God's ultimate sovereignty.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:26 teaches that God uniquely created humanity in His image and likeness and appointed humanity to exercise delegated dominion over the earth and its living creatures.**

The progression from Genesis 1:24 through Genesis 1:26 is:

**Genesis 1:24**

God commands:

**“Let the earth bring forth living creatures.”**

**Genesis 1:25**

God makes:

**beasts**

**livestock**

**creeping things**

God declares:

**“it was good.”**

**Genesis 1:26**

God now says:

**“Let us make man…”**

Humanity is made:

**“in our image”**

**“after our likeness.”**

Humanity receives:

**dominion over the fish**

**dominion over the birds**

**dominion over livestock**

**dominion over all the earth**

**dominion over every creeping thing.**

The pattern is:

**God creates the world.**

**God fills the world with life.**

**God declares creation good.**

**God creates humanity uniquely.**

**God gives humanity His image.**

**God entrusts humanity with dominion.**
