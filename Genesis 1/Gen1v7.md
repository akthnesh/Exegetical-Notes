# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:7

**Genesis 1:7 —** “And God made the expanse and separated the waters that were under the expanse from the waters that were above the expanse.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיַּעַשׂ אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָרָקִיעַ וַיַּבְדֵּל בֵּין הַמַּיִם אֲשֶׁר מִתַּחַת לָרָקִיעַ וּבֵין הַמַּיִם אֲשֶׁר מֵעַל לָרָקִיעַ**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyaʿaś ʾĕlōhîm ʾet-hārāqîaʿ wayyavdēl bên ham-mayim ʾăšer mittaḥat lārāqîaʿ ûvên ham-mayim ʾăšer mēʿal lārāqîaʿ*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And God made the expanse and separated between the waters that were beneath the expanse and between the waters that were above the expanse.”**

---

# 1. “And God made” — וַיַּעַשׂ אֱלֹהִים (*wayyaʿaś ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:7 begins:

**וַיַּעַשׂ אֱלֹהִים**

*Wayyaʿaś ʾĕlōhîm*

**“And God made…”**

The verb **וַיַּעַשׂ (*wayyaʿaś*)** comes from the Hebrew root **עשה (*ʿ-s-h*)**, meaning **“to do,” “to make,” or “to accomplish.”**

This wording is significant because verse 6 recorded God's command:

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

Verse 7 now records the fulfillment:

**“And God made the expanse…”**

The narrative therefore continues its characteristic pattern:

**God speaks → God acts → God's purpose is accomplished.**

---

# 2. God acts upon His command

Genesis 1:6 presented the divine command.

Genesis 1:7 presents the divine accomplishment.

God does not merely issue an instruction and leave creation to determine the outcome.

He Himself establishes the expanse.

The verse therefore emphasizes God's direct sovereignty over the creative process.

The One who commands is the One who accomplishes.

---

# 3. “The expanse” — אֶת־הָרָקִיעַ (*ʾet-hārāqîaʿ*)

The object of God's action is:

**הָרָקִיעַ (*hārāqîaʿ*)**

**“the expanse.”**

This is the same **רָקִיעַ (*rāqîaʿ*)** introduced in verse 6.

The definite article **הַ (*ha-*)** identifies it as the particular expanse God commanded to be established.

Genesis 1:7 therefore confirms that the expanse of verse 6 has actually been established.

Later, in Genesis 1:8, God gives the expanse its name:

**“Heaven.”**

Thus the sequence is:

**Verse 6 — God commands the expanse.**

**Verse 7 — God makes the expanse.**

**Verse 8 — God names the expanse Heaven.**

---

# 4. “And separated” — וַיַּבְדֵּל (*wayyavdēl*)

The second major action is:

**וַיַּבְדֵּל**

*Wayyavdēl*

**“and separated.”**

This comes from the Hebrew root:

**בדל (*b-d-l*)**

meaning:

**“to separate,” “to divide,” or “to distinguish.”**

The same root appeared in Genesis 1:4:

**“And God separated the light from the darkness.”**

It appears again here:

**“And God separated…”**

This repetition establishes separation as one of the central organizing actions in Genesis 1.

---

# 5. Separation as a major creation theme

Genesis 1 repeatedly shows God creating order through separation.

Genesis 1:4:

**Light ↔ Darkness**

Genesis 1:7:

**Waters below ↔ Waters above**

Genesis 1:9:

**Waters ↔ Dry Land**

These distinctions are not accidental.

God establishes them.

The created world becomes ordered because God gives it boundaries and divisions.

Thus Genesis 1 presents creation not merely as the production of matter but as the establishment of an ordered environment.

---

# 6. “Between the waters” — בֵּין הַמַּיִם (*bên ham-mayim*)

The Hebrew uses:

**בֵּין (*bên*)**

meaning:

**“between.”**

The phrase is:

**בֵּין הַמַּיִם**

**“between the waters.”**

The verse then specifies which waters are being distinguished:

**waters beneath**

and

**waters above.**

The expanse therefore functions as the separating boundary between two domains of waters.

---

# 7. “The waters that were under the expanse”

The first group is described as:

**הַמַּיִם אֲשֶׁר מִתַּחַת לָרָקִיעַ**

*ham-mayim ʾăšer mittaḥat lārāqîaʿ*

**“the waters that were under the expanse.”**

The phrase:

**מִתַּחַת (*mittaḥat*)**

means:

**“under”** or **“beneath.”**

The waters beneath the expanse will later become particularly significant when God gathers the waters together and dry land appears in Genesis 1:9.

Thus the lower waters belong to the terrestrial realm being prepared for habitation.

---

# 8. “The waters that were above the expanse”

The second group is:

**וּבֵין הַמַּיִם אֲשֶׁר מֵעַל לָרָקִיעַ**

*ûvên ham-mayim ʾăšer mēʿal lārāqîaʿ*

**“and between the waters that were above the expanse.”**

The phrase:

**מֵעַל (*mēʿal*)**

means:

**“above”** or **“over.”**

The verse therefore establishes a vertical distinction:

**waters below**

**expanse**

**waters above**

This is the structure presented by the Genesis creation narrative.

---

# 9. The repeated “waters”

Notice how deliberately the verse repeats the word:

**מַיִם (*mayim*) — “waters.”**

The Hebrew essentially says:

**between the waters**

**that are beneath the expanse**

**and between the waters**

**that are above the expanse.**

The repetition emphasizes the separation.

Two groups of waters are distinguished by the expanse God establishes between them.

---

# 10. Genesis 1:7 as the fulfillment of Genesis 1:6

Verse 7 should be read together with verse 6.

### Genesis 1:6

God says:

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

and

**“let it separate the waters from the waters.”**

### Genesis 1:7

God makes the expanse.

God separates the waters.

The relationship is straightforward:

**Command → accomplishment.**

The Word of God produces the reality He commands.

---

# 11. God's Word and God's work

Genesis 1 repeatedly combines God's speech with God's action.

God says.

God makes.

God separates.

God names.

This prevents us from viewing divine speech as mere information.

God's Word is effective.

His command produces the intended result.

Genesis therefore presents God's speech as inseparable from His sovereign activity.

---

# 12. The expanse as an ordered boundary

The expanse is given a specific function:

**separation.**

It distinguishes one domain of waters from another.

This illustrates an important principle of Genesis 1:

**God creates order by establishing boundaries.**

Boundaries are not presented as limitations upon God's power.

They are expressions of God's wisdom.

God determines the proper arrangement of His creation.

---

# 13. Genesis 1:7 and the ancient worldview

The language of Genesis 1:7 describes:

- waters below
- an expanse
- waters above

This reflects the cosmological language and conceptual world of the ancient Near East.

The purpose of the text is not to provide a modern scientific explanation of the atmosphere, hydrological cycle, or physical structure of the universe.

Its primary theological purpose is to proclaim that the structure of the created world is established by God.

The text communicates God's sovereignty through the cosmological framework of its original audience.

---

# 14. Theological significance of the waters

The waters introduced in Genesis 1:2 might initially appear to represent an unstructured environment.

By Genesis 1:7, however, God has brought the waters into an ordered relationship.

The waters are assigned distinct positions.

They are not autonomous.

They are not divine.

They are not beyond God's control.

God establishes their boundaries.

This reinforces the central theological message of Genesis 1:

**God is sovereign over the entire created order.**

---

# 15. God does not battle the waters

Genesis 1:7 contains no account of divine combat.

God does not fight the waters.

He does not need to defeat a water deity.

He does not struggle to impose His will.

He simply makes the expanse and separates the waters.

This is another expression of the absolute sovereignty of the biblical Creator.

God's authority over creation is effortless.

---

# 16. Genesis 1:7 and ancient pagan religions

In many ancient cultures, cosmic waters could be associated with gods, monsters, or forces of chaos.

Genesis strips the waters of divinity.

The waters are simply part of creation.

They are controlled by God's command.

The theological message is therefore profound:

**The forces that ancient peoples might fear as divine powers are themselves subject to the Creator.**

Genesis presents one God above the entire created order.

---

# 17. The Creator–creature distinction

Genesis 1:7 reinforces the distinction between God and creation.

God makes.

The expanse is made.

The waters are created realities.

God is therefore not identified with the cosmos.

The cosmos is His workmanship.

This distinction is foundational to biblical theology.

God is Creator.

Everything else belongs to creation.

---

# 18. God's transcendence

God's authority over the expanse and waters demonstrates His transcendence.

He is not merely another component within the universe.

He establishes the universe's structure.

The created order depends upon Him.

He does not depend upon it.

Genesis therefore presents God as fundamentally distinct from everything He has made.

---

# 19. God's immanence

At the same time, Genesis presents God as actively involved in the ordering of creation.

He makes.

He separates.

He establishes.

He names.

Thus the God of Genesis is not a distant deity who creates and then withdraws.

He actively orders His creation according to His purpose.

God is transcendent above creation while also being actively involved in it.

---

# 20. Genesis 1:7 and divine wisdom

The establishment of the expanse is part of God's preparation of the world for life.

The creation narrative proceeds from the ordering of the heavens and waters toward the preparation of the earth.

The structure being established will become the environment in which plants, animals, and humanity will exist.

Thus God's work demonstrates not merely power but wisdom.

He creates an ordered environment suitable for His purposes.

---

# 21. Genesis 1:7 and divine providence

The boundaries established by God continue to shape the biblical understanding of creation.

The heavens, waters, seas, and land remain under God's authority.

The world operates according to an order that ultimately depends upon Him.

This contributes to the doctrine of providence:

**God not only creates the world; He remains sovereign over the order He has established.**

---

# 22. Genesis 1:7 and the preparation of the earth

The creation narrative is moving toward a world suitable for habitation.

The sequence can be summarized:

**Genesis 1:2**

Waters cover the scene.

**Genesis 1:3–5**

Light and darkness are ordered.

**Genesis 1:6–7**

The waters are divided.

**Genesis 1:8**

The expanse is called Heaven.

**Genesis 1:9**

The waters below are gathered so that dry land appears.

The narrative therefore progressively transforms the initial watery environment into an ordered world.

---

# 23. Genesis 1:7 and the second day

Genesis 1:7 describes the central work of the second creation day.

The first day established:

**light**

**Day**

**Night**

The second day establishes:

**the expanse**

**the distinction between waters above and waters below.**

The creation account is therefore progressively developing the structure of the world.

---

# 24. The importance of divine separation

The repeated use of the root **בדל (*b-d-l*)** deserves special attention.

Genesis 1:4:

**God separates light from darkness.**

Genesis 1:7:

**God separates waters from waters.**

Later biblical passages will use separation language in theological contexts involving holiness, worship, and distinction.

The creation narrative therefore provides an early biblical pattern:

**God establishes meaningful distinctions.**

Creation is ordered through distinctions established by God.

---

# 25. Genesis 1:7 and holiness

The separation language of Genesis 1 should not be equated directly with later ceremonial concepts of holiness.

Nevertheless, there is a conceptual connection worth recognizing.

In Scripture, holiness frequently involves **distinction** or **separation for a particular purpose**.

Genesis 1 establishes the foundational picture of God creating an ordered world through distinctions.

Later Scripture develops this theme in the contexts of people, places, times, and objects being distinguished for God's purposes.

The creation account therefore provides an important background for later biblical concepts of separation and consecration.

---

# 26. Genesis 1:7 and God's authority over boundaries

God determines:

**what belongs above**

and

**what belongs below.**

The created order does not establish these boundaries independently.

This emphasizes divine authority.

The universe is not self-defining.

Its structure ultimately comes from God.

Therefore the created order should be understood as dependent upon divine wisdom and sovereignty.

---

# 27. Genesis 1:7 and the later biblical picture of heaven

Verse 7 establishes the expanse.

Verse 8 will say:

**“And God called the expanse Heaven.”**

This means the biblical concept of **heaven** begins here within the creation narrative as part of God's ordered creation.

Later Scripture develops the term in several contexts, including the heavens as the sky and the heavenly realm associated with God's presence.

These later meanings should be distinguished according to context.

Genesis 1:7 specifically concerns the created expanse established between the waters.

---

# 28. Genesis 1:7 and Christological creation

Genesis 1:7 does not explicitly mention Christ.

However, the New Testament provides a fuller revelation of Christ's role in creation.

John 1:3 teaches:

**“All things were made through him.”**

Colossians 1:16 similarly teaches that all things were created through Christ and for Christ.

Therefore, within Christian canonical theology, the creative work described in Genesis belongs to the work of the triune God.

Genesis reveals the Creator.

The New Testament reveals the Son's participation in creation.

---

# 29. Genesis 1:7 and the Trinity

Genesis 1:2 has already introduced the Spirit of God.

Genesis 1:6–7 continues describing the sovereign creative activity of God.

The New Testament later identifies the eternal Word, the Son, as the One through whom all things were made.

Thus the broader canonical picture is:

**The Father creates.**

**The Son creates.**

**The Spirit is active in creation.**

Genesis 1:7 should therefore be read within the complete biblical revelation while preserving its immediate emphasis on God's sovereign action.

---

# 30. Genesis 1:7 and the authority of Scripture

Because Genesis presents God's creative Word as effective, it establishes an important theological foundation for understanding divine revelation.

God's speech is authoritative.

What God says corresponds to what He accomplishes.

The creation narrative therefore gives the reader a picture of divine speech that is reliable and effective.

God's Word is not empty.

It accomplishes His purposes.

---

# 31. Literary structure

Genesis 1:7 contributes to the repeated literary rhythm of the chapter:

**God said.**

**God made.**

**God separated.**

This pattern emphasizes the relationship between divine command and divine accomplishment.

The chapter repeatedly directs attention away from creation as an autonomous process and toward God as its active sovereign agent.

---

# 32. The movement from formlessness to order

Genesis 1:7 is part of the larger movement established in Genesis 1.

### Genesis 1:2

**Formless**

**Empty**

**Dark**

**Watery**

### Genesis 1:3–5

**Light**

**Day**

**Night**

### Genesis 1:6–7

**Expanse**

**Waters above**

**Waters below**

The world is progressively receiving structure.

God is preparing the creation for habitation.

---

# 33. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:7 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God accomplishes His command by establishing the expanse and separating the waters above from the waters below, demonstrating His sovereign authority to structure and order the entire created world.**

Or more simply:

**God establishes the boundaries of creation by His sovereign Word.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:7

### **“And God made the expanse”**

→ God accomplishes what He commanded.

→ The expanse exists by divine action.

### **“And separated”**

→ God establishes order through distinction.

→ Separation is a recurring theme in Genesis 1.

### **“The waters that were under the expanse”**

→ The lower waters are assigned their place.

→ The waters remain under divine authority.

### **“From the waters that were above the expanse”**

→ The upper waters are distinguished from the lower waters.

→ God establishes a structured cosmic order.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:7 establishes or contributes to at least these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God actively establishes the structure of creation.
2. **Effectiveness of God's Word** — what God commands is accomplished.
3. **Divine order** — God transforms an initially unstructured environment into an ordered world.
4. **Divine separation** — God establishes distinctions and boundaries.
5. **Creator–creature distinction** — the expanse and waters are created realities, not divine beings.
6. **God's sovereignty over the waters** — the waters are completely subject to God.
7. **God's transcendence** — God is sovereign over the created order.
8. **God's immanence** — God actively works within creation.
9. **Divine wisdom** — creation is structured according to purposeful design.
10. **Anti-dualism** — the waters are not rival divine powers.
11. **Ancient cosmological framework** — the text describes the ordered world using the conceptual language of its historical setting.
12. **Providence** — the ordered structure of creation remains under God's sovereign governance.
13. **Preparation for habitation** — the ordering of the waters prepares the created environment for subsequent life.
14. **Trinitarian creation** — the broader biblical canon identifies Father, Son, and Spirit with God's creative work.
15. **Christological creation** — the New Testament identifies Christ as the divine agent through whom all things were made.

---

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:7 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD ACCOMPLISHES WHAT HE COMMANDS

**“And God made the expanse…”**

God's Word does not fail.

His command is followed by His accomplishment.

---

## II. GOD ESTABLISHES ORDER

**“And separated…”**

God continues transforming the creation into an ordered world.

He establishes distinctions where there was previously no defined separation.

---

## III. GOD SETS THE BOUNDARIES OF CREATION

**“The waters that were under the expanse from the waters that were above the expanse.”**

The waters do not determine their own boundaries.

God does.

The structure of creation rests upon the authority and wisdom of its Creator.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:7 teaches that God faithfully accomplishes His creative command by establishing the expanse and separating the waters above from the waters below, demonstrating that the structure, boundaries, and order of creation are established by His sovereign wisdom and authority.**

The movement from Genesis 1:2 through Genesis 1:7 is therefore remarkable:

**Genesis 1:2**

Darkness.

Waters.

An unformed and empty world.

**Genesis 1:3**

God speaks:

**“Let there be light.”**

**Genesis 1:4**

God separates:

**light from darkness.**

**Genesis 1:5**

God names:

**Day and Night.**

**Genesis 1:6**

God speaks again:

**“Let there be an expanse…”**

**Genesis 1:7**

God accomplishes His command:

**the expanse is established**

and

**the waters are separated.**

The pattern remains consistent:

**God speaks.**

**God acts.**

**God separates.**

**God orders.**

**Creation obeys.**
