# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:15

**Genesis 1:15 —** “and let them be lights in the expanse of the heavens to give light upon the earth.” And it was so.

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וְהָיוּ לִמְאוֹרֹת בִּרְקִיעַ הַשָּׁמַיִם לְהָאִיר עַל־הָאָרֶץ וַיְהִי־כֵן**

---

## Transliteration

*Wəhāyû limʾōrōt birqîaʿ haššāmayim ləhāʾîr ʿal-hāʾāreṣ wayəhî-kēn*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And let them be for lights in the expanse of the heavens, to give light upon the earth. And it was so.”**

---

# 1. “And let them be for lights” — וְהָיוּ לִמְאוֹרֹת (*wəhāyû limʾōrōt*)

Genesis 1:15 continues the divine command of verse 14.

The Hebrew phrase is:

**וְהָיוּ לִמְאוֹרֹת**

*Wəhāyû limʾōrōt*

**“And let them be for lights.”**

The heavenly bodies are given a specific function.

They are to serve as:

**lights.**

The emphasis is therefore not merely upon their existence but upon their purpose.

---

# 2. The function of the heavenly lights

Verse 14 already identified several functions:

**to separate day from night**

**to serve as signs**

**to mark appointed times**

**to mark days and years.**

Verse 15 adds another explicit function:

**to give light upon the earth.**

The heavenly lights therefore have a direct relationship to the terrestrial world.

---

# 3. “In the expanse of the heavens” — בִּרְקִיעַ הַשָּׁמַיִם (*birqîaʿ haššāmayim*)

The lights are located:

**“in the expanse of the heavens.”**

The word **רָקִיעַ (*rāqîaʿ*)** refers to the expanse established in Genesis 1:6–8.

The heavenly lights therefore occupy the realm that God previously established.

---

# 4. The heavens are part of God's creation

The phrase:

**“the expanse of the heavens”**

reminds the reader that the heavenly realm is itself created.

The lights are not independent divine beings.

They are placed within God's created order.

God remains sovereign over them.

---

# 5. “To give light” — לְהָאִיר (*ləhāʾîr*)

The Hebrew infinitive:

**לְהָאִיר**

*ləhāʾîr*

means:

**“to give light”**

or

**“to illuminate.”**

It comes from the root **אור (*ʾ-w-r*)**, associated with light.

The heavenly lights therefore have an appointed task:

**illumination.**

---

# 6. Light upon the earth

The verse concludes the functional statement with:

**עַל־הָאָרֶץ**

*ʿal-hāʾāreṣ*

**“upon the earth.”**

The purpose of the heavenly lights is directed toward the earth.

The created heavens serve the created earth.

---

# 7. The relationship between heaven and earth

Genesis 1 repeatedly distinguishes:

**heavens**

and

**earth.**

The lights belong to the heavenly realm but serve the earthly realm by giving light upon it.

This demonstrates the interconnectedness of God's creation.

---

# 8. The lights serve creation

The heavenly lights are not portrayed as autonomous rulers.

They serve a purpose assigned by God.

They provide:

**light**

**temporal markers**

**distinctions between day and night.**

Creation is therefore ordered toward the flourishing of life.

---

# 9. The purpose-driven character of creation

Verse 15 demonstrates that creation has purpose.

God does not merely say:

**“Let there be lights.”**

He specifies what they are for:

**“to give light upon the earth.”**

The created world therefore has functional order.

---

# 10. The connection with Genesis 1:3

Genesis 1:3 says:

**“Let there be light.”**

Genesis 1:15 speaks of the heavenly lights giving light upon the earth.

The account therefore distinguishes between:

**light**

and

**the lights that illuminate the earth.**

---

# 11. Light existed before the luminaries

Within the narrative sequence, light is introduced in Day One.

The luminaries are introduced in Day Four.

This means the narrative does not present the existence of light as dependent upon the heavenly bodies themselves.

God is the ultimate source and sovereign Creator of light.

---

# 12. The lights are instruments

The heavenly bodies function as instruments within God's created order.

They carry out the purpose assigned to them.

They are not the ultimate source of divine power.

God created them and gave them their function.

---

# 13. “And it was so” — וַיְהִי־כֵן (*wayəhî-kēn*)

The verse ends:

**וַיְהִי־כֵן**

*Wayəhî-kēn*

**“And it was so.”**

This is one of the recurring formulas of Genesis 1.

God commands.

The command is fulfilled.

The narrative confirms:

**“And it was so.”**

---

# 14. The effectiveness of God's Word

The phrase:

**“And it was so”**

demonstrates the effectiveness of God's command.

God speaks, and creation responds.

There is no struggle.

There is no opposition.

There is no delay described.

The divine command is accomplished.

---

# 15. Divine sovereignty

Genesis 1:15 reinforces God's sovereignty.

He determines:

**what the lights are**

**where they belong**

**what they do**

**how they serve the earth.**

The heavenly lights have their purpose because God gives them purpose.

---

# 16. Creation obeys the Creator

The statement:

**“And it was so”**

shows that creation conforms to God's command.

The heavenly realm operates according to the will of its Creator.

---

# 17. The distinction between Creator and creation

The verse provides another clear distinction:

**God → commands**

**heavenly lights → serve**

The lights are creatures.

God alone is Creator.

---

# 18. The lights do not possess divine authority

Because the lights exist by God's command and serve God's purposes, they cannot be treated as ultimate powers.

The sun, moon, and stars are not presented as gods.

They are instruments within God's created order.

---

# 19. A polemic against celestial worship

The ancient world often attached religious significance to celestial bodies.

Genesis presents a radically different perspective.

The heavenly lights are simply created objects under God's command.

They give light because God appointed them to do so.

---

# 20. The earth receives light

The phrase:

**“upon the earth”**

shows that the created earth is a beneficiary of God's ordering of the heavens.

The heavenly lights provide illumination for terrestrial creation.

---

# 21. Light and life

Light plays an important role in the creation narrative.

Day One establishes light.

Day Three establishes vegetation.

Day Four establishes the heavenly lights.

The narrative progressively prepares the world for life.

---

# 22. The relationship between Day Three and Day Four

Day Three introduced:

**vegetation**

Day Four introduces:

**luminaries**

The lights provide illumination within the world in which vegetation and later living creatures exist.

The creation account therefore presents an increasingly ordered environment.

---

# 23. The forming and filling pattern

Genesis 1 can be viewed through the pattern:

**forming → filling**

Day One forms the realm of light and darkness.

Day Four introduces the luminaries associated with that realm.

Genesis 1:15 belongs to this correspondence.

---

# 24. Day One and Day Four

The correspondence can be summarized:

### Day One

**Light and darkness**

### Day Four

**Lights in the heavens**

The fourth day develops and populates the realm introduced on the first day.

---

# 25. The heavenly lights and day

The lights serve the distinction between:

**day**

and

**night.**

They therefore contribute to the regular rhythm of earthly existence.

---

# 26. The heavenly lights and night

The lights also establish the conditions under which night is distinguished from day.

Their function is therefore related to both sides of the day-night cycle.

---

# 27. God's ordering of time

Verse 15 continues the theme of ordered time introduced in verse 14.

The lights:

**give light**

while also serving as markers of:

**days**

and

**years.**

God therefore establishes both illumination and temporal order.

---

# 28. The created world is intelligible

Because the lights follow an ordered function, humans can observe recurring patterns.

The world is not presented as fundamentally chaotic.

It possesses recognizable structure.

---

# 29. The lights and human life

Human beings will eventually live within this ordered environment.

The regularity of light and darkness will govern:

**work**

**rest**

**agriculture**

**travel**

**worship**

and many other aspects of life.

The heavenly lights therefore serve the created order broadly.

---

# 30. The lights and agriculture

Agricultural life depends upon predictable cycles.

The heavenly lights mark temporal patterns associated with:

**days**

**seasons**

**years.**

The illumination they provide also belongs to the environment in which plant life flourishes.

---

# 31. The lights and the seasons

Verse 14 connected the lights with:

**“seasons.”**

Verse 15 now emphasizes their illumination.

Together the verses present the heavenly bodies as both:

**time markers**

and

**sources of illumination.**

---

# 32. Creation is interconnected

Genesis does not portray the components of creation as isolated.

The heavens serve the earth.

The earth produces vegetation.

Vegetation will provide food.

Later, animals and humanity will inhabit the earth.

The created order is therefore interconnected.

---

# 33. Divine provision

God prepares an environment suitable for life.

The lights contribute to that preparation.

They provide illumination and temporal order.

This demonstrates God's purposeful provision within creation.

---

# 34. The goodness of God's ordering

Verse 15 itself does not include the phrase:

**“God saw that it was good.”**

That evaluation comes in verse 18.

Nevertheless, verse 15 shows the successful establishment of the heavenly lights according to God's command.

---

# 35. “And it was so” as confirmation

The phrase:

**“And it was so”**

functions as a narrative confirmation.

What God commanded has become reality.

The divine Word has accomplished its purpose.

---

# 36. The pattern of Genesis 1

A repeated pattern can be observed:

**God speaks**

→ **God commands**

→ **creation responds**

→ **the result is described**

→ **God evaluates**

→ **the day concludes.**

Genesis 1:15 occupies the fulfillment portion of this pattern.

---

# 37. God's Word creates order

God's speech does not merely produce objects.

It establishes:

**function**

**relationship**

**purpose**

and

**order.**

The heavenly lights are created to perform specific functions within the world.

---

# 38. The purpose of created things

Genesis 1:15 contributes to a broader biblical principle:

Created things have purposes assigned by their Creator.

The heavenly lights have a purpose.

The earth has a purpose.

Vegetation has a purpose.

Animals have a purpose.

Humanity will have a purpose.

Creation is therefore teleological—ordered toward purposes.

---

# 39. The lights serve the earth, not humanity alone

The text says:

**“to give light upon the earth.”**

The immediate recipient is the earth as a whole.

The function is therefore broader than merely serving human needs.

The entire terrestrial creation exists within God's ordered environment.

---

# 40. The earth as a prepared dwelling place

The earth is progressively prepared for life.

By Day Four:

**land exists**

**vegetation exists**

**light is established**

**temporal rhythms are marked.**

The world is becoming increasingly suitable for its future inhabitants.

---

# 41. The heavenly realm serves God's creative purposes

The heavenly lights do not exist independently from the terrestrial story.

They serve the purposes of the Creator for the created world.

Heaven and earth are therefore related within God's design.

---

# 42. The lights and divine providence

The continuing patterns of illumination and time become part of God's providential ordering of creation.

Day follows night.

Seasons recur.

Years pass.

These rhythms provide stability for life.

---

# 43. Genesis 1:15 and divine faithfulness

The recurring order established by God provides a stable environment.

Later Scripture frequently appeals to the regularity of creation as evidence of God's faithfulness.

Genesis 1 provides the foundation for that understanding.

---

# 44. The heavenly lights and God's glory

The lights themselves are not divine.

Their existence and order point toward the wisdom and power of their Creator.

The proper response is therefore not celestial worship but worship of God.

---

# 45. Psalm 19 and the heavens

Psalm 19 later declares that:

**“The heavens declare the glory of God.”**

Genesis 1:15 provides the creation foundation for this testimony.

The heavens reveal God's handiwork.

---

# 46. Celestial bodies as created servants

The lights have no independent divine status.

They are created servants within God's ordered universe.

Their greatness should lead the observer beyond the creation to the Creator.

---

# 47. Genesis 1:15 and monotheism

The verse contributes to the biblical confession of one sovereign Creator.

The heavens contain many lights.

But there is only one Creator who commands them.

The multiplicity of celestial objects does not imply a multiplicity of gods.

---

# 48. Genesis 1:15 and idolatry

Because celestial bodies are creatures, worshiping them is a category error.

They belong to the created order.

God alone deserves worship.

---

# 49. The contrast between God and the lights

God:

**speaks**

**commands**

**assigns purpose**

**creates**

The lights:

**exist**

**give light**

**serve their appointed purpose.**

The distinction is absolute.

---

# 50. Genesis 1:15 and God's authority over nature

Nature is not independent of God.

The heavenly lights operate within an order established by divine command.

God therefore exercises authority over what modern language might call the natural order.

---

# 51. Genesis 1:15 and the reliability of creation

The phrase:

**“And it was so”**

demonstrates that God's command is dependable.

The created order comes into existence according to His Word.

---

# 52. Creation responds immediately

The narrative does not describe a prolonged process between command and fulfillment.

The statement:

**“And it was so”**

compresses the fulfillment of God's command.

The emphasis is on divine effectiveness.

---

# 53. Genesis 1:15 and divine simplicity of command

The command is straightforward:

**“Let them be lights…”**

The result is equally straightforward:

**“And it was so.”**

This simplicity highlights the extraordinary power of God.

---

# 54. God's Word versus human words

Human words require effort and external means to accomplish things.

God's creative Word possesses intrinsic authority.

When God commands creation, creation responds.

This becomes a foundational biblical theme.

---

# 55. Genesis 1:15 and the Word in John 1

John's Gospel later teaches that the Word was with God and was God and that all things were made through Him.

Genesis 1's repeated emphasis on God's speaking therefore provides important background for New Testament teaching about creation through the divine Word.

---

# 56. Genesis 1:15 and Christological creation

Genesis 1:15 does not explicitly mention Jesus Christ.

Nevertheless, the New Testament identifies Christ as the One through whom all things were created.

Therefore the heavenly lights described in this verse ultimately belong to the created order established through Christ.

---

# 57. Colossians 1 and creation

Colossians 1:16 teaches that all things were created through Christ and for Christ.

The heavenly lights therefore exist:

**through Him**

and

**for Him.**

---

# 58. Christ and the created heavens

The heavenly lights are magnificent examples of created order.

Yet they remain creatures.

Christ, according to the New Testament, stands on the Creator side of the Creator-creature distinction.

---

# 59. Genesis 1:15 and the new creation

The biblical storyline eventually moves from the original creation toward the new creation.

Revelation describes the final consummation of God's purposes.

The created lights therefore belong to the beginning of a story that ultimately culminates in God's glory filling the renewed creation.

---

# 60. God as the ultimate light

The Bible ultimately identifies God Himself as the ultimate source of light.

Created lights serve the world temporarily.

God's glory is everlasting.

This provides a theological trajectory from:

**created light**

toward

**the glory of God.**

---

# 61. The earthly benefit of heavenly creation

The lights are placed in the heavens but their function reaches the earth.

This illustrates a broader biblical pattern:

God uses the heavenly realm to serve His purposes for earthly creation.

---

# 62. The lights and the future human vocation

Humanity will later be commanded to exercise dominion over the earth.

But humanity does not establish the earth's fundamental order.

God establishes it first.

Humans therefore receive a world already ordered by their Creator.

---

# 63. Human dependence upon God's order

Human existence will depend upon the rhythms established by God:

**light**

**darkness**

**days**

**seasons**

**years.**

Human beings are therefore dependent creatures.

---

# 64. Genesis 1:15 and humility

The heavenly lights are enormous and powerful from a human perspective.

Yet Genesis places them under God's command.

If even the heavens serve the Creator, humanity should likewise live in humble dependence upon Him.

---

# 65. Creation and worship

The proper direction of worship is:

**creation → Creator**

not:

**creation → creation.**

The heavenly lights should cause awe toward God, not worship of the lights themselves.

---

# 66. The literary function of Genesis 1:15

Verse 15 explains the purpose introduced in verse 14.

Verse 14:

**God commands the lights.**

Verse 15:

**their function is specified and fulfilled.**

Verse 16:

**the lights are described more specifically.**

Verse 17:

**God places them in the heavens.**

Verse 18:

**their function and goodness are confirmed.**

---

# 67. Genesis 1:14–18 as a unified section

The fourth day can therefore be understood as a coherent sequence:

**Command**

→ **Purpose**

→ **Creation**

→ **Placement**

→ **Function**

→ **Evaluation.**

Genesis 1:15 belongs to the command-and-purpose portion of this sequence.

---

# 68. The phrase “and it was so”

This phrase provides certainty.

The text does not say:

**“perhaps it happened.”**

It says:

**“And it was so.”**

God's creative command is presented as accomplished fact.

---

# 69. Divine purpose and fulfillment

There is correspondence between God's command and the resulting reality.

God intends:

**lights**

The result:

**lights.**

God intends:

**illumination**

The result:

**light upon the earth.**

The divine purpose is fulfilled.

---

# 70. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:15 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God appoints the heavenly lights to illuminate the earth, and His creative command is perfectly accomplished.**

Or more simply:

**God creates the heavenly lights to give light upon the earth, and it is so.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:15

### **“And let them be for lights”**

→ The heavenly bodies are assigned the function of illumination.

### **“In the expanse of the heavens”**

→ They are placed within God's created heavenly realm.

### **“To give light upon the earth”**

→ Their illumination serves the terrestrial creation.

### **“And it was so”**

→ God's command is accomplished exactly as He intended.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:15 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God commands the heavenly lights.
2. **Divine creation** — the heavenly lights belong to the created order.
3. **Creator-creature distinction** — celestial bodies are not divine.
4. **Divine purpose** — created things have functions assigned by God.
5. **Divine providence** — the heavens serve the needs of earthly creation.
6. **Created order** — heaven and earth function together within God's design.
7. **Illumination** — the heavenly lights give light upon the earth.
8. **Temporal order** — the lights participate in the rhythms of days, seasons, and years.
9. **Creation's intelligibility** — the heavenly order is observable and recognizable.
10. **Rejection of celestial worship** — heavenly bodies are servants, not gods.
11. **Anti-idolatry** — creation must not receive worship belonging to the Creator.
12. **Divine faithfulness** — recurring celestial patterns provide stability for creation.
13. **Divine wisdom** — the heavenly system demonstrates purposeful design.
14. **Human dependence** — human life exists within God's established rhythms.
15. **Agricultural provision** — light and temporal order contribute to terrestrial productivity.
16. **Creation and providence** — God establishes an environment suitable for life.
17. **Effectiveness of God's Word** — God's command produces the intended result.
18. **Divine authority over nature** — the natural order operates under God's command.
19. **Christological creation** — the New Testament teaches that all things were created through Christ.
20. **Christological lordship** — the heavenly lights ultimately exist through Christ and for Christ.
21. **God's glory** — the heavens reveal the wisdom and majesty of their Creator.
22. **New creation** — created light ultimately points toward the greater glory of God in the consummation.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:15 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD GIVES CREATION ITS PURPOSE

**“Let them be for lights…”**

The heavenly lights exist because God gives them a specific function.

---

## II. GOD ORDERS THE HEAVENS TO SERVE THE EARTH

**“To give light upon the earth.”**

The heavenly realm is incorporated into God's provision for terrestrial creation.

---

## III. GOD'S WORD ALWAYS ACCOMPLISHES HIS PURPOSE

**“And it was so.”**

God speaks, and His command is fulfilled.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:15 teaches that God sovereignly appoints the heavenly lights to illuminate the earth, placing them within the expanse of the heavens for the benefit of the created world, and that His creative Word accomplishes exactly what He commands.**

The progression from Genesis 1:14 through Genesis 1:15 is:

**Genesis 1:14**

God commands:

**“Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens.”**

Their purposes are established:

**to separate day from night**

**for signs**

**for appointed times**

**for days**

**for years**

**Genesis 1:15**

Their purpose is further specified:

**to give light upon the earth.**

Then:

**“And it was so.”**

The pattern continues:

**God speaks.**

**God assigns purpose.**

**Creation responds.**

**God's Word is fulfilled.**
