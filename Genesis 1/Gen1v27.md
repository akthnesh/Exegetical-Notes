# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:27

**Genesis 1:27 —** “So God created man in his own image, in the image of God he created him; male and female he created them.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיִּבְרָא אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הָאָדָם בְּצַלְמוֹ בְּצֶלֶם אֱלֹהִים בָּרָא אֹתוֹ זָכָר וּנְקֵבָה בָּרָא אֹתָם׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyivrā ʾĕlōhîm ʾeṯ-hāʾādām bəṣalmô bəṣelem ʾĕlōhîm bārā ʾōṯô zākār ûnəqēḇāh bārā ʾōṯām*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And God created the man/humanity in His image; in the image of God He created him; male and female He created them.”**

---

# 1. “So God Created” — וַיִּבְרָא אֱלֹהִים (*wayyivrā ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:27 begins with a significant change in wording from verse 26.

Verse 26 says:

**“Let us make…”**

Verse 27 says:

**“So God created…”**

The Hebrew verb is:

**בָּרָא (*bārāʾ*)**

meaning:

**“to create.”**

---

# 2. The Verb *Bārāʾ*

The verb **בָּרָא** is particularly associated in the Old Testament with God's creative activity.

In Genesis 1, it has already appeared in:

**Genesis 1:1**

**“God created the heavens and the earth.”**

It also appeared in:

**Genesis 1:21**

concerning God's creation of the great sea creatures.

Now it appears in connection with humanity.

---

# 3. Humanity's Creation Is Deliberately Emphasized

The use of **bārāʾ** highlights humanity as the result of God's direct creative activity.

The text does not present humanity as an independent product of the created order.

God creates humanity.

---

# 4. “God Created Man” — אֶת־הָאָדָם (*ʾeṯ-hāʾādām*)

The Hebrew phrase contains:

**הָאָדָם (*hāʾādām*)**

meaning:

**“the human”**

or

**“the man/humanity.”**

The term is related to **אֲדָמָה (*ʾădāmâ*)**, meaning ground or earth.

---

# 5. Humanity as *Adam*

The word **ʾādām** can function as both:

**a personal name — Adam**

and

**a designation for humanity — human/humankind.**

In Genesis 1:27, the immediate context emphasizes humanity as a whole.

---

# 6. “In His Own Image” — בְּצַלְמוֹ (*bəṣalmô*)

The phrase:

**בְּצַלְמוֹ**

means:

**“in his image.”**

The noun:

**צֶלֶם (*ṣelem*)**

means:

**“image”**

or

**“representation.”**

The suffix **־וֹ** means:

**“his.”**

---

# 7. The Image Belongs to God

The verse does not say that humanity created God's image.

It says:

**“in his own image.”**

God is the source of the image.

---

# 8. “In the Image of God” — בְּצֶלֶם אֱלֹהִים (*bəṣelem ʾĕlōhîm*)

The statement is repeated:

**“in the image of God he created him.”**

The repetition strongly emphasizes the unique identity of humanity.

---

# 9. Repetition for Emphasis

The verse says:

**“in his own image”**

and then:

**“in the image of God.”**

This repetition makes the image-bearing status unmistakable.

---

# 10. Humanity's Unique Status

Among the creatures described in Genesis 1, humanity alone is explicitly identified as being created in God's image.

This gives humanity a unique place within creation.

---

# 11. The Image Is Creational

The image is not something humanity earns.

God creates humanity in His image.

Therefore image-bearing is foundational to human existence.

---

# 12. The Image Is Universal to Humanity

Genesis 1:27 does not limit the image of God to:

**kings**

**priests**

**leaders**

**the wealthy**

**the intelligent**

or

**the powerful.**

It is attributed to humanity.

---

# 13. Human Dignity

The image of God provides a theological foundation for human dignity.

Every human being possesses a dignity rooted in God's creative act.

---

# 14. Humanity Is Not Merely an Animal

Humans are creatures, but Genesis gives humanity a unique status among earthly creatures.

Humanity bears God's image.

---

# 15. Image and Representation

The concept of an image can communicate representation.

Humanity is uniquely appointed to represent God's rule within creation.

---

# 16. Image and Dominion

Genesis 1:26 connected the image of God with dominion.

Verse 27 now confirms that humanity has actually been created in that image.

Verse 28 will then give humanity the blessing and dominion mandate.

---

# 17. The Image and Human Vocation

The image of God is therefore closely connected to humanity's calling.

Human beings are created to live under God and exercise responsible authority within creation.

---

# 18. “He Created Him” — בָּרָא אֹתוֹ (*bārāʾ ʾōtô*)

The phrase:

**“he created him”**

uses the singular pronoun:

**“him.”**

This corresponds grammatically with the singular presentation of **ʾādām**.

---

# 19. The Singular and the Plural

The verse then changes:

**“he created him”**

to:

**“he created them.”**

This movement is significant.

Humanity is spoken of both collectively and individually.

---

# 20. “Male and Female” — זָכָר וּנְקֵבָה (*zākār ûnəqēḇāh*)

The Hebrew words are:

**זָכָר (*zākār*)** — male

**נְקֵבָה (*nəqēḇāh*)** — female

The text explicitly identifies humanity in male-and-female form.

---

# 21. “He Created Them” — בָּרָא אֹתָם (*bārāʾ ʾōtām*)

The plural pronoun:

**אֹתָם (*ʾōtām*)**

means:

**“them.”**

The verse therefore concludes by emphasizing the twofold sexual differentiation of humanity.

---

# 22. Male and Female Are Both Created by God

The text uses the same divine creative verb:

**בָּרָא**

for both the creation of humanity and the creation of male and female.

---

# 23. Both Bear God's Image

The structure of the verse places:

**“in the image of God”**

before:

**“male and female.”**

The natural theological conclusion is that both male and female belong to the humanity created in God's image.

---

# 24. Equal Image-Bearing Dignity

The verse therefore establishes the equal fundamental dignity of male and female as image-bearers.

---

# 25. Distinction Without Inferiority

Genesis simultaneously teaches:

**distinction**

and

**shared dignity.**

Male and female are distinct, yet both belong to the image-bearing humanity.

---

# 26. The Image Is Not Male-Only

The verse prevents an interpretation in which only males bear God's image.

It explicitly says:

**“male and female he created them.”**

---

# 27. The Image Is Not Female-Only

Likewise, the image is not assigned exclusively to females.

Both sexes are included.

---

# 28. Humanity Is Male and Female

Genesis presents sexual differentiation as part of the original created order.

---

# 29. The Twofold Structure of Humanity

The verse gives a simple but profound structure:

**humanity**

→

**male and female**

Both are created by God.

---

# 30. Unity and Distinction

Genesis 1:27 therefore holds together:

**one humanity**

and

**two sexes.**

---

# 31. The Image and Human Community

The image of God is not presented merely as an isolated individual characteristic.

The verse speaks of humanity collectively:

**“them.”**

---

# 32. Humanity as a Community

Humanity's image-bearing vocation is lived out within relationships.

---

# 33. The Image and Relationship

The creation of male and female establishes the relational dimension of humanity that will be developed further in Genesis 2.

---

# 34. Genesis 1:27 and Genesis 2

Genesis 1:27 provides the broad theological statement.

Genesis 2 provides a more detailed narrative concerning the creation of Adam and Eve.

---

# 35. Male and Female in God's Original Design

Genesis 1:27 presents male and female differentiation as part of God's original creative work.

---

# 36. Creation, Not Accident

The repeated:

**“he created”**

emphasizes divine intentionality.

---

# 37. Divine Intentionality

Humanity's existence is presented as purposeful rather than accidental.

---

# 38. God Is the Subject

The grammatical and theological emphasis remains on God:

**God created.**

**God created.**

**God created.**

---

# 39. The Threefold “Created”

The verb **bārāʾ** appears three times in the verse:

**“God created…”**

**“He created…”**

**“He created…”**

This repetition gives the verse extraordinary emphasis.

---

# 40. Creation Is God's Work

The repeated verb makes the theological point unmistakable:

**humanity owes its existence to God.**

---

# 41. The Climax of Genesis 1

Genesis 1:27 stands at the climax of the creation account.

The previous verses prepared the earth for humanity.

Now humanity is created.

---

# 42. Humanity Is the Image-Bearer

The creation narrative has moved toward a creature who uniquely bears God's image.

---

# 43. Creation and Kingship

In the ancient world, images of kings could represent the king's authority.

The biblical statement that humanity is God's image therefore carries strong representative and royal implications.

---

# 44. Humanity Represents God's Rule

Genesis 1:26–28 presents humanity as God's appointed representative within the created world.

---

# 45. Dominion Follows Image

The sequence is:

**image**

→

**male and female**

→

**blessing**

→

**fruitfulness**

→

**dominion.**

---

# 46. Humanity Is Created With a Mission

The image of God is connected to vocation.

Humanity is created to fulfill God's purposes within creation.

---

# 47. Human Authority Is Delegated

The authority described in verse 26 is not independent sovereignty.

It is authority received from God.

---

# 48. God Remains the Creator

Even though humanity receives dominion, God remains the ultimate owner and ruler of creation.

---

# 49. Human Dominion Must Reflect God

If humanity represents God, human rule should correspond to God's character.

This means human dominion should be exercised responsibly rather than destructively.

---

# 50. God's Character and Human Rule

The creation account has already revealed God as:

**powerful**

**wise**

**orderly**

**good.**

Humanity's rule should therefore reflect these qualities.

---

# 51. Human Dignity and Human Responsibility

The image of God gives humanity dignity.

The dominion mandate gives humanity responsibility.

These two concepts belong together.

---

# 52. Dignity Without Responsibility Is Incomplete

Humanity is not merely valuable.

Humanity is also commissioned.

---

# 53. Responsibility Without Dignity Is Incomplete

Human beings are not merely workers or rulers.

They are image-bearers.

---

# 54. Image and Vocation

Genesis presents human identity and human vocation together.

Human beings are created **in God's image** and appointed to **rule within creation**.

---

# 55. The Image and Human Ethics

The doctrine of God's image has major ethical implications.

If human beings bear God's image, then human life possesses exceptional dignity.

---

# 56. Genesis 9:6

Later, Genesis grounds the prohibition of murder in the image of God.

The theological reasoning is that human beings were made in God's image.

---

# 57. James 3:9

The New Testament likewise appeals to humanity being made in God's likeness when addressing the way people speak about one another.

---

# 58. The Image After the Fall

The Fall does not erase the significance of God's image.

Later Scripture continues to describe human beings in image-bearing terms.

---

# 59. Human Dignity Is Not Based on Sinlessness

Even fallen humanity retains the creational dignity associated with bearing God's image.

---

# 60. Every Human Being

Genesis 1:27 provides the foundation for a broad doctrine of human dignity.

The text does not distinguish between humans according to social rank.

---

# 61. The Rich and the Poor

Both are human beings.

Both belong to humanity.

Both possess the dignity associated with God's image.

---

# 62. The Powerful and the Weak

Human power does not determine image-bearing status.

Human weakness does not remove it.

---

# 63. The Image and Human Worth

Human worth therefore comes fundamentally from God rather than from human achievement.

---

# 64. Male and Female Together

Genesis does not place male and female in separate categories of image-bearing.

Both belong to the same humanity.

---

# 65. Shared Dignity

The verse provides a strong foundation for affirming the equal dignity of men and women before God.

---

# 66. Distinct Sexes

At the same time, Genesis does not erase sexual distinction.

It explicitly says:

**“male and female.”**

---

# 67. Equality and Difference

The verse therefore establishes:

**equal image-bearing dignity**

alongside

**male-female distinction.**

---

# 68. The Image and Marriage

Genesis 1:27 does not yet describe marriage.

However, the creation of male and female prepares for the marriage account of Genesis 2.

---

# 69. Humanity as God's Creation

The verse emphasizes that both male and female originate from God.

---

# 70. God's Creative Authority

Humanity has no authority to redefine the fact that God created humanity according to His design.

---

# 71. The Goodness of Humanity's Creation

Genesis 1:27 follows God's declaration concerning the goodness of creation and prepares for the broader declaration of Genesis 1:31.

---

# 72. Humanity Is Part of Good Creation

Humanity belongs to the created order.

Yet humanity possesses a unique status within it.

---

# 73. Humanity Is Creaturely

Being made in God's image does not make humans divine.

Humans remain creatures.

---

# 74. Image Does Not Equal Deity

An image represents another.

It is not identical with the one represented.

---

# 75. Creator-Creature Distinction

The distinction remains:

**God → Creator**

**humanity → image-bearing creature.**

---

# 76. Human Greatness and Humility

Genesis gives humanity extraordinary dignity while maintaining humanity's dependence upon God.

---

# 77. Humanity's Dependence

Human beings receive:

**existence**

**identity**

**purpose**

**authority**

from God.

---

# 78. The Image Is a Gift

Humanity does not manufacture its own dignity.

God gives it.

---

# 79. The Image and Worship

Because God alone created humanity, humanity should ultimately direct worship toward the Creator rather than toward itself or creation.

---

# 80. Humanity Should Not Worship Itself

The image of God gives humanity dignity but does not make humanity divine.

---

# 81. The Image and Idolatry

Genesis establishes the proper direction:

**God creates the image-bearer.**

Therefore the image-bearer must not replace the Creator.

---

# 82. Humanity's Proper Position

Humanity stands:

**under God**

**within creation**

**over the creatures entrusted to human care.**

---

# 83. The Threefold Relationship

Genesis 1:27–28 establishes a pattern:

**God above humanity**

**humanity over creation**

**humanity responsible to God.**

---

# 84. God's Image and God's Rule

The image is closely related to God's intention that humanity exercise dominion.

---

# 85. Royal Representation

Humanity functions as God's representative ruler within the created world.

---

# 86. The Image and Kingdom

This theme becomes increasingly important throughout Scripture as God's purposes concerning His kingdom unfold.

---

# 87. Theological Anthropology

Genesis 1:27 is foundational for biblical anthropology—the biblical doctrine of humanity.

It answers the question:

**What is a human being?**

Humanity is God's image-bearing creature.

---

# 88. Humanity's Origin

Humanity originates in God.

---

# 89. Humanity's Identity

Humanity's identity is defined by God's creative act.

---

# 90. Humanity's Dignity

Human dignity is rooted in God's image.

---

# 91. Humanity's Difference

Humanity is distinct from the other creatures because humanity uniquely bears God's image.

---

# 92. Humanity's Sexuality

Humanity exists as:

**male and female.**

---

# 93. Humanity's Mission

The surrounding verses reveal humanity's mission:

**fruitfulness**

**multiplication**

**filling the earth**

**subduing**

**dominion.**

---

# 94. Humanity's Accountability

Because the mission comes from God, humanity remains accountable to God.

---

# 95. Creation and Covenant History

The image doctrine becomes foundational for understanding later biblical relationships between God and humanity.

---

# 96. Christ and the Image

The New Testament develops the theme of God's image further, especially in relation to Christ, who is described as the image of the invisible God.

Genesis 1:27 therefore provides the foundational human image concept that later biblical revelation develops.

---

# 97. Restoration of the Image

New Testament teaching concerning believers being conformed to Christ and renewed according to God's image builds upon the biblical concept established in Genesis.

---

# 98. The Central Theological Proposition

If Genesis 1:27 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God created humanity in His own image, and He created humanity as male and female, giving both the dignity of bearing His image.**

Or more simply:

**God created humanity in His image, male and female, so that both share the dignity of being God's image-bearers.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:27

### **“So God created”**

→ Humanity's existence originates directly in God's creative work.

### **“Man”**

→ The statement concerns humanity.

### **“In his own image”**

→ Humanity uniquely bears God's image.

### **“In the image of God”**

→ The image is explicitly identified as God's image.

### **“He created him”**

→ Humanity exists because God created it.

### **“Male and female”**

→ Humanity is created in male-and-female distinction.

### **“He created them”**

→ Both male and female belong to the image-bearing humanity.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:27 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God created humanity** — human beings originate from God's creative action.
2. **Humanity bears God's image** — humans possess a unique status within creation.
3. **The image is God-given** — human dignity originates in God's creative act.
4. **Human dignity is universal** — the text does not restrict image-bearing to a social class.
5. **Male and female both bear God's image** — both are included within humanity's image-bearing identity.
6. **Humanity is distinct from animals** — humans are uniquely described as God's image-bearers.
7. **Humanity is not divine** — image-bearing does not erase the Creator-creature distinction.
8. **Humanity has a representative vocation** — the image is connected to God's delegated rule.
9. **Humanity has delegated dominion** — the surrounding context assigns authority over creation.
10. **Human authority comes from God** — dominion is received rather than self-generated.
11. **Humanity remains accountable to God** — delegated authority remains under the Creator.
12. **Male and female share fundamental dignity** — neither sex is excluded from God's image.
13. **Sexual differentiation belongs to creation** — Genesis explicitly identifies humanity as male and female.
14. **Unity and distinction coexist** — humanity is one race/species of image-bearers while being male and female.
15. **Human life has unique value** — the image provides the foundation for later biblical ethical teaching.
16. **The image remains significant after the Fall** — later Scripture continues to ground human dignity in it.
17. **Humanity's creation is purposeful** — humans are created with a divine vocation.
18. **Humanity is dependent upon God** — existence and identity are received from the Creator.
19. **Humanity should not worship itself** — image-bearing does not make humanity divine.
20. **God alone is Creator** — the repeated use of *bārāʾ* emphasizes divine creative authority.
21. **Humanity represents God's rule** — the image is closely associated with dominion.
22. **Human dignity and responsibility belong together** — humanity is both image-bearer and appointed ruler.
23. **Creation is intentional** — the repeated “he created” emphasizes divine purpose.
24. **The image is foundational to biblical anthropology** — Genesis 1:27 provides a central definition of humanity's identity.
25. **The creation of humanity prepares for humanity's mission** — the next verse expands the blessing and dominion mandate.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:27 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD CREATED HUMANITY

**“So God created man…”**

Humanity exists because God intentionally created it.

---

## II. GOD CREATED HUMANITY IN HIS IMAGE

**“In the image of God he created him…”**

Human beings possess unique dignity because they bear the image of their Creator.

---

## III. GOD CREATED HUMANITY MALE AND FEMALE

**“Male and female he created them.”**

Both male and female share the dignity of God's image while retaining the distinction established in creation.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:27 teaches that God uniquely created humanity in His own image and created humanity as male and female, giving both the dignity and responsibility of being His image-bearers.**

The progression from Genesis 1:26 to Genesis 1:27 is:

**Genesis 1:26**

God announces:

**“Let us make man…”**

Humanity will be made:

**“in our image”**

**“after our likeness.”**

Humanity will receive:

**dominion over creation.**

**Genesis 1:27**

God fulfills His purpose:

**“So God created man…”**

Humanity is created:

**“in his own image.”**

The image is emphasized:

**“in the image of God…”**

Then humanity is identified as:

**“male and female.”**

And the verse concludes:

**“he created them.”**

The pattern is:

**God creates.**

**God gives His image.**

**God establishes human dignity.**

**God creates humanity male and female.**

**Both belong to God's image-bearing humanity.**
