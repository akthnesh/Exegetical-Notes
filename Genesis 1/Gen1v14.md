# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:14

**Genesis 1:14 —** “And God said, ‘Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens to separate the day from the night. And let them be for signs and for seasons, and for days and years.’”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים יְהִי מְאֹרֹת בִּרְקִיעַ הַשָּׁמַיִם לְהַבְדִּיל בֵּין הַיּוֹם וּבֵין הַלַּיְלָה וְהָיוּ לְאֹתֹת וּלְמוֹעֲדִים וּלְיָמִים וְשָׁנִים**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm yəhî məʾōrōt birqîaʿ haššāmayim ləhaḇdîl bēn hayyôm ûḇēn hallaylâ wəhāyû ləʾōṯōṯ ûləmôʿădîm ûləyāmîm wəšānîm*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And God said, ‘Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens, to separate between the day and between the night; and let them be for signs and for appointed times, and for days and years.’”**

---

# 1. “And God said” — וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים (*wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:14 begins with the familiar creation formula:

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים**

*Wayyōmer ʾĕlōhîm*

**“And God said…”**

This marks the beginning of another creative command.

The previous day established:

**land**

**seas**

**vegetation**

Now God turns His creative command toward the heavenly realm.

The creation account continues to emphasize that God's Word governs the created order.

---

# 2. “Let there be lights” — יְהִי מְאֹרֹת (*yəhî məʾōrōt*)

God commands:

**יְהִי מְאֹרֹת**

*Yəhî məʾōrōt*

**“Let there be lights.”**

The noun **מָאוֹר (*māʾôr*)** means:

**“light”**

or

**“luminary.”**

The plural **מְאֹרֹת (*məʾōrōt*)** therefore means:

**“lights”**

or

**“luminaries.”**

The focus is on heavenly bodies that function as sources or bearers of light.

---

# 3. The distinction between Genesis 1:3 and Genesis 1:14

Genesis 1:3 says:

**“Let there be light.”**

Genesis 1:14 says:

**“Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens.”**

The text therefore distinguishes between:

**light itself**

and

**the heavenly luminaries associated with the ordering of light.**

The existence of light precedes the appearance of the luminaries described on Day Four.

---

# 4. The heavenly location of the lights

The verse specifies:

**בִּרְקִיעַ הַשָּׁמַיִם**

*birqîaʿ haššāmayim*

**“in the expanse of the heavens.”**

The word **רָקִיעַ (*rāqîaʿ*)** refers to the expanse established by God in Genesis 1:6–8.

The heavenly lights are therefore placed within the created heavenly realm.

---

# 5. “The heavens” — הַשָּׁמַיִם (*haššāmayim*)

The word:

**שָׁמַיִם (*šāmayim*)**

means:

**“heavens”**

or

**“sky.”**

Here it identifies the realm in which the heavenly lights are placed.

The lights belong to creation.

They are not themselves divine beings.

---

# 6. Heavenly bodies are created things

Genesis deliberately presents the heavenly lights as things God creates and appoints.

They do not possess divine status.

God commands them.

God assigns their functions.

God places them in the heavens.

This is significant in contrast with ancient cultures that commonly associated celestial bodies with deities.

Genesis places the sun, moon, and stars under the authority of the Creator.

---

# 7. “To separate” — לְהַבְדִּיל (*ləhaḇdîl*)

The purpose of the lights is first stated:

**לְהַבְדִּיל**

*ləhaḇdîl*

**“to separate.”**

This comes from the Hebrew root **בדל (*b-d-l*)**, meaning:

**“to separate,” “distinguish,” or “divide.”**

The same concept has already appeared in Genesis 1.

God separated:

**light from darkness**

and

**waters from waters.**

Now the heavenly lights function in the continuing distinction between day and night.

---

# 8. “Between the day and the night”

The verse says:

**בֵּין הַיּוֹם וּבֵין הַלַּיְלָה**

*bēn hayyôm ûḇēn hallaylâ*

**“between the day and between the night.”**

The heavenly lights therefore participate in the regular distinction between:

**day**

and

**night.**

This continues the ordering established on Day One.

---

# 9. The lights govern the day-night distinction

The lights are not introduced merely as beautiful objects.

They have a functional purpose.

They help distinguish:

**day**

from

**night.**

The creation account therefore emphasizes their role within God's ordered temporal system.

---

# 10. “And let them be” — וְהָיוּ (*wəhāyû*)

The verse continues:

**וְהָיוּ**

*wəhāyû*

**“and let them be”**

or

**“and let them serve as.”**

This introduces additional purposes for the heavenly lights.

They are not simply present.

They have assigned functions.

---

# 11. “For signs” — לְאֹתֹת (*ləʾōṯōṯ*)

The first additional function is:

**לְאֹתֹת**

*ləʾōṯōṯ*

**“for signs.”**

The noun **אוֹת (*ʾôt*)** means:

**“sign,” “mark,” or “token.”**

Here the plural refers to observable signs or markers.

The heavenly lights provide recognizable markers within creation.

---

# 12. “For seasons” — וּלְמוֹעֲדִים (*ûləmôʿădîm*)

The next function is:

**וּלְמוֹעֲדִים**

*ûləmôʿădîm*

**“for seasons.”**

The noun **מוֹעֵד (*môʿēd*)** can refer to:

**an appointed time**

**appointed occasion**

**set time**

or

**season.**

The plural **מוֹעֲדִים (*môʿădîm*)** therefore carries the idea of appointed or fixed times.

---

# 13. “Seasons” and “appointed times”

The word **מֹועֵד (*môʿēd*)** is broader than the English word **“season.”**

It can describe periods or occasions that are appointed or fixed.

In later biblical usage, the word becomes especially important for Israel's appointed religious times.

Here in Genesis 1:14, however, the immediate context concerns the ordering of time through the heavenly lights.

---

# 14. The heavenly lights and the calendar

The lights provide markers by which time can be recognized.

The regular movements of the heavenly bodies provide a framework for:

**days**

**years**

and

**appointed times.**

Creation therefore contains an observable temporal order.

---

# 15. “For days” — וּלְיָמִים (*ûləyāmîm*)

The verse explicitly mentions:

**וּלְיָמִים**

*ûləyāmîm*

**“for days.”**

The singular **יוֹם (*yôm*)** means:

**“day.”**

The plural **יָמִים (*yāmîm*)** means:

**“days.”**

The heavenly lights therefore function as markers within the recurring cycle of days.

---

# 16. “And years” — וְשָׁנִים (*wəšānîm*)

The final temporal category is:

**וְשָׁנִים**

*wəšānîm*

**“and years.”**

The noun **שָׁנָה (*šānâ*)** means:

**“year.”**

The heavenly lights therefore provide markers not only for daily cycles but also for longer annual cycles.

---

# 17. Day and year

Genesis 1:14 establishes two fundamental temporal patterns:

**day**

and

**year.**

The day is associated with the regular alternation of light and darkness.

The year corresponds to larger recurring cycles.

The heavenly lights therefore function as part of God's ordering of time.

---

# 18. Time is ordered by God

Genesis 1:14 presents time as something God orders.

The heavenly lights are assigned functions within that temporal order.

Therefore time is not presented as meaningless or random.

God establishes recognizable rhythms within creation.

---

# 19. The lights are servants of God's purposes

The heavenly lights are assigned specific functions.

They:

**separate day from night**

**serve as signs**

**mark appointed times**

**mark days**

**mark years.**

They therefore function as servants within God's created order.

---

# 20. The sun and moon are not named here

Interestingly, Genesis 1:14 does not initially identify the heavenly lights as:

**sun**

and

**moon.**

Those terms appear in verse 16.

The narrative first emphasizes their function before identifying them more specifically.

This keeps attention focused upon their role within God's created order.

---

# 21. The theological significance of withholding their names

The surrounding ancient world often treated the sun and moon as divine beings.

Genesis instead presents them simply as **lights** created and appointed by God.

The text strips them of divine status.

They are part of creation.

God alone is the Creator.

---

# 22. The stars also belong to creation

Verse 16 will mention the stars.

Therefore the heavenly realm is populated by created lights under God's authority.

The stars are not autonomous divine powers.

They belong to God's creation.

---

# 23. Genesis and ancient Near Eastern religion

Genesis 1 provides a fundamentally different worldview from systems that deified celestial bodies.

The sun is not a god.

The moon is not a god.

The stars are not gods.

They are created lights.

The Creator stands above them all.

---

# 24. God rules the heavens

The heavenly lights are placed:

**“in the expanse of the heavens.”**

They occupy a created realm.

God determines their purpose.

This reinforces God's supremacy over the heavens.

---

# 25. Genesis 1:14 and Day One

Day One introduced light and darkness.

Day Four introduces the luminaries that govern the visible distinction between them.

This creates a literary correspondence:

### Day One

**Light and darkness**

### Day Four

**Luminaries governing day and night**

The structure of Genesis 1 therefore contains deliberate relationships between the days.

---

# 26. Genesis 1:14 and Day Four

Day Four begins the second major half of the creation account.

The first three days establish and prepare realms.

The next three days increasingly fill those realms.

Day Four fills the heavenly realm with luminaries.

Day Five fills the waters and skies.

Day Six fills the land.

---

# 27. The forming and filling pattern

The correspondence can be represented:

**Day 1 → Day 4**

Light → Luminaries

**Day 2 → Day 5**

Sky and waters → Birds and sea creatures

**Day 3 → Day 6**

Land → Land animals and humanity

Genesis 1:14 therefore participates in this larger literary architecture.

---

# 28. The lights and God's ordering of creation

The creation account repeatedly emphasizes separation and distinction.

God separates:

**light from darkness**

**waters from waters**

**land from seas**

Now the heavenly lights help maintain the distinction between:

**day**

and

**night.**

The theme of ordered separation continues.

---

# 29. The lights and human orientation

The heavenly lights provide observable markers by which creatures can orient themselves within time.

Humans can recognize:

**day**

**night**

**seasons**

**years.**

The created world therefore contains signs that make temporal order perceptible.

---

# 30. “Signs” does not necessarily mean omens

The word **אוֹת (*ʾôt*)** means **sign** or **marker**.

In this context, the verse does not require the idea of astrological prediction.

The heavenly bodies function as signs because they mark and communicate the rhythms of created time.

This is different from treating celestial bodies as divine sources of hidden knowledge.

---

# 31. Genesis 1:14 and astrology

The verse does not teach astrology.

The heavenly bodies are created by God and assigned functions within creation.

They are not presented as independent spiritual powers controlling human destiny.

The biblical worldview places celestial bodies under God's authority.

---

# 32. Signs within creation

A sign points beyond itself to something recognizable.

The heavenly lights provide observable indicators of:

**day**

**night**

**seasons**

**years.**

Their regularity allows humans to recognize God's ordered world.

---

# 33. “Appointed times”

The phrase **לְמוֹעֲדִים (*ləmôʿădîm*)** is particularly significant.

It can refer to:

**appointed times**

or

**fixed times.**

The heavenly lights therefore participate in God's ordering of time for human life and worship.

---

# 34. Connection with later biblical festivals

Later in Scripture, Israel receives appointed times associated with the covenant calendar.

The same Hebrew root **יעד (*y-ʿ-d*)** lies behind **מוֹעֵד**.

However, Genesis 1:14 itself does not yet establish Israel's later feast calendar.

The verse establishes the broader principle that God has created heavenly markers for appointed times.

---

# 35. Genesis 1:14 and worship

The ordering of time eventually becomes important for worship.

Israel's appointed feasts are scheduled according to God's calendar.

Thus the heavenly ordering established in Genesis provides the broader creation framework for later sacred time.

---

# 36. Creation establishes a calendar

The heavenly lights make possible the recognition of:

**days**

**months**

**seasons**

**years**

and other appointed times.

The created world therefore contains a built-in temporal rhythm.

---

# 37. Genesis 1:14 and human civilization

Human societies depend upon the ability to recognize time.

Agriculture depends upon seasons.

Travel can depend upon celestial observation.

Calendars depend upon recurring cycles.

Genesis 1:14 establishes the heavenly foundations for these temporal patterns.

---

# 38. Genesis 1:14 and agriculture

The seasons are particularly significant for agricultural life.

Planting and harvesting depend upon recurring cycles.

The lights in the heavens provide markers within these cycles.

Thus the heavenly order contributes to the fruitfulness of the earth established on Day Three.

---

# 39. Day Four and Day Three

There is a meaningful relationship between:

**vegetation on Day Three**

and

**luminaries on Day Four.**

Vegetation depends upon the continuing rhythms of light and time.

The narrative therefore moves from:

**productive earth**

to

**heavenly markers governing its temporal environment.**

---

# 40. God's provision through time

God provides not only physical resources but temporal order.

The earth has:

**food**

and

**fruitfulness.**

The heavens have:

**markers**

and

**cycles.**

Together they form an ordered environment for life.

---

# 41. Genesis 1:14 and providence

The regularity of day and night, seasons, and years becomes part of God's providential ordering of creation.

The created world continues according to patterns established by God.

---

# 42. Divine sovereignty over celestial bodies

God determines what the heavenly lights are for.

They do not determine their own purpose.

They do not rule God.

God rules them.

This is a major theological emphasis of the creation account.

---

# 43. Creation versus Creator

Genesis 1:14 reinforces the distinction:

**God → Creator**

**heavenly lights → creatures**

This distinction is foundational to biblical worship.

Only the Creator deserves worship.

Created things must not receive the worship that belongs to God.

---

# 44. The heavens declare God's glory

Later Scripture teaches that the heavens declare God's glory.

Psalm 19:1 says:

**“The heavens declare the glory of God.”**

Genesis 1 provides the foundation for this theology.

The heavenly lights are not gods.

They are witnesses to the glory of their Creator.

---

# 45. Genesis 1:14 and God's wisdom

The heavenly system displays remarkable order.

There are:

**cycles**

**patterns**

**distinctions**

**seasons**

**years.**

The text attributes this ordered system to God's creative wisdom.

---

# 46. Genesis 1:14 and God's faithfulness

The regularity of celestial cycles provides a stable environment for life.

Day follows night.

Seasons recur.

Years pass.

The regularity of creation reflects the continuing faithfulness of God's providential order.

---

# 47. Genesis 1:14 and the biblical understanding of time

Time is neither ultimate nor divine.

God is the Creator.

Time belongs to creation.

The heavenly lights mark temporal cycles within the created order.

This prevents time itself from becoming an object of worship.

---

# 48. Genesis 1:14 and eternity

God is not presented as being bound by the cycles He establishes.

The heavenly lights mark:

**days**

and

**years.**

God creates and governs these cycles.

The Creator therefore stands above the temporal order He establishes.

---

# 49. Genesis 1:14 and human mortality

Later Scripture repeatedly connects human life with the passage of days and years.

Human beings experience time as creatures.

Genesis 1:14 establishes the created framework within which human life will occur.

---

# 50. Genesis 1:14 and human stewardship of time

Because God establishes days and years, human beings later live within a divinely ordered temporal world.

Scripture consequently teaches people to number their days wisely.

Time is a gift from God and should be used faithfully.

---

# 51. Genesis 1:14 and the Sabbath

The ordering of days becomes especially significant when God later establishes the Sabbath rhythm.

Genesis 2:2–3 will describe God's completion of creation and His blessing of the seventh day.

Genesis 1:14 provides the temporal framework in which this weekly rhythm operates.

---

# 52. Genesis 1:14 and the seventh-day pattern

The creation account develops a sequence:

**Day One**

**Day Two**

**Day Three**

**Day Four**

**Day Five**

**Day Six**

followed by:

**Day Seven.**

The heavenly lights therefore belong to the larger temporal architecture leading toward the Sabbath.

---

# 53. Genesis 1:14 and biblical chronology

The verse provides another indication that Genesis is concerned with temporal order.

It explicitly mentions:

**days**

and

**years.**

The heavenly bodies serve as markers for these cycles.

---

# 54. Genesis 1:14 and scientific observation

The verse recognizes observable celestial patterns without attempting to provide a modern astronomical model.

Its purpose is theological:

**God created the heavenly lights and assigned them functions within His ordered creation.**

The verse should therefore be read according to its literary and theological purpose.

---

# 55. Genesis 1:14 and astronomy

The heavenly bodies belong to the created order.

Studying them does not inherently conflict with biblical faith.

Indeed, the biblical text presents the heavens as ordered and observable.

The problem arises not from studying the heavens but from assigning created celestial objects divine authority.

---

# 56. Genesis 1:14 and ancient paganism

The theological force of the verse becomes clearer when read against the ancient world.

Many surrounding cultures associated celestial bodies with gods.

Genesis reverses that worldview.

The celestial lights are not gods.

They are God's creatures.

They exist according to His command and purpose.

---

# 57. The lights do not control human destiny

Genesis does not present the heavenly bodies as personal gods controlling human fate.

They mark time.

They govern the visible rhythms of day and night.

They identify seasons and years.

Their function is created and ordered rather than divine.

---

# 58. Genesis 1:14 and divine purpose

The repeated phrase:

**“let them be…”**

followed by assigned functions shows purposeful creation.

The lights exist:

**to separate**

**to mark**

**to indicate**

**to order.**

Creation has purpose because the Creator gives it purpose.

---

# 59. Genesis 1:14 and creation's intelligibility

The universe is presented as ordered and recognizable.

Humans can observe:

**day**

**night**

**seasons**

**years.**

This assumes that creation is sufficiently ordered to be understood.

The biblical worldview therefore provides a foundation for studying the natural world.

---

# 60. Genesis 1:14 and the glory of God

The heavenly lights display:

**power**

**order**

**beauty**

**regularity**

**purpose.**

These characteristics direct attention toward their Creator.

The heavens are magnificent because the Creator is magnificent.

---

# 61. Genesis 1:14 and the supremacy of God

God does not ask the sun or moon for permission.

He commands the heavenly lights.

He assigns their functions.

The Creator therefore possesses absolute authority over the heavenly realm.

---

# 62. Genesis 1:14 and the created hierarchy

The verse establishes an important hierarchy:

**God**

↓

**heavenly realm**

↓

**heavenly lights**

The lights are within creation.

God is above creation.

---

# 63. Genesis 1:14 and divine providence over seasons

The changing seasons are part of the ordered world God establishes.

The heavenly lights serve as markers of those recurring periods.

This becomes especially important for agriculture and human civilization.

---

# 64. Genesis 1:14 and God's care for life

Vegetation was established on Day Three.

The lights appear on Day Four.

The temporal rhythms marked by the lights provide an environment in which terrestrial life can continue.

The created system is interconnected.

---

# 65. Genesis 1:14 and the structure of the creation week

The verse introduces Day Four.

The creation week now moves into its second half.

The first three days establish the realms.

The second three days increasingly fill them.

Day Four fills the heavens.

---

# 66. Day Four as counterpart to Day One

Day One:

**light and darkness**

Day Four:

**lights governing day and night**

This correspondence reinforces the literary unity of Genesis 1.

The second half of the week corresponds to the first half.

---

# 67. Day Four as a sign of order

The heavenly lights demonstrate that the universe has predictable patterns.

These patterns allow creatures to live within a structured environment.

The creation is therefore not chaotic.

It is ordered by God.

---

# 68. Genesis 1:14 and God's Word

Once again, the creative work begins with:

**“And God said…”**

The heavenly lights appear because God commands them.

The authority of God's Word extends from:

**light**

to

**land**

to

**vegetation**

to

**heavenly luminaries.**

---

# 69. Genesis 1:14 and the authority of God's Word

The universe is responsive to divine command.

God speaks.

The created order conforms.

This repeated pattern establishes the authority and effectiveness of God's Word.

---

# 70. Genesis 1:14 and Christological creation

Genesis 1:14 does not explicitly mention Christ.

However, the New Testament teaches that Christ is the agent through whom all things were created.

John 1:3 says:

**“All things were made through him.”**

Colossians 1:16 says:

**“all things were created through him and for him.”**

Therefore, the heavenly lights described here ultimately belong within the New Testament's doctrine of creation through Christ.

---

# 71. Genesis 1:14 and Christ's lordship

If all things were created through Christ, then the heavenly lights are ultimately subject to His lordship.

The stars, sun, moon, and entire created order exist through Him and for Him.

---

# 72. Genesis 1:14 and worship

The correct response to the heavens is not worship of the heavenly bodies.

It is worship of their Creator.

The heavenly lights point beyond themselves to God.

Creation is a witness to divine glory.

---

# 73. Genesis 1:14 and the final biblical vision

The Bible's final vision includes a renewed creation in which God's glory provides illumination.

Revelation 21:23 describes the New Jerusalem as having no need of sun or moon because God's glory gives it light.

This creates a profound biblical contrast:

**Genesis 1**

God establishes created lights.

**Revelation 21**

God's glory itself supplies the final city's light.

The created lights are therefore not ultimate.

God Himself is ultimate.

---

# 74. Genesis 1:14 and the ultimate source of light

Genesis begins with:

**“Let there be light.”**

Later Scripture identifies God as light.

The biblical storyline therefore moves from created light toward the ultimate glory of God.

The heavenly lights are servants of the Creator.

They are not the final source of divine glory.

---

# 75. The literary function of Genesis 1:14

Genesis 1:14 introduces the fourth day's creative command.

It establishes:

**what God creates**

**where He places it**

and

**what purposes it serves.**

The verse therefore serves as the foundation for verses 15–18.

---

# 76. The purposes of the heavenly lights

Genesis 1:14 gives several purposes:

### 1. Separation

**Day from night**

### 2. Signs

Observable markers

### 3. Appointed times

Fixed seasons or times

### 4. Days

Daily cycles

### 5. Years

Annual cycles

The verse therefore emphasizes function rather than merely appearance.

---

# 77. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:14 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God creates and appoints the heavenly lights to govern the distinction between day and night and to mark the ordered rhythms of signs, appointed times, days, and years.**

Or more simply:

**God appoints the heavenly lights to establish and mark the rhythms of created time.**

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:14

### **“And God said”**

→ God's Word initiates another stage of creation.

### **“Let there be lights”**

→ God establishes heavenly luminaries.

### **“In the expanse of the heavens”**

→ The lights are placed within the created heavenly realm.

### **“To separate the day from the night”**

→ The lights serve the distinction between day and night.

### **“For signs”**

→ They function as observable markers.

### **“For seasons”**

→ They mark appointed or recurring times.

### **“For days and years”**

→ They establish recognizable temporal cycles.

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:14 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God commands and appoints the heavenly lights.
2. **Divine creation** — celestial bodies are created things.
3. **Creator–creature distinction** — heavenly bodies are not divine.
4. **Monotheism** — one God rules the heavens.
5. **Rejection of celestial worship** — sun, moon, and stars belong to creation.
6. **Divine order** — God establishes regular temporal rhythms.
7. **Created time** — days and years belong to the created order.
8. **Divine providence** — celestial cycles serve God's purposes for creation.
9. **Day-night distinction** — God establishes observable temporal boundaries.
10. **Signs within creation** — heavenly bodies provide recognizable markers.
11. **Appointed times** — creation contains fixed and recurring temporal patterns.
12. **Human orientation** — the heavenly lights enable recognition of time.
13. **Agricultural order** — seasons contribute to the productivity of the earth.
14. **Sabbath framework** — the ordered days provide the temporal structure for the creation week.
15. **Creation's intelligibility** — the universe is sufficiently ordered to be observed and understood.
16. **Divine wisdom** — the heavenly system demonstrates purposeful design.
17. **Divine faithfulness** — recurring celestial patterns contribute to the stability of creation.
18. **Anti-idolatry** — created lights must not receive worship belonging to God.
19. **Christological creation** — the New Testament teaches that all things were created through Christ.
20. **Christological lordship** — the heavenly realm ultimately belongs to Christ.
21. **God's glory** — the heavens reveal the glory of their Creator.
22. **New creation** — the biblical storyline ultimately moves toward a creation illuminated by God's own glory.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:14 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD ORDERS THE HEAVENS

**“Let there be lights in the expanse of the heavens…”**

The heavenly bodies exist because God created and appointed them.

They are creatures, not gods.

---

## II. GOD ORDERS TIME

**“To separate the day from the night…”**

**“For signs and for seasons, and for days and years.”**

God establishes recognizable rhythms within creation.

Time itself operates according to His ordering.

---

## III. CREATION POINTS TO THE CREATOR

The heavenly lights are not objects of worship.

They are signs within creation that ultimately direct attention toward the One who created them.

The heavens display the wisdom, power, and glory of God.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:14 teaches that God sovereignly creates and appoints the heavenly lights within the expanse of the heavens to distinguish day from night and to mark the ordered rhythms of appointed times, days, and years, demonstrating that both the heavens and time itself operate according to His wise and purposeful design.**

The progression from Genesis 1:11 through Genesis 1:14 is:

**Genesis 1:11**

God commands:

**“Let the earth sprout vegetation.”**

**Genesis 1:12**

The earth responds:

**vegetation appears**

**plants yield seed**
