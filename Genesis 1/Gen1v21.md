# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:21

**Genesis 1:21 —** “So God created the great sea creatures and every living creature that moves, with which the waters swarm, according to their kinds, and every winged bird according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיִּבְרָא אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הַתַּנִּינִם הַגְּדֹלִים וְאֵת כָּל־נֶפֶשׁ הַחַיָּה הָרֹמֶשֶׂת אֲשֶׁר שָׁרְצוּ הַמַּיִם לְמִינֵהֶם וְאֵת כָּל־עוֹף כָּנָף לְמִינֵהוּ וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים כִּי־טוֹב׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyiḇrāʾ ʾĕlōhîm ʾet-hattanînîm haggədōlîm wəʾēt kol-nepeš hayyāh hārōmeśet ʾăšer šārəṣû hammayim ləmînēhem wəʾēt kol-ʿôp̄ kānāp̄ ləmînēhû wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm kî-ṭôḇ*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And God created the great sea creatures and every living creature that moves, with which the waters swarm according to their kinds, and every winged creature according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.”**

---

# 1. “So God created” — וַיִּבְרָא אֱלֹהִים (*wayyiḇrāʾ ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:21 begins with one of the most important verbs in the creation account:

**וַיִּבְרָא**

*wayyiḇrāʾ*

**“And God created.”**

The verb comes from the Hebrew root:

**בָּרָא (*bārāʾ*)**

meaning:

**“to create.”**

The subject is explicitly:

**אֱלֹהִים (*ʾĕlōhîm*)**

**“God.”**

---

# 2. God is the Creator of animal life

Verse 20 recorded God's command:

**“Let the waters swarm…”**

Verse 21 now explicitly states:

**“God created…”**

The command of verse 20 is followed by God's creative action.

---

# 3. The distinction between command and fulfillment

The sequence is significant:

### Genesis 1:20

**God commands.**

### Genesis 1:21

**God creates.**

The narrative therefore emphasizes that creation occurs according to God's sovereign word.

---

# 4. “The great sea creatures” — הַתַּנִּינִם הַגְּדֹלִים (*hattannînîm haggədōlîm*)

The first creatures specifically mentioned are:

**הַתַּנִּינִם הַגְּדֹלִים**

*hattannînîm haggədōlîm*

**“the great sea creatures.”**

The noun **תַּנִּין (*tannîn*)** can refer to:

**large sea creatures**

**great aquatic creatures**

or, depending on context elsewhere in Scripture,

**dragon-like creatures.**

The ESV renders the phrase:

**“the great sea creatures.”**

---

# 5. The great creatures are created by God

Whatever specific creatures are included in the Hebrew term, the theological point is unmistakable:

**God created them.**

Even the largest and most impressive creatures of the sea remain creatures under God's authority.

---

# 6. God's sovereignty over the sea

The sea is presented as a realm completely under God's creative authority.

God does not battle the sea creatures.

He creates them.

---

# 7. The great sea creatures and ancient imagery

In the ancient world, enormous sea creatures could be associated with terrifying or chaotic powers.

Genesis places even these creatures within God's created order.

They are not divine rivals.

They are God's creatures.

---

# 8. No cosmic rival

Genesis 1:21 contains no struggle between God and the sea.

God simply creates the great sea creatures.

This reinforces the absolute sovereignty of the Creator.

---

# 9. The sea is filled with life

Verse 20 commanded:

**“Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures.”**

Verse 21 now describes the fulfillment.

The waters are populated with an enormous variety of living creatures.

---

# 10. “Every living creature” — כָּל־נֶפֶשׁ הַחַיָּה (*kol-nepeš hayyāh*)

The phrase:

**כָּל־נֶפֶשׁ הַחַיָּה**

means:

**“every living creature.”**

The noun **נֶפֶשׁ (*nepeš*)** here refers to a living being or creature.

The adjective **חַיָּה (*ḥayyāh*)** means:

**“living.”**

---

# 11. The breadth of creation

The phrase:

**“every living creature”**

emphasizes the comprehensive scope of God's creative work.

Genesis is not describing a single species.

It presents a broad world of living creatures.

---

# 12. “That moves” — הָרֹמֶשֶׂת (*hārōmeśet*)

The text describes creatures:

**“that moves.”**

The Hebrew verb **רָמַשׂ (*rāmaś*)** conveys the idea of moving, creeping, or moving about.

The emphasis is on living creatures moving through the waters.

---

# 13. The waters swarm

The phrase connects directly with Genesis 1:20.

God commanded the waters to swarm.

Verse 21 describes the creatures with which the waters actually swarm.

---

# 14. God's command is fulfilled

The relationship between verses 20 and 21 can be summarized:

**Verse 20:**

“Let the waters swarm.”

**Verse 21:**

“God created…”

The created reality corresponds to God's command.

---

# 15. “According to their kinds” — לְמִינֵהֶם (*ləmînēhem*)

The text says:

**לְמִינֵהֶם**

*ləmînēhem*

**“according to their kinds.”**

The Hebrew noun:

**מִין (*mîn*)**

refers to a:

**“kind”**

or

**“type.”**

---

# 16. Created kinds

Genesis emphasizes that living creatures are created:

**according to their kinds.**

This phrase appears repeatedly in the creation account.

---

# 17. Order within biological diversity

The expression indicates both:

**diversity**

and

**order.**

Creation contains many kinds of living creatures, but their diversity exists within God's ordered creation.

---

# 18. Diversity is intentional

The abundance of creatures does not imply disorder.

There are different kinds of creatures within the created order.

---

# 19. “Every winged bird” — וְאֵת כָּל־עוֹף כָּנָף (*wəʾēt kol-ʿôp̄ kānāp̄*)

The verse then moves from aquatic life to flying creatures:

**“every winged bird.”**

The Hebrew expression literally conveys the idea of:

**“every winged flying creature.”**

---

# 20. “Winged”

The Hebrew noun:

**כָּנָף (*kānāp̄*)**

means:

**“wing”**

or

**“winged part.”**

The expression therefore emphasizes creatures characterized by wings.

---

# 21. The birds of the heavens

The flying creatures correspond to the command in verse 20:

**“let birds fly above the earth.”**

Verse 21 records their creation.

---

# 22. Aquatic and aerial life

Verse 21 therefore covers two major categories:

**creatures of the waters**

and

**winged creatures of the air.**

---

# 23. The fifth day is fulfilled

The command of Day Five has now been carried out.

The waters are populated.

The skies are populated.

---

# 24. “According to its kind” — לְמִינֵהוּ (*ləmînēhû*)

For the winged creatures, the text says:

**לְמִינֵהוּ**

*ləmînēhû*

**“according to its kind.”**

Again, the emphasis is upon ordered diversity.

---

# 25. Singular and plural

The aquatic creatures are described:

**“according to their kinds.”**

The winged creature category is described:

**“according to its kind.”**

The important theological point is the same:

**God creates living creatures within an ordered diversity of kinds.**

---

# 26. God creates variety

The passage does not portray creation as consisting of only a few identical organisms.

It presents an abundant and varied living world.

---

# 27. God creates order

At the same time, this diversity is not random.

The repeated expression:

**“according to its kind”**

shows structured diversity.

---

# 28. The goodness of diversity

The diversity of created life is part of God's good creation.

Different creatures occupy different environments and fulfill different roles.

---

# 29. “And God saw” — וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים (*wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm*)

The verse concludes with another familiar creation formula:

**“And God saw…”**

God evaluates the creatures He has made.

---

# 30. “That it was good” — כִּי־טוֹב (*kî-ṭôḇ*)

God's evaluation is:

**“that it was good.”**

The adjective:

**טוֹב (*ṭôḇ*)**

means:

**“good”**

**“beneficial”**

**“pleasant”**

or

**“excellent.”**

---

# 31. God's approval of animal life

God declares the newly created animal life:

**good.**

This includes the great sea creatures and the winged creatures.

---

# 32. The goodness of animals

Animal life is therefore part of God's good creation.

Animals are not presented as inherently evil.

---

# 33. The goodness of the sea

The sea itself is part of God's created order.

The creatures inhabiting it are declared good.

---

# 34. The goodness of the sky

The winged creatures are also part of God's good creation.

Their existence and diversity are affirmed by the Creator.

---

# 35. Creation receives divine approval

The declaration:

**“God saw that it was good”**

is more than an aesthetic observation.

It is divine approval of the created order.

---

# 36. God's evaluation is authoritative

God is the Creator.

Therefore His declaration of goodness establishes the objective status of His creation.

---

# 37. The created world is good

Genesis 1:21 contributes strongly to the biblical doctrine that God's original creation is good.

---

# 38. Animal life is purposeful

The creatures are not described as meaningless additions.

They inhabit the realms prepared for them.

---

# 39. The correspondence between realm and creature

The pattern is:

**waters → sea creatures**

**expanse → winged creatures**

This correspondence reflects God's wisdom.

---

# 40. The filling of Day Five

The fifth day corresponds to the realms established on Day Two.

### Day Two

God establishes:

**waters**

**expanse**

### Day Five

God fills them with:

**sea creatures**

**flying creatures**

---

# 41. The forming-and-filling pattern

Genesis 1 therefore follows a deliberate structure:

**God forms.**

**God fills.**

Day Five demonstrates the filling side of this pattern.

---

# 42. Creation is progressively populated

The creation account moves from:

**uninhabited realms**

toward

**inhabited realms.**

The waters and skies are now filled with life.

---

# 43. The abundance of life

The phrase:

**“with which the waters swarm”**

communicates abundance.

God's creative work is characterized by fullness.

---

# 44. The richness of God's creation

The diversity of aquatic and flying creatures reveals the richness of God's creative work.

---

# 45. God's creative generosity

God does not merely create life.

He creates abundant life.

The waters swarm with creatures.

---

# 46. Life is not accidental

The verse repeatedly attributes the existence of these creatures to God:

**“God created…”**

This is the theological center of the verse.

---

# 47. God creates living creatures

The ultimate explanation given by Genesis for animal life is divine creation.

---

# 48. Creation by God's word

Verse 20 contains the command.

Verse 21 contains the fulfillment.

Together they emphasize creation through God's authoritative word.

---

# 49. Divine sovereignty

God determines:

**what creatures exist**

**where they exist**

**their kinds**

and

**their environments.**

---

# 50. Divine wisdom

The correspondence between creature and habitat demonstrates purposeful organization.

---

# 51. Divine goodness

God creates a world filled with diverse living creatures and declares the result:

**good.**

---

# 52. Divine abundance

The sea creatures are described as swarming.

This points to an abundant creation.

---

# 53. Divine order

The phrase:

**“according to their kinds”**

shows that abundance does not eliminate order.

---

# 54. Diversity and order together

Genesis presents two truths simultaneously:

**creation is diverse**

and

**creation is ordered.**

There is no contradiction between the two.

---

# 55. Created kinds and modern taxonomy

The biblical expression **“according to their kinds”** should not automatically be equated with modern biological taxonomic ranks such as:

**species**

**genus**

or

**family.**

The Hebrew term is broader and functions within the narrative's own classification system.

---

# 56. The primary theological purpose

The primary concern is not to provide a modern scientific taxonomy.

The passage emphasizes:

**God's creative authority**

**the abundance of life**

**the order of creation**

and

**the goodness of God's work.**

---

# 57. The great sea creatures

The specific mention of the great sea creatures is significant.

The text deliberately includes even the largest and most impressive creatures of the sea within God's creative work.

---

# 58. God's authority over terrifying creatures

Whatever fearsome creatures humans may encounter, they remain creatures made by God.

Nothing in creation rivals the Creator.

---

# 59. The sea is not God's enemy

The biblical creation account does not portray God struggling against the sea.

He commands it and fills it.

---

# 60. The absence of cosmic combat

Unlike some ancient creation myths, Genesis does not describe creation as the result of a battle between competing gods.

God creates by His sovereign word.

---

# 61. Monotheistic creation

One God creates the entire realm of:

**sea**

**sky**

**animals**

and

**humanity.**

---

# 62. The Creator-creature distinction

The great sea creatures may be enormous and powerful.

Yet they remain creatures.

Their greatness does not make them divine.

---

# 63. Rejection of animal worship

Because creatures are created by God, they should not receive the worship belonging to the Creator.

---

# 64. The living creatures belong to God

The creatures exist because God creates them.

Their ultimate ownership therefore belongs to God.

---

# 65. Implications for stewardship

Later Scripture gives humanity responsibility over creation.

But humanity's authority is delegated.

The animals already belong to God.

---

# 66. Creation care

The goodness of animal life provides a theological foundation for responsible treatment of the created world.

---

# 67. God's concern for creation

The fact that God evaluates animal life as good demonstrates His positive valuation of the created world.

---

# 68. Animals have creaturely value

Animals are not merely objects created for human convenience.

They are God's creatures and part of His good creation.

---

# 69. Humanity has not yet appeared

This is important.

Genesis 1:21 describes the creation of animals before humanity.

The world is already filled with living creatures before humans are created.

---

# 70. Humanity enters an already living world

When humanity eventually appears, it enters a world already populated by:

**plants**

**sea creatures**

**birds**

and later

**land animals.**

---

# 71. Humanity is not the creator of life

Humans enter a world whose life originates in God.

This reinforces human dependence upon God.

---

# 72. Shared creatureliness

Humans and animals will both be living creatures.

But humanity will possess a distinctive status:

**bearing God's image.**

---

# 73. Humanity's unique vocation

The creation of animals prepares the stage for humanity's later commission to exercise dominion.

---

# 74. Animal creation and human dominion

Human dominion is given over creatures God has already created.

Therefore dominion should be understood as stewardship under God's authority.

---

# 75. God's ownership precedes human stewardship

God creates the animals first.

Human authority comes later.

---

# 76. The goodness of the natural world

Genesis 1:21 strongly supports the biblical affirmation that the natural world, as originally created, is good.

---

# 77. Creation is not inherently evil

The sea and its creatures are declared good.

The air and its creatures are declared good.

The physical world is therefore not inherently evil.

---

# 78. The goodness of embodied life

Living creatures exist physically within God's good creation.

This becomes important for biblical theology concerning the value of embodied life.

---

# 79. Creation and redemption

Biblical redemption ultimately concerns not merely escape from creation but the restoration and renewal of creation.

---

# 80. New creation

The goodness of the original creation provides the theological foundation for the hope of renewed creation.

---

# 81. The fifth day and blessing

Genesis 1:21 is immediately followed by God's blessing in verse 22.

The sequence is:

**creation**

→

**evaluation**

→

**blessing**

→

**fruitfulness.**

---

# 82. God's blessing follows His creative work

God first creates the creatures.

Then He blesses them.

This demonstrates that fruitfulness ultimately operates within God's blessing.

---

# 83. Fruitfulness is part of God's design

The abundance described in verse 21 anticipates multiplication in verse 22.

The created world is designed for flourishing.

---

# 84. God's generosity toward creatures

God not only creates living beings.

He establishes conditions for their continuation and multiplication.

---

# 85. The relationship between life and blessing

The fifth day presents life as something capable of:

**multiplication**

**fruitfulness**

and

**flourishing.**

---

# 86. The goodness of reproduction

The later command to be fruitful and multiply belongs to God's good design for animal life.

---

# 87. Creation and biological continuity

The living creatures are created with the capacity to continue life within their kinds.

Genesis presents this continuity as part of God's ordered creation.

---

# 88. God's providence

The continued existence of animal populations ultimately depends upon the created order God establishes.

---

# 89. The fifth day and divine providence

The aquatic and aerial environments are suitable for the creatures God creates.

This reveals providential preparation.

---

# 90. The fifth day and divine wisdom

God creates creatures appropriate to their environments.

This demonstrates order rather than randomness within the theological presentation.

---

# 91. The fifth day and divine power

The variety and abundance of life testify to God's creative power.

---

# 92. The fifth day and divine goodness

God's evaluation confirms:

**“it was good.”**

Life is a good gift from the Creator.

---

# 93. The fifth day and divine glory

The diversity of life can direct human attention toward the wisdom and greatness of God.

---

# 94. Psalm 104

Psalm 104 later celebrates God's provision for the creatures of the earth and sea.

Genesis 1 provides the foundational creation framework for this later praise.

---

# 95. Psalm 148

Psalm 148 calls the heavens, sea creatures, and other elements of creation to praise the Lord.

The creatures belong to God's created order and ultimately testify to His glory.

---

# 96. The sea creatures and God's wisdom

The enormous diversity of life in the sea can be understood as a display of God's wisdom.

---

# 97. The winged creatures and God's wisdom

The diversity of flying creatures likewise demonstrates the richness of God's creative work.

---

# 98. The heavens and the waters are filled

Day Five transforms previously established realms into populated habitats.

This is a major step toward the fullness of creation.

---

# 99. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:21 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God created the great creatures of the sea, the living beings that swarm in the waters, and the winged creatures of the sky according to their kinds, and He declared this abundant diversity of animal life good.**

Or more simply:

**God created abundant and diverse animal life according to His ordered design and declared it good.**

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:21

### **“So God created”**

→ God Himself is the Creator of animal life.

### **“The great sea creatures”**

→ Even the largest and most impressive creatures of the sea are God's creatures.

### **“Every living creature that moves”**

→ The waters are filled with diverse living beings.

### **“According to their kinds”**

→ Creation possesses ordered diversity.

### **“Every winged bird according to its kind”**

→ The aerial realm is likewise filled with diverse flying creatures.

### **“And God saw that it was good”**

→ God approves the animal life He has created.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:21 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God is the Creator of life** — animal life originates from God's creative work.
2. **Creation by divine word** — God's command in verse 20 is fulfilled in verse 21.
3. **Divine sovereignty** — God determines the existence and order of living creatures.
4. **Abundance of creation** — the waters swarm with living creatures.
5. **Diversity of creation** — numerous kinds of creatures inhabit the created world.
6. **Order within diversity** — creatures exist according to their kinds.
7. **Goodness of animal life** — God declares the created animals good.
8. **Goodness of the physical world** — the sea, air, and living creatures belong to God's good creation.
9. **Creator-creature distinction** — even the great sea creatures remain creatures.
10. **Rejection of creature worship** — created beings are not divine.
11. **Divine wisdom** — creatures correspond appropriately to their environments.
12. **Divine generosity** — God creates life abundantly.
13. **Divine providence** — God prepares habitats for the creatures He creates.
14. **Fruitfulness** — the abundance of Day Five anticipates God's blessing to multiply.
15. **Human dependence** — humanity enters an already populated world.
16. **Human stewardship** — later human dominion is exercised over God's creatures.
17. **Value of animal life** — animals have creaturely significance because they belong to God's good creation.
18. **No cosmic dualism** — the sea and its creatures are not rival powers to God.
19. **Monotheism** — one God creates the entire living world.
20. **Christological creation** — the New Testament teaches that all things were created through Christ.
21. **God's glory** — the diversity and abundance of life display the Creator's wisdom and power.
22. **New creation** — the goodness of the original creation points toward its ultimate renewal.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:21 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD IS THE CREATOR OF ALL LIFE

**“So God created…”**

Every living creature ultimately owes its existence to God.

---

## II. GOD CREATES WITH ORDER AND ABUNDANCE

**“According to their kinds…”**

**“With which the waters swarm…”**

God's creation contains both remarkable diversity and meaningful order.

---

## III. GOD DECLARES HIS LIVING CREATION GOOD

**“And God saw that it was good.”**

Animal life is part of the good world God originally created.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:21 teaches that God sovereignly created the great creatures of the sea, the living creatures that swarm in the waters, and the winged creatures according to their kinds, filling the created realms with abundant and ordered life, and He declared His work good.**

The progression from Genesis 1:20 through Genesis 1:21 is:

**Genesis 1:20**

God commands:

**waters → swarm with living creatures**

**birds → fly above the earth**

**Genesis 1:21**

God fulfills the command:

**great sea creatures → created**

**living aquatic creatures → created**

**winged creatures → created**

**according to their kinds → ordered diversity**

Then God evaluates the result:

**“it was good.”**

The pattern is:

**God speaks.**

**God creates.**

**God fills the realms.**

**God establishes diversity.**

**God establishes order.**

**God sees.**

**God declares it good.**
