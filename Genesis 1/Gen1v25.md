# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:25

**Genesis 1:25 —** “And God made the beasts of the earth according to their kinds and the livestock according to their kinds, and everything that creeps on the ground according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיַּעַשׂ אֱלֹהִים אֶת־חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ לְמִינָהּ וְאֶת־הַבְּהֵמָה לְמִינָהּ וְאֵת כָּל־רֶמֶשׂ הָאֲדָמָה לְמִינֵהוּ וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים כִּי־טוֹב׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyaʿaś ʾĕlōhîm ʾeṯ-ḥayyat hāʾāreṣ ləmînâ wəʾeṯ-habbəhēmāh ləmînâ wəʾēṯ kol-remeś hāʾădāmâ ləmînēhû wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm kî-ṭôḇ*

---

## Literal Rendering

**“And God made the beast of the earth according to its kind, and the livestock according to its kind, and every creeping thing of the ground according to its kind. And God saw that it was good.”**

---

# 1. “And God made” — וַיַּעַשׂ אֱלֹהִים (*wayyaʿaś ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:25 begins with a direct statement of divine action:

**“And God made…”**

The Hebrew verb:

**עָשָׂה (*ʿāśâ*)**

means:

**“to make”**

**“to do”**

or

**“to accomplish.”**

The text explicitly identifies God as the maker of the terrestrial animals.

---

# 2. God Is the Creator of Land Animals

Verse 24 presented God's command:

**“Let the earth bring forth living creatures…”**

Verse 25 now emphasizes God's action:

**“And God made…”**

The earth is the commanded means, but God remains the ultimate Creator.

---

# 3. Divine Command and Divine Action

The relationship between verses 24 and 25 is important.

### Genesis 1:24

**God commands.**

### Genesis 1:25

**God makes.**

This reinforces the sovereignty of God over terrestrial creation.

---

# 4. “The beasts of the earth” — חַיַּת הָאָרֶץ (*ḥayyat hāʾāreṣ*)

The phrase refers broadly to:

**wild animals**

or

**beasts of the earth.**

The Hebrew word:

**חַיָּה (*ḥayyāh*)**

is related to the concept of living things or animals.

---

# 5. “According to their kinds” — לְמִינָהּ (*ləmînâ*)

Once again Genesis emphasizes:

**“according to their kinds.”**

This phrase occurs repeatedly in the creation account.

---

# 6. Ordered Diversity

The animal kingdom contains remarkable diversity.

Genesis nevertheless presents that diversity as ordered according to God's design.

---

# 7. The Repeated Phrase “According to Its Kind”

The phrase occurs with:

**the beasts of the earth**

**the livestock**

**the creeping things.**

This repetition strongly emphasizes the organized character of terrestrial creation.

---

# 8. “And the livestock” — וְאֶת־הַבְּהֵמָה (*wəʾeṯ-habbəhēmāh*)

The second category is:

**“the livestock.”**

The Hebrew:

**בְּהֵמָה (*bəhēmāh*)**

can refer to:

**livestock**

**cattle**

**large animals**

or

**beasts.**

---

# 9. Domestic-Associated Animals

The term can include animals associated with human domestic life.

However, it should not be treated as an exact equivalent of the modern zoological category “domesticated animals.”

---

# 10. “According to Their Kind”

The livestock are also created:

**“according to their kind.”**

The same principle of ordered diversity applies to them.

---

# 11. “Everything That Creeps” — וְאֵת כָּל־רֶמֶשׂ (*wəʾēṯ kol-remeś*)

The third category is:

**“everything that creeps.”**

The Hebrew:

**רֶמֶשׂ (*remeś*)**

refers broadly to creatures that move along or close to the ground.

---

# 12. “On the Ground” — הָאֲדָמָה (*hāʾădāmâ*)

The text specifies:

**“the ground.”**

The Hebrew noun:

**אֲדָמָה (*ʾădāmâ*)**

means:

**“ground”**

**“soil”**

or

**“land.”**

---

# 13. The Terrestrial Realm

Genesis 1:25 therefore describes creatures occupying the terrestrial realm:

**wild animals**

**livestock**

and

**creeping creatures.**

---

# 14. The Threefold Classification

The verse presents three broad categories:

### 1. Beasts of the earth

Wild terrestrial animals.

### 2. Livestock

Larger animals associated with domestic or pastoral life.

### 3. Creeping things

Ground-moving creatures.

---

# 15. Not a Modern Zoological Taxonomy

These categories should not be forced into modern scientific classifications.

The biblical text is describing the terrestrial animal world in its own literary and observational framework.

---

# 16. God Makes the Diversity

The repeated:

**“according to its kind”**

emphasizes that diversity is part of God's creative work.

---

# 17. Diversity Does Not Mean Disorder

Genesis does not portray the animal kingdom as an accidental collection of unrelated creatures.

It is presented as ordered creation.

---

# 18. “And God Saw” — וַיַּרְא אֱלֹהִים (*wayyarʾ ʾĕlōhîm*)

The second major movement of the verse is:

**“And God saw…”**

This introduces God's evaluation of His creative work.

---

# 19. God Evaluates His Creation

The Creator does not merely make the animals.

He evaluates what He has made.

---

# 20. “That It Was Good” — כִּי־טוֹב (*kî-ṭôḇ*)

God declares:

**“it was good.”**

The Hebrew adjective:

**טוֹב (*ṭôḇ*)**

means:

**“good”**

**“pleasant”**

**“fitting”**

or

**“beneficial,”**

depending upon context.

---

# 21. Divine Approval

In this context, **“good”** communicates God's positive evaluation of His created work.

The terrestrial animal world conforms to His creative purpose.

---

# 22. The Goodness of Animal Creation

Genesis explicitly identifies the land animals as good.

This is important for biblical theology.

Animal life is part of God's good creation.

---

# 23. The Goodness of Wild Animals

The text includes:

**“beasts of the earth.”**

Even wild animals are included within God's declaration of goodness.

---

# 24. The Goodness of Livestock

Livestock are likewise included within God's good creation.

---

# 25. The Goodness of Creeping Things

Even small ground-moving creatures are included in the divine evaluation.

---

# 26. No Category Is Excluded

The verse deliberately encompasses multiple categories of terrestrial life.

God sees the whole work as good.

---

# 27. Goodness Comes From the Creator

The goodness of creation is not merely a human judgment.

Genesis specifically says:

**“God saw…”**

and

**“it was good.”**

---

# 28. Divine Evaluation Is Authoritative

God's evaluation establishes the theological character of the created order.

---

# 29. Creation Is Not Evil

Genesis 1:25 directly contradicts any worldview in which physical creation is inherently evil.

The animals of the earth are declared good.

---

# 30. Against Creation Dualism

The physical world is not presented as a prison from which spiritual beings must escape.

God's material creation is good.

---

# 31. Animal Life Has Created Goodness

Animals possess goodness because God created them according to His purpose and declared His work good.

---

# 32. Creation Is Distinct From the Creator

Although creation is good, it is not God.

God makes the animals.

The animals remain creatures.

---

# 33. The Creator-Creature Distinction

Genesis maintains a clear distinction:

**God → Creator**

**animals → creatures**

**earth → created realm**

---

# 34. God's Sovereignty

God determines:

**what creatures exist**

**where they belong**

**how they are ordered**

and

**whether His creation is good.**

---

# 35. The Earth Is Not Autonomous

The earth produces according to God's command.

God remains sovereign over the process.

---

# 36. God's Word and God's Work

Genesis 1:24–25 provides a useful sequence:

**God speaks**

→

**the earth brings forth**

→

**God makes**

→

**God evaluates.**

---

# 37. Divine Speech Produces Divine Action

The command in verse 24 is followed by fulfillment in verse 25.

---

# 38. “It Was So” and “God Made”

Verse 24 concludes:

**“And it was so.”**

Verse 25 explains:

**“And God made…”**

Together these statements emphasize the effectiveness of God's command.

---

# 39. God's Word Is Effective

God does not merely express an intention.

His word accomplishes His purpose.

---

# 40. God's Creation Is Deliberate

The animals are not presented as accidental products of an uncontrolled process.

They are described as belonging to God's purposeful creative activity.

---

# 41. The Kinds of Animals

The phrase:

**“according to their kinds”**

appears three times in the verse's description.

This repetition is deliberate.

---

# 42. Repetition Creates Emphasis

The repeated phrase draws attention to the ordered nature of God's animal creation.

---

# 43. Diversity and Order

Genesis therefore presents two truths together:

**great diversity**

and

**created order.**

---

# 44. God's Wisdom

The existence of distinct kinds demonstrates the wisdom and intentionality of God's creative work.

---

# 45. God's Power

The sheer variety of terrestrial life demonstrates God's creative power.

---

# 46. God's Goodness

The concluding evaluation demonstrates God's goodness.

---

# 47. God's Sovereignty, Wisdom, and Goodness

Genesis 1:25 therefore reveals three major attributes of God:

**Sovereignty**

→ He commands and makes.

**Wisdom**

→ He creates ordered diversity.

**Goodness**

→ He declares the creation good.

---

# 48. The Pattern of Genesis 1

Verse 25 follows the recurring creation pattern:

**God speaks**

→

**creation occurs**

→

**God sees**

→

**God declares goodness.**

---

# 49. Day Six

Genesis 1:25 belongs to the sixth day.

This is the day in which God creates:

**land animals**

and

**humanity.**

---

# 50. Humanity Is About to Appear

The verse immediately precedes the creation of humanity.

The narrative is approaching its climax.

---

# 51. Animals Before Humanity

Land animals are created before human beings.

This establishes the environment into which humanity will enter.

---

# 52. A Prepared World

Humanity will not enter an empty world.

It will enter a world already populated with:

**vegetation**

**birds**

**sea creatures**

and

**land animals.**

---

# 53. Humanity's Future Dominion

The creation of terrestrial animals prepares for the later command concerning human dominion.

---

# 54. Dominion Over God's Creatures

Humanity will exercise authority over creatures that God Himself has declared good.

---

# 55. Dominion Is Not Permission for Destruction

Because the animals are God's good creation, human dominion should be understood as responsible stewardship under God's authority.

---

# 56. The Value of Animals Before Humanity

Animals are declared good before humanity exists.

Therefore their goodness does not depend upon their usefulness to humans.

---

# 57. Creation Has Value in Relation to God

The primary evaluation is:

**“God saw that it was good.”**

The animals possess created goodness because they fulfill God's purpose.

---

# 58. The Earth Is Rich With Life

The creation of three broad categories of terrestrial creatures demonstrates the increasing fullness of the earth.

---

# 59. From Empty Land to Living Land

The land established earlier is now populated by living creatures.

---

# 60. The Forming-and-Filling Pattern

Day Three:

**land is established.**

Day Six:

**land is filled with animals.**

---

# 61. The Land Is Being Filled

Genesis 1:25 represents another step toward the fullness of creation.

---

# 62. Creation Is Progressing

The sequence moves from:

**environment**

to

**vegetation**

to

**animal life**

to

**humanity.**

---

# 63. God's Provision

The creation of animals contributes to the richness and functioning of the world humanity will inhabit.

---

# 64. Animal Life Is Part of God's Provision

The animal kingdom is not an afterthought.

It is part of the deliberate order of creation.

---

# 65. The Goodness of the Physical World

Genesis 1:25 strongly affirms the goodness of material creation.

The physical bodies of animals belong to God's good work.

---

# 66. Creation Is Not Spiritually Inferior

The biblical narrative does not teach that material existence is inherently inferior or evil.

---

# 67. God's Good Creation and the Fall

The declaration of goodness occurs before Genesis 3.

The later entrance of sin should therefore be understood against the background of an originally good creation.

---

# 68. The Fall Is Not the Original State

Genesis 1:25 establishes that the created order was originally good.

Corruption and death are not presented as the defining original character of creation.

---

# 69. The Goodness of God's Design

The animals are created according to their kinds and declared good.

The created order therefore reflects divine purpose.

---

# 70. Creation and God's Glory

The diversity and order of animal life provide reasons to recognize God's wisdom and power.

---

# 71. God's Creativity

The multitude of animal forms reveals the richness of God's creative work.

---

# 72. The Beauty of Diversity

Genesis presents diversity within creation as part of its goodness.

---

# 73. God's Wisdom in Variety

Different animals occupy different ecological and behavioral roles.

Genesis summarizes this diversity through its categories and kinds.

---

# 74. Creation Is Comprehensive

The verse does not focus on one preferred type of animal.

It includes:

**wild**

**domestic-associated**

and

**ground-moving**

creatures.

---

# 75. God Creates What Humans May Consider Ordinary

Livestock and creeping creatures are included alongside wild beasts.

All are part of God's creative work.

---

# 76. No Creature Is Outside God's Creative Authority

The entire terrestrial animal world belongs to God's creation.

---

# 77. God's Evaluation Covers the Whole

The verse concludes with:

**“God saw that it was good.”**

The evaluation follows the complete description of the terrestrial animals.

---

# 78. The Goodness Is Comprehensive

The statement applies broadly to the terrestrial animal creation described in the verse.

---

# 79. Creation and Classification

The categories are not primarily scientific.

They communicate an ordered and understandable created world.

---

# 80. Biblical Observation of Nature

Genesis speaks about animals according to recognizable distinctions within ordinary human observation.

---

# 81. The Purpose of “Kinds”

The repeated concept of kinds emphasizes continuity and distinction within animal life.

---

# 82. The Verse Does Not Explain Biological Mechanisms

Genesis 1:25 focuses on:

**who created**

**what was created**

**how it was ordered**

and

**how God evaluated it.**

It does not attempt to provide a technical biological explanation.

---

# 83. The Main Concern Is Theological

The central question is not:

**“What modern scientific taxonomy corresponds to each category?”**

The central message is:

**God made the terrestrial animals according to His ordered design, and He declared them good.**

---

# 84. Divine Purpose in Creation

Every category exists within God's creative purpose.

---

# 85. Creaturely Identity

The phrase:

**“according to their kinds”**

affirms distinctions among creatures.

---

# 86. The Unity of Animal Creation

Although creatures differ greatly, they all belong to the same created order.

---

# 87. The Diversity of Animal Creation

At the same time, Genesis refuses to collapse all animals into one undifferentiated category.

There are different kinds and categories.

---

# 88. Unity Within Diversity

This is a recurring feature of biblical creation theology:

**unity**

within

**diversity.**

---

# 89. God's Order Is Good

The ordered distinctions within creation are not presented as defects.

They are part of what God declares good.

---

# 90. God's Evaluation Is Final

Human beings do not determine whether the creation is ultimately good.

God evaluates His own work.

---

# 91. The Creator Knows His Creation

The phrase:

**“God saw”**

communicates divine awareness and evaluation.

---

# 92. God's Knowledge of Creation

The Creator is intimately aware of what He has made.

---

# 93. Creation Is Under Divine Observation

Nothing in the created order is outside God's knowledge.

---

# 94. God's Approval Precedes Humanity

Before humanity appears, God has already evaluated the animal creation as good.

---

# 95. Humanity Is Not the Standard of Creation's Goodness

The animals are good before humans can evaluate them.

Their goodness derives from God's creative purpose.

---

# 96. Implications for Stewardship

Human beings should therefore approach animals with recognition that they belong to God's good creation.

---

# 97. Dominion Under God

Later human dominion must remain subordinate to God's ownership and purposes.

---

# 98. Theological Summary

Genesis 1:25 records God's actual creation of the terrestrial animals.

The verse identifies three broad categories:

**beasts of the earth**

**livestock**

**creeping things.**

Each is described as existing:

**“according to its kind.”**

The verse then gives God's evaluation:

**“And God saw that it was good.”**

The verse therefore emphasizes:

**God as Creator**

**ordered diversity**

**the goodness of animal life**

**the productivity of the earth**

**the effectiveness of God's word**

and

**the preparation of creation for humanity.**

---

# 99. The Central Theological Proposition

If Genesis 1:25 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God made the diverse terrestrial animals according to their kinds and declared the completed work good.**

Or more simply:

**God sovereignly created the land animals in ordered diversity and declared them good.**

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:25

### **“And God made”**

→ God is the ultimate Creator of terrestrial animal life.

### **“The beasts of the earth”**

→ Wild terrestrial animals are included in God's creation.

### **“According to their kinds”**

→ The diversity of creatures exists within God's ordered design.

### **“And the livestock”**

→ Domestic-associated and larger land animals belong to God's creation.

### **“According to their kinds”**

→ These creatures likewise possess ordered distinctions.

### **“Everything that creeps on the ground”**

→ Ground-moving creatures are also included.

### **“According to its kind”**

→ Even these creatures belong to the ordered created realm.

### **“And God saw”**

→ God evaluates His creative work.

### **“That it was good”**

→ God declares the terrestrial animal creation good.

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:25 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **God is the Creator** — terrestrial animal life ultimately comes from God's creative work.
2. **Divine sovereignty** — God determines the existence and order of animal life.
3. **Divine wisdom** — creation contains ordered diversity.
4. **Divine goodness** — God declares His animal creation good.
5. **The goodness of physical creation** — material life is part of God's good creation.
6. **The goodness of animal life** — animals possess created goodness before humanity appears.
7. **The Creator-creature distinction** — animals are God's creatures, not divine beings.
8. **Ordered diversity** — creatures exist according to their kinds.
9. **Unity within creation** — diverse animals belong to one created order.
10. **Creaturely dependence** — animals depend upon God's creative ordering.
11. **The effectiveness of God's word** — God's command in verse 24 is fulfilled.
12. **The productivity of the earth** — the earth brings forth terrestrial life according to God's command.
13. **Divine evaluation** — God Himself evaluates His creation as good.
14. **The original goodness of creation** — this evaluation occurs before the Fall.
15. **Human stewardship** — humanity will later govern creatures that God has declared good.
16. **Delegated dominion** — human authority over animals remains subordinate to the Creator.
17. **The value of creation apart from human usefulness** — animals are good before humans exist.
18. **The forming-and-filling pattern** — the land established earlier is now populated with animals.
19. **Divine preparation** — the earth is increasingly prepared for humanity.
20. **God's glory** — the diversity and goodness of creation display God's wisdom, power, and generosity.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:25 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD IS THE CREATOR OF ALL TERRESTRIAL LIFE

**“And God made…”**

The animals of the earth ultimately exist because God created them.

---

## II. GOD CREATES DIVERSITY WITHIN ORDER

**“According to their kinds…”**

The variety of terrestrial life reflects God's purposeful creative design.

---

## III. GOD DECLARES HIS CREATION GOOD

**“And God saw that it was good.”**

Animal life is part of God's original good creation.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:25 teaches that God created the diverse animals of the earth according to their kinds and declared the terrestrial animal creation good.**

The progression from Genesis 1:24 through Genesis 1:25 is:

**Genesis 1:24**

God speaks:

**“Let the earth bring forth living creatures…”**

The categories are introduced:

**livestock**

**creeping things**

**beasts of the earth**

The command is fulfilled:

**“And it was so.”**

**Genesis 1:25**

God makes:

**beasts of the earth**

**livestock**

**creeping things**

Each is created:

**“according to their kinds.”**

God evaluates:

**“God saw that it was good.”**

The pattern is:

**God commands.**

**The earth responds.**

**God makes.**

**Creation is ordered.**

**God evaluates.**

**God declares it good.**
