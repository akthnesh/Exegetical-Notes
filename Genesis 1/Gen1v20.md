# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 1:20

**Genesis 1:20 —** “And God said, ‘Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures, and let birds fly above the earth across the expanse of the heavens.’”

— ESV

---

## Hebrew Text

**וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים יִשְׁרְצוּ הַמַּיִם שֶׁרֶץ נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה וְעוֹף יְעוֹפֵף עַל־הָאָרֶץ עַל־פְּנֵי רְקִיעַ הַשָּׁמָיִם׃**

---

## Transliteration

*Wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm yišrəṣû hammayim šereṣ nep̄eš ḥayyâ wəʿôp̄ yəʿôp̄ēp̄ ʿal-hāʾāreṣ ʿal-pənê rəqîaʿ haššāmāyim*

---

## Literal rendering

**“And God said, ‘Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures, and let birds fly over the earth across the face of the expanse of the heavens.’”**

---

# 1. “And God said” — וַיֹּאמֶר אֱלֹהִים (*wayyōʾmer ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 1:20 begins with the familiar creation formula:

**“And God said.”**

The Hebrew verb **אָמַר (*ʾāmar*)** means:

**“to say”**

**“to speak”**

or

**“to declare.”**

Once again, creation proceeds according to God's sovereign word.

---

# 2. Creation by divine command

The pattern of Genesis 1 continues:

**God speaks.**

**Creation responds.**

This emphasizes that God's creative authority is expressed through His word.

---

# 3. The transition to Day Five

Genesis 1:19 concluded:

**“the fourth day.”**

Genesis 1:20 begins the work of:

**the fifth day.**

The focus now moves from the heavenly lights to living creatures.

---

# 4. From heavenly lights to living creatures

The fourth day established the heavenly lights.

The fifth day introduces living creatures inhabiting:

**the waters**

and

**the skies.**

The creation account continues its ordered progression.

---

# 5. The waters — הַמַּיִם (*hammayim*)

God says:

**“Let the waters swarm…”**

The Hebrew word:

**מַיִם (*mayim*)**

means:

**“waters.”**

The waters that were established and ordered earlier in Genesis now become the realm filled with living creatures.

---

# 6. The waters were already prepared

On Day Two, God separated the waters and established the expanse.

On Day Five, those ordered waters become the environment for aquatic life.

Creation is therefore cumulative.

---

# 7. “Let the waters swarm” — יִשְׁרְצוּ (*yišrəṣû*)

The verb:

**שָׁרַץ (*šāraṣ*)**

means:

**“to swarm”**

**“to teem”**

or

**“to multiply abundantly.”**

The command describes an abundance of living creatures.

---

# 8. Abundance

The wording does not portray the waters as receiving only a small number of creatures.

They are commanded to:

**swarm.**

The picture is one of abundance, movement, and biological fullness.

---

# 9. “With swarms” — שֶׁרֶץ (*šereṣ*)

The noun:

**שֶׁרֶץ (*šereṣ*)**

refers to:

**swarming creatures**

or

**teeming creatures.**

The repetition of the verbal and nominal ideas intensifies the sense of abundance.

---

# 10. The language of multiplication

The command anticipates the blessing of multiplication that appears later in Genesis 1:22.

The waters are to become populated with living creatures.

---

# 11. “Living creatures” — נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה (*nepeš ḥayyâ*)

The phrase:

**נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה**

*nepeš ḥayyâ*

literally means:

**“living creature”**

or

**“living being.”**

The Hebrew noun **נֶפֶשׁ (*nepeš*)** can refer to:

**life**

**a living being**

**a creature**

or

**a person/being.**

Its meaning depends upon context.

---

# 12. “Living”

The adjective:

**חַיָּה (*ḥayyâ*)**

comes from the root associated with:

**life**

and

**living.**

The waters are therefore commanded to teem with creatures possessing life.

---

# 13. The emergence of animal life

Genesis 1:20 marks the introduction of animal life into the creation narrative.

Vegetation appeared on Day Three.

Now animal life begins to fill the created realms.

---

# 14. The distinction between vegetation and animals

Genesis presents vegetation before animal life.

Day Three:

**plants and vegetation**

Day Five:

**aquatic creatures and birds**

Day Six:

**land animals and humanity**

This establishes an ordered progression.

---

# 15. The waters become inhabited

The waters are no longer merely a realm established by God.

They become populated with living creatures.

---

# 16. The sky becomes inhabited

The verse also commands:

**“let birds fly above the earth.”**

The sky likewise becomes populated with life.

---

# 17. “And let birds fly” — וְעוֹף יְעוֹפֵף (*wəʿôp̄ yəʿôp̄ēp̄*)

The Hebrew expression is:

**וְעוֹף יְעוֹפֵף**

*wəʿôp̄ yəʿôp̄ēp̄*

Literally:

**“and let flying creatures fly.”**

The noun **עוֹף (*ʿôp̄*)** can refer broadly to:

**birds**

**flying creatures**

or

**winged creatures.**

---

# 18. Flying creatures

The text describes creatures associated with the aerial realm.

Their characteristic movement is:

**flight.**

---

# 19. The repeated root

The noun and verb are closely related:

**עוֹף (*ʿôp̄*)**

**יְעוֹפֵף (*yəʿôp̄ēp̄*)**

This creates a vivid Hebrew expression emphasizing the activity of flying.

---

# 20. “Above the earth” — עַל־הָאָרֶץ (*ʿal-hāʾāreṣ*)

The birds are commanded to fly:

**“above the earth.”**

The Hebrew noun:

**אֶרֶץ (*ʾereṣ*)**

can mean:

**“earth”**

**“land”**

or

**“ground.”**

Here the birds occupy the aerial space above the earth.

---

# 21. The relationship between sky and earth

The birds are associated with the heavens but fly:

**above the earth.**

This connects the aerial realm with the terrestrial realm.

---

# 22. “Across the expanse” — עַל־פְּנֵי רְקִיעַ (*ʿal-pənê rəqîaʿ*)

The birds fly:

**“across the expanse.”**

The Hebrew noun:

**רָקִיעַ (*rāqîaʿ*)**

was introduced in Genesis 1:6.

It refers to:

**the expanse**

or

**firmament.**

---

# 23. The expanse was established on Day Two

God established the expanse on Day Two.

Now the birds inhabit the space associated with it.

This reinforces the forming-and-filling structure of Genesis 1.

---

# 24. Day Two and Day Five

There is a clear correspondence:

### Day Two

God establishes:

**the expanse**

and orders the waters.

### Day Five

God fills:

**the waters**

with aquatic life and

**the expanse**

with flying creatures.

---

# 25. Forming and filling

The pattern can therefore be summarized:

**Day Two → form the realm**

**Day Five → fill the realm**

This is one of the major structural features of Genesis 1.

---

# 26. The waters and the birds

Both major realms associated with Day Two are now populated.

**waters → aquatic creatures**

**expanse → flying creatures**

---

# 27. God's word fills what He has formed

The God who establishes the realms also fills them with appropriate life.

The structure demonstrates intentionality.

---

# 28. The order of life

Genesis presents life appearing in an ordered sequence:

**vegetation**

→

**aquatic creatures**

→

**flying creatures**

→

**land animals**

→

**humanity.**

---

# 29. The first explicit animal creation command

Genesis 1:20 is the first command specifically directing the waters to produce living animal life.

This marks a major development in the creation narrative.

---

# 30. Animal life belongs to God's creation

Aquatic creatures and birds do not appear independently of God.

They exist because God commands their creation.

---

# 31. Life originates under God's authority

The verse attributes the arrival of living creatures to God's command.

The ultimate source of life is therefore God.

---

# 32. God's sovereignty over life

God commands the waters to teem with living creatures.

This presents life as something governed by divine authority.

---

# 33. Life is abundant by divine command

The abundance of aquatic life is not presented as accidental.

God commands:

**“Let the waters swarm.”**

---

# 34. Creation is fruitful

The fifth day begins a new stage characterized by:

**abundance**

**movement**

**life**

and

**multiplication.**

---

# 35. The goodness of living creatures

Although the explicit declaration:

**“it was good”**

comes in verse 21, verse 20 establishes the living creatures whose creation will immediately receive divine approval.

---

# 36. The living world is part of God's design

Animal life is not outside God's creative purpose.

It is deliberately included in the created order.

---

# 37. Aquatic diversity

The expression:

**“swarms of living creatures”**

communicates variety and abundance within the waters.

The waters are designed to support extensive living populations.

---

# 38. A populated ocean

The picture is not of an empty ocean.

The waters become a living environment filled with creatures.

---

# 39. A populated sky

Likewise, the expanse above the earth becomes populated with flying creatures.

The created world is progressively filled.

---

# 40. Creation moves toward fullness

Genesis 1 repeatedly moves from:

**emptiness**

toward

**fullness.**

The waters and skies are now being filled with life.

---

# 41. From uninhabited to inhabited

Earlier in Genesis, the earth is described as lacking the fullness that will characterize it later.

The fifth day begins the filling of the created realms with living beings.

---

# 42. The abundance of creation

The command to swarm suggests that God's creation is characterized by extraordinary abundance.

The living world is not minimalist.

---

# 43. Divine generosity

The abundance of living creatures can be understood as an expression of God's generosity in creation.

He does not merely create enough life to exist.

He creates a world rich with life.

---

# 44. God's creative imagination

The diversity implied by:

**“swarms of living creatures”**

and

**“birds”**

reflects the richness of God's creative work.

---

# 45. The distinction between Creator and creation

Even the most magnificent creatures are creatures.

They owe their existence to God's command.

---

# 46. No creature is divine

The animals introduced in this passage are living beings created by God.

They are not gods.

---

# 47. Rejection of creature worship

The passage contributes to the biblical understanding that creatures belong to the created order and therefore should not receive the worship due to God alone.

---

# 48. God's authority over the waters

In ancient thought, waters could symbolize chaos or dangerous forces.

Genesis presents the waters as completely subject to God's command.

---

# 49. The waters obey

God does not struggle with the waters.

He simply commands:

**“Let the waters swarm…”**

The waters become the environment in which life appears.

---

# 50. Divine supremacy over chaos

The waters are not an independent cosmic power.

They are part of the realm governed by God.

---

# 51. The expanse obeys God's purpose

The expanse established on Day Two becomes the realm through which birds fly.

It functions exactly within the order God established.

---

# 52. Creation is functional

The expanse is not merely created as an object.

It provides a realm for flying creatures.

The waters likewise provide a realm for aquatic life.

---

# 53. The earth is prepared for life

By Day Five, the created environment is sufficiently ordered to receive extensive animal life.

---

# 54. God's preparation of habitats

Genesis demonstrates a pattern:

**God establishes environments.**

**God fills those environments.**

This reflects intentional design.

---

# 55. Habitat and creature correspondence

The creatures are associated with appropriate realms:

**water → aquatic creatures**

**air → flying creatures**

Later:

**land → land animals and humanity.**

---

# 56. Creation and ecological order

The passage establishes a basic relationship between living creatures and their environments.

Life occupies the realms God has prepared.

---

# 57. The fifth day and the creation hierarchy

Genesis continues toward increasingly complex forms of creaturely life.

The narrative culminates in humanity as God's image-bearer.

---

# 58. Humanity is not yet created

Genesis 1:20 deliberately stops before humanity.

The fifth day concerns:

**aquatic life**

and

**flying life.**

Humanity belongs to the sixth day.

---

# 59. Humanity's unique position

Although all living creatures are created by God, humanity will receive a unique status as God's image-bearer.

Genesis 1:20 therefore prepares for the later distinction.

---

# 60. Life before humanity

Animal life precedes humanity.

Humanity enters a world already populated by living creatures.

---

# 61. Creation is not centered on human production

Humans do not create the animal kingdom.

God creates it before humanity appears.

---

# 62. Human dependence upon animal life

Humanity will depend upon the broader created environment.

Animals and plants will contribute to the functioning of the ecosystem in which humans live.

---

# 63. Creation and provision

Later Genesis will show humanity receiving plants for food.

The broader created world therefore serves human life while remaining God's creation.

---

# 64. God's word and biological life

Genesis emphasizes God's command rather than explaining biological mechanisms.

The text's primary concern is theological:

**God is the Creator of life.**

---

# 65. Genesis and scientific questions

Genesis 1:20 does not attempt to provide a modern biological classification system.

Its primary purpose is to proclaim God's creative authority and the ordered origin of living creatures.

---

# 66. The scope of “living creatures”

The phrase **נֶפֶשׁ חַיָּה (*nepeš ḥayyâ*)** is broad.

It emphasizes living beings rather than providing a modern taxonomic category.

---

# 67. The scope of “birds”

The term **עוֹף (*ʿôp̄*)** can refer more broadly to flying creatures.

The ESV renders the expression naturally as:

**“birds.”**

The Hebrew wording emphasizes creatures characterized by flight.

---

# 68. The command concerns realms

The verse is organized geographically:

**waters**

and

**air above the earth.**

Each realm receives its corresponding inhabitants.

---

# 69. God's ordered geography

Genesis presents the created world as consisting of distinct environments.

Living creatures occupy their appointed domains.

---

# 70. The relationship to Genesis 1:2

Genesis 1:2 describes the waters in the early unformed creation.

By Genesis 1:20, those waters become a realm filled with life.

The narrative therefore moves from:

**unformed**

toward

**ordered and inhabited.**

---

# 71. The movement from emptiness to fullness

This is a major theme in Genesis 1.

The creation begins without the fullness of life that later appears.

God progressively fills it.

---

# 72. The fifth day as filling

The fifth day is especially significant for the filling pattern.

The waters and expanse receive their living inhabitants.

---

# 73. Divine abundance versus scarcity

The command to swarm communicates abundance rather than scarcity.

God's creation is richly populated.

---

# 74. Life as blessing

The command prepares for God's blessing in Genesis 1:22:

**“Be fruitful and multiply.”**

Life is not merely created.

It is destined for fruitfulness.

---

# 75. The relationship between creation and blessing

Verse 20 establishes the creatures.

Verse 22 blesses them.

The sequence is:

**creation**

→

**blessing**

→

**fruitfulness**

---

# 76. God's blessing follows creation

God first creates the creatures.

Then He blesses them.

This distinction is important.

Blessing does not replace creation.

It follows creation.

---

# 77. Fruitfulness as God's gift

The ability of creatures to multiply is ultimately presented as something granted within God's blessing.

---

# 78. God's generosity toward creatures

The Creator not only makes living creatures.

He provides the conditions under which they can multiply.

---

# 79. Life and multiplication

The fifth day therefore introduces two closely related ideas:

**life**

and

**fruitfulness.**

---

# 80. The fifth day and divine blessing

The abundance commanded in verse 20 anticipates the blessing of verse 22.

God's creative purpose includes the flourishing of living creatures.

---

# 81. The goodness of creaturely life

The later declaration that the creation is good demonstrates that animal life belongs to God's good order.

---

# 82. Animal life has value

Because animals are created by God, their existence has intrinsic creaturely significance.

They are part of God's good creation.

---

# 83. Creation care

The biblical doctrine of creation provides a theological basis for responsible care of the created world.

Animals belong to God before they belong to humanity's sphere of stewardship.

---

# 84. Human dominion is not absolute autonomy

Later humanity receives dominion.

But God remains the Creator and owner of creation.

---

# 85. God's ownership

Psalm 24 later states that the earth belongs to the Lord.

Genesis provides the foundation for that claim.

---

# 86. The living world belongs to God

Aquatic creatures and birds exist because God created them.

Their ultimate ownership therefore belongs to God.

---

# 87. Worship belongs to the Creator

The appropriate response to the richness of animal life is ultimately praise of the Creator rather than worship of creation.

---

# 88. Theological anthropology

Genesis 1:20 helps prepare the distinction between humanity and other creatures.

Animals are living creatures.

Humanity will uniquely bear God's image.

---

# 89. Shared creatureliness

Humans and animals share the reality of being created living beings.

But humanity will receive a unique calling and status.

---

# 90. Humanity's uniqueness

Genesis 1:20 therefore helps establish the background against which Genesis 1:26–28 will reveal humanity's distinctive role.

---

# 91. God's creative command is sufficient

The passage contains no struggle, experimentation, or failure.

God speaks.

Life appears according to His command.

---

# 92. Divine authority and effortless creation

The simplicity of:

**“And God said…”**

emphasizes God's absolute authority.

---

# 93. God's word and reality

What God commands becomes reality.

This is one of the defining patterns of Genesis 1.

---

# 94. The word of God creates order

God's speech establishes not only existence but ordered relationships:

**water and aquatic life**

**expanse and flying creatures.**

---

# 95. The fifth day fits the larger pattern

The sequence is:

**God speaks**

→

**a realm is filled**

→

**life becomes abundant**

→

**God later evaluates the work as good**

→

**the day concludes.**

---

# 96. The fifth day and divine wisdom

The creatures are placed within appropriate environments.

This reflects order and purpose.

---

# 97. The fifth day and divine providence

The habitats prepared earlier become environments capable of supporting abundant life.

This demonstrates God's provision within creation.

---

# 98. The fifth day and divine generosity

The command:

**“Let the waters swarm”**

suggests remarkable abundance.

God's creation is characterized by fullness.

---

# 99. The fifth day and divine power

Creating living creatures demonstrates God's creative power.

Life is not presented as independent from God.

---

# 100. The central theological proposition

If Genesis 1:20 is reduced to its fundamental theological assertion, it could be stated this way:

**God commanded the waters to teem with living creatures and commanded flying creatures to fill the expanse above the earth, thereby beginning the filling of the realms He had previously ordered.**

Or more simply:

**God filled the waters and skies with living creatures by His sovereign word.**

---

# Exegetical Outline

## Genesis 1:20

### **“And God said”**

→ Creation continues through God's sovereign command.

### **“Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures”**

→ God commands the waters to become abundant with living beings.

### **“And let birds fly above the earth”**

→ God commands flying creatures to inhabit the aerial realm.

### **“Across the expanse of the heavens”**

→ The creatures occupy the realm established by God on Day Two.

---

# Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 1:20 establishes or contributes to these major doctrines:

1. **Divine sovereignty** — God commands the creation of animal life.
2. **Creation by divine word** — living creatures arise according to God's command.
3. **God as the source of life** — animal life ultimately originates from God's creative activity.
4. **Abundance of creation** — the waters are commanded to swarm with living creatures.
5. **Divine wisdom** — creatures inhabit appropriate realms.
6. **Ordered creation** — water creatures and flying creatures occupy distinct environments.
7. **Forming and filling** — the realms established earlier are now filled with life.
8. **Goodness of animal life** — living creatures belong to God's good creation.
9. **Divine generosity** — creation is characterized by abundance and fullness.
10. **Fruitfulness** — the command anticipates the blessing to multiply.
11. **Creaturely dependence** — living creatures depend upon God's creative provision.
12. **Creator-creature distinction** — animals are creatures, not gods.
13. **Rejection of creature worship** — living creatures belong to God's creation.
14. **Human uniqueness** — animal life precedes humanity and prepares the context for humanity's distinctive status.
15. **Human stewardship** — later human dominion operates within God's prior ownership of creation.
16. **Divine providence** — God prepares environments appropriate for living creatures.
17. **Divine wisdom and order** — creation progresses according to a coherent structure.
18. **Christological creation** — the New Testament teaches that all things were created through Christ.
19. **God's glory** — the abundance and diversity of life testify to the Creator's wisdom and power.

# A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

If Genesis 1:20 were developed into a Bible study or sermon, it could be structured around three propositions:

## I. GOD IS THE SOURCE OF LIFE

**“And God said…”**

Living creatures exist because God commands their creation.

---

## II. GOD FILLS THE REALMS HE HAS PREPARED

**“Let the waters swarm…”**

**“Let birds fly…”**

The realms established earlier are now filled with appropriate life.

---

## III. GOD CREATES WITH ABUNDANCE

**“Let the waters swarm with swarms of living creatures…”**

The fifth day introduces a creation characterized by fullness, diversity, and fruitfulness.

---

# Main Proposition

**Genesis 1:20 teaches that God sovereignly creates abundant animal life by His word, filling the waters and the expanse with living creatures according to the ordered environments He had already established.**

The progression from Genesis 1:18 through Genesis 1:20 is:

**Genesis 1:18**

The heavenly lights are appointed to:

**rule the day**

**rule the night**

and

**separate light from darkness.**

God declares the arrangement:

**“good.”**

**Genesis 1:19**

The fourth day concludes:

**“And there was evening and there was morning, the fourth day.”**

**Genesis 1:20**

The fifth day begins:

**“And God said…”**

God commands:

**waters → swarm with living creatures**

**expanse → filled with flying creatures**

The pattern is:

**God orders the realms.**

**God fills the realms.**

**God creates life.**

**God creates abundantly.**

**God prepares creation for the living creatures that will inhabit it.**
