# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 2:21

## Genesis 2:21

> **“So the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and while he slept took one of his ribs and closed up its place with flesh.”**

— ESV

## Hebrew Text

**וַיַּפֵּל יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים תַּרְדֵּמָה עַל־הָאָדָם וַיִּישָׁן וַיִּקַּח אַחַת מִצַּלְעֹתָיו וַיִּסְגֹּר בָּשָׂר תַּחְתֶּנָּה**

## Transliteration

*Vayyappēl YHWH ʾĕlōhîm tardēmāh ʿal-hāʾādām; vayyîšān; vayyikkaḥ ʾaḥat miṣṣalʿōtāyw; vayyisgōr bāśār taḥtennāh.*

## Literal Rendering

**“And the LORD God caused a deep sleep to fall upon the man, and he slept; and He took one from his sides/ribs, and He closed up flesh in its place.”**

---

## 1. The Verse in Its Immediate Context

Genesis 2:21 marks the decisive transition from Adam's recognized need to God's provision. In Genesis 2:18 the LORD God declared that it was not good for the man to be alone and promised to make a helper corresponding to him. In verses 19–20 the animals were brought before Adam and named, but none was found to correspond to him. Now God Himself acts.

The initiative belongs entirely to God. Adam does not create the woman, discover her, or devise a solution to his solitude. The LORD God causes Adam to enter a deep sleep, takes from him, closes the wound, and then forms the woman in verse 22.

The verse is therefore foundational for biblical teaching concerning the origin of woman, the unity of man and woman, marriage, divine providence, and the goodness of God's creational design.

---

## 2. “The LORD God”

The divine name remains significant.

The Hebrew text uses **YHWH Elohim**, the LORD God.

The Creator who acted in Genesis 1 continues to act personally and covenantally in Genesis 2.

God is not distant from Adam's need.

He observes.

He speaks.

He brings.

He forms.

He provides.

The creation of woman is therefore an intentional divine act.

---

## 3. “Caused a Deep Sleep”

The Hebrew noun **תַּרְדֵּמָה (*tardēmāh*)** refers to a deep sleep.

This is more than ordinary nighttime rest.

It describes a divinely induced condition in which Adam is passive while God performs the operation.

The text emphasizes that the creation of woman is God's work.

Adam contributes nothing to the procedure.

---

## 4. Divine Initiative and Human Passivity

The contrast with Adam's earlier activity is striking.

In verse 20 Adam actively names the animals.

In verse 21 Adam becomes passive.

God causes him to sleep.

God takes.

God closes.

The change in activity communicates something important.

The woman is not produced through Adam's work.

She is God's gift.

---

## 5. The Deep Sleep in Biblical Theology

The term *tardēmāh* occurs elsewhere in the Old Testament in contexts involving unusual divine activity.

Deep sleep can accompany a profound revelation or divine intervention.

The use of this term here gives the scene a solemn and mysterious character.

God is doing something that Adam cannot observe directly.

The creation of the woman originates in God's hidden work.

---

## 6. “Upon the Man”

The Hebrew text again uses *hāʾādām*, “the man.”

The man is the recipient of God's action.

The verse does not portray Adam as initiating the creation of his counterpart.

Instead, God acts upon him.

This reinforces the divine origin of the woman.

---

## 7. “And He Slept”

The brief statement that Adam slept emphasizes his complete dependence upon God.

He cannot assist.

He cannot supervise.

He cannot control the process.

The creation of the woman is entirely God's work.

The narrative therefore presents the woman as God's provision rather than Adam's achievement.

---

## 8. “Took One of His…”

The Hebrew verb **לָקַח (*lāqaḥ*)**, “to take,” describes God's action.

God takes something from Adam.

The precise noun that follows is important because it has generated considerable discussion.

The ESV translates the word as **“rib.”**

The Hebrew term is **צֵלָע (*ṣēlāʿ*)**.

---

## 9. The Meaning of *Ṣēlāʿ*

The noun *ṣēlāʿ* can refer to a “side,” “side portion,” or “rib,” depending upon context.

It is used elsewhere in the Old Testament for the side of a structure or object.

In Genesis 2:21, “rib” is a traditional and legitimate translation.

However, the broader lexical range allows the phrase to be understood as something taken from Adam's side.

The theological point is not that the woman is biologically reducible to a single bone, but that she is derived from the man in a profound way.

---

## 10. “One of His Ribs”

The ESV's “one of his ribs” communicates the traditional interpretation.

The important narrative point is singularity.

God takes one portion from Adam.

The woman is then formed from what God takes.

This establishes a profound relationship between the origin of the woman and the identity of the man.

Adam will immediately recognize this relationship in verse 23.

---

## 11. The Unity of Man and Woman

The creation of the woman from Adam establishes unity.

She is not created independently from unrelated material.

She comes from Adam.

This prepares for Adam's declaration:

**“This at last is bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh.”**

The woman's origin from Adam explains why the man recognizes her as corresponding to him.

She is of his own human nature.

---

## 12. Unity Without Identity

The woman is derived from Adam, but she is not Adam.

She remains a distinct person.

The passage therefore establishes unity without collapsing personal distinction.

This becomes foundational to biblical marriage.

The man and woman are distinct persons who become one flesh.

---

## 13. “Closed Up Its Place”

The LORD God closes the place from which the *ṣēlāʿ* was taken.

The Hebrew verb **סָגַר (*sāgar*)** means to shut or close.

The narrative emphasizes God's complete control over the process.

God does not merely remove something.

He restores Adam's body.

There is no unfinished wound left by the divine act.

---

## 14. “With Flesh”

The text states that God closes the place with flesh.

This detail emphasizes restoration.

Adam experiences a genuine physical act performed by God, yet God Himself provides the closure.

The woman comes from Adam, but Adam is not left permanently damaged.

The creation of woman is therefore not a destructive act but a creative and providential act.

---

## 15. Theological Significance of Adam's Sleep

Adam's sleep creates an important theological pattern.

The woman comes into existence while Adam is unable to act.

The gift comes entirely from God.

This highlights grace and divine provision.

Adam's role is reception rather than production.

---

## 16. The Woman as God's Gift

Genesis 2:21–22 presents the woman as a divine gift.

Adam's need was real.

God's response is deliberate.

The woman is not an accident of human development within the narrative.

She is God's answer to God's own declaration in verse 18.

The text therefore places marriage within divine providence.

---

## 17. The Woman Is Not an Afterthought

The sequence demonstrates that the woman was part of God's purpose.

God first declared the need.

He then demonstrated that the animals could not meet it.

Finally, He creates the woman.

The narrative progression reveals intention.

The woman belongs to the original creational design.

---

## 18. The Goodness of the Woman's Creation

Genesis 1 repeatedly describes God's creation as good.

Genesis 2:21 continues that good design.

The creation of woman does not represent a concession to sin.

It occurs before the Fall.

Marriage and male-female complementarity therefore belong to God's original good creation.

---

## 19. The Woman and the Image of God

Genesis 1:27 declares that both male and female are created in God's image.

Genesis 2:21 provides the narrative development of that truth.

The woman is not a lower category of human being.

She shares the same human origin and dignity.

Her creation from Adam demonstrates unity rather than inferiority.

---

## 20. Equality and Distinction

The text supports a careful distinction between equality and role.

Adam and the woman are equally human.

They share the same created dignity.

Yet they are distinguished as male and female.

The biblical concept is not equality through erasing distinction.

It is unity and dignity within distinction.

---

## 21. The Foundation for “One Flesh”

The woman's physical derivation from Adam anticipates the statement of Genesis 2:24.

The one who came from the man becomes the one with whom the man is united.

There is therefore a profound movement:

**from one flesh, two persons; to two persons, one flesh.**

The woman comes from Adam's body, and marriage establishes a new one-flesh union.

---

## 22. Marriage as Reunification

Genesis 2:21–24 provides a profound theological picture.

God takes from Adam.

God forms the woman.

Adam recognizes her as his own flesh.

The man leaves father and mother and holds fast to his wife.

The two become one flesh.

Marriage can therefore be understood as a God-designed union in which two distinct persons become one covenantal and bodily unit.

---

## 23. The Significance of the Side

The traditional imagery of the woman being taken from Adam's side has often been used devotionally to describe her as being beside him rather than above or beneath him.

While such language should not be treated as the lexical meaning of *ṣēlāʿ*, it captures a legitimate theological truth present in the passage: the woman corresponds to the man and shares his human nature.

The text itself emphasizes correspondence rather than inferiority.

---

## 24. The Woman as Counterpart

Genesis 2:21 should always be read with verse 18.

God promised a helper corresponding to Adam.

The extraction from Adam's side prepares for the creation of precisely such a counterpart.

She is neither another animal nor an unrelated creature.

She is the human counterpart for whom Adam was waiting.

---

## 25. The Mystery of Divine Formation

The verse describes a mysterious divine operation without giving technical biological details.

Genesis is theological narrative, not a surgical textbook.

The emphasis is on what God did and what that action means.

God formed the woman from the man.

The exact biological mechanism is secondary to the theological claim.

---

## 26. Theological Implication: God's Sovereignty

God is sovereign over human relationships.

Adam's marriage is not merely the product of human desire.

God Himself provides the woman.

This demonstrates that marriage belongs to God's creational ordering.

---

## 27. Theological Implication: Providence

Providence is visible in the details.

Adam's solitude is not ignored.

The animals are brought.

The inadequacy becomes evident.

Adam sleeps.

God acts.

The woman is formed.

The entire sequence is ordered toward God's purpose.

---

## 28. Theological Implication: Human Dependence

Adam's sleep is a picture of dependence.

The man cannot create his own solution.

He must receive.

The biblical account therefore challenges the idea that human beings are self-sufficient.

God provides what Adam cannot provide for himself.

---

## 29. Theological Implication: Grace

Although the term “grace” is not explicitly used in Genesis 2:21, the theological pattern is gracious.

Adam does not earn the woman.

He does not manufacture her.

God gives.

The relationship is received as a divine provision.

This anticipates the broader biblical pattern in which God's gifts are received rather than earned.

---

## 30. Theological Implication: Human Relationality

Human beings are created for relationship.

Adam's solitude is declared “not good.”

The woman is God's solution.

Relational existence therefore belongs to the creational design.

Human beings are not merely individual biological units.

They are relational persons.

---

## 31. Theological Implication: Marriage Before the Fall

Marriage is established before sin enters the world.

This is important for biblical theology.

Marriage is not a temporary remedy for the Fall.

It belongs to creation itself.

The Fall damages marriage, but it does not create marriage.

---

## 32. Theological Implication: Sexual Complementarity

The woman is created as a female counterpart to the male.

The later one-flesh union assumes bodily complementarity.

Sexual differentiation is therefore presented as part of God's original design.

It is neither accidental nor introduced by sin.

---

## 33. Interpretative Challenge: Rib or Side?

The most discussed lexical question concerns *ṣēlāʿ*.

“Rib” is a longstanding translation and fits the immediate context.

“Side” reflects the word's wider Old Testament usage.

The safest theological conclusion is that God took a portion or side of Adam's body from which He formed the woman.

The doctrine does not depend upon deciding whether modern anatomical terminology would call it precisely a rib.

---

## 34. Interpretative Challenge: Literal or Symbolic?

The narrative presents the event as a real divine creative act.

The text does not signal that it is merely symbolic.

At the same time, the theological meaning is greater than the physical procedure.

The physical origin of the woman communicates her unity with Adam and prepares for the one-flesh theology of marriage.

---

## 35. Interpretative Challenge: Did Adam Literally Undergo Surgery?

The text describes an extraordinary divine act using ordinary physical language.

The important claim is that God caused deep sleep, took from Adam, and closed the place.

The narrative does not provide enough information to reconstruct the mechanics.

Speculation beyond the text is unnecessary.

---

## 36. Interpretative Challenge: Does the Verse Imply Inferiority?

No.

The woman's derivation from Adam does not imply lesser dignity.

The immediate purpose is to establish correspondence.

Genesis 1 already affirms that male and female bear God's image.

The woman is therefore fully human and fully dignified.

---

## 37. Interpretative Challenge: Is the Woman Created from “Bone” or “Side”?

The ESV's “rib” reflects one traditional understanding.

The Hebrew *ṣēlāʿ* can mean side.

The central theological point remains the same: the woman is derived from Adam and therefore profoundly corresponds to him.

This is confirmed by Adam's response in verse 23.

---

## 38. Adam's Sleep and the Pattern of Revelation

Adam does not see the creation process.

He wakes to God's completed work.

This reinforces the distinction between divine action and human discovery.

God creates realities that humans receive and later recognize.

Adam will recognize the woman after God brings her to him.

---

## 39. Christological Significance: Adam as a Type

The creation of the woman from Adam provides imagery that later Christian interpretation has connected with Christ and the church.

The New Testament explicitly develops Adam-Christ typology.

Adam is the head of the original human order.

Christ is the head of the redeemed order.

The correspondence is not exact at every point, but the canonical pattern is significant.

---

## 40. Christ and His Church

Paul uses Genesis 2:24 in Ephesians 5 to describe the relationship between Christ and the church.

Marriage becomes a profound mystery pointing beyond itself.

The husband-wife relationship is used to illustrate Christ's covenantal love for His people.

Thus Genesis 2:21 belongs to the foundation of a major New Testament theological image.

---

## 41. The Side of Christ

Christian tradition has sometimes drawn a further typological connection between Adam's side being opened and Christ's side being pierced at the crucifixion.

This can be a meaningful theological meditation, but it should be distinguished from the direct meaning of Genesis 2:21.

Genesis 2 itself concerns the creation of woman.

The Christological connection is typological and canonical rather than an explicit prediction contained in the verse.

---

## 42. Adam's Wound and Christ's Sacrifice

The contrast is theologically rich.

Adam's side is opened as God creates the woman.

Christ's side is pierced in His suffering and death.

The first event leads to the formation of the first human marriage.

The second belongs to the accomplishment of redemption and the formation of Christ's redeemed people.

This should be treated as theological typology rather than as a claim that Genesis 2:21 directly predicts the crucifixion.

---

## 43. The Bride Theme

The Bible develops a recurring bride theme.

Israel is sometimes portrayed through marital imagery.

The church is presented as the bride of Christ.

The final biblical vision includes the marriage supper of the Lamb.

Genesis 2 provides one of the foundational scenes for this biblical imagery.

---

## 44. Theological and Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 2:21 teaches that God is the author of human marriage, that woman shares man's human nature and dignity, that male-female complementarity belongs to creation, that human beings are relational, and that God's provision is sovereign and purposeful.

The verse also establishes the physical and theological unity that will culminate in the one-flesh principle.

---

## 45. Implications for the Doctrine of Creation

Creation is not random.

God forms with purpose.

The creation of woman demonstrates divine intentionality.

Human relationships therefore belong within God's created order rather than being treated as merely arbitrary social constructs.

---

## 46. Implications for the Doctrine of Marriage

Marriage begins with God's creative act.

It is therefore fundamentally covenantal and creational.

The woman is God's provision for Adam.

The one-flesh union that follows is rooted in this divine creation.

Marriage is consequently more than companionship; it is a God-ordained union between man and woman.

---

## 47. Implications for the Dignity of Women

The woman comes from the man, but this does not make her less human.

On the contrary, her derivation establishes her intimate correspondence with him.

She shares his flesh.

She shares his humanity.

She will bear God's image.

The passage therefore provides a theological foundation for affirming the full human dignity of woman.

---

## 48. Implications for Human Dependence

Adam's deep sleep illustrates that some of God's greatest gifts are received rather than produced.

Human beings are dependent upon God.

The woman is given.

Life itself is given.

Creation itself is given.

Redemption, in the later biblical story, is likewise ultimately God's gift.

---

## 49. Implications for Christian Community

Human relationality begins before the Fall.

Community is therefore not merely a response to sin.

Human beings were designed for fellowship.

Marriage is the first explicit covenantal human relationship in the biblical narrative, but the broader principle of relational existence extends throughout Scripture.

---

## 50. Implications for the Theology of the Body

Genesis 2 presents the body as meaningful.

The woman is formed from Adam's bodily substance.

The later one-flesh union involves actual embodied union.

Biblical anthropology therefore does not treat the body as incidental to personal identity.

The human person is embodied.

Male and female embodiment is significant within God's design.

---

## 51. Implications for the Theology of Work

The woman is created within the same creational setting in which Adam has received his vocation.

She is therefore not created merely as a decorative companion.

She is Adam's corresponding partner within the human calling.

The broader creation mandate is given to humanity as male and female.

---

## 52. Canonical Significance

Genesis 2:21 is foundational for numerous biblical themes.

It contributes to the doctrine of creation, anthropology, marriage, sexuality, covenant, human dignity, and Christ-church typology.

The verse also provides a foundation for later biblical reflections on the unity of husband and wife.

---

## 53. Relation to the Whole Biblical Enterprise

The Bible begins with God creating humanity for fellowship and vocation.

Sin later fractures human relationships.

Marriage becomes subject to conflict and distortion.

Yet God's creational design remains the foundation for biblical teaching.

Christ comes to redeem His people.

The New Testament uses marriage imagery to describe Christ's covenant with the church.

The biblical story ultimately moves toward the marriage supper of the Lamb.

Genesis 2:21 therefore stands near the beginning of a major canonical theme.

---

## 54. Final Exegetical Reflection

Genesis 2:21 is one of the most significant verses in the biblical account of human origins because it records the moment immediately preceding the creation of the woman. Adam has already discovered that no animal corresponds to him. The problem of his solitude remains. Now God Himself provides the answer.

The first feature to notice is the absolute initiative of God. Adam is not searching for a wife and then discovering one. God causes him to sleep. God takes from him. God closes the place. God then forms the woman. The entire movement is initiated and accomplished by God.

Adam's deep sleep emphasizes his inability to participate in the creative act. The woman is therefore not Adam's accomplishment. She is God's gift.

This has profound implications for marriage. Marriage is rooted in divine design rather than merely human invention. Before Adam can make a covenant with the woman, God must first create the woman. Before human beings establish marriage, God establishes the creational basis for marriage.

The Hebrew term *ṣēlāʿ* is traditionally translated “rib,” although “side” is also lexically possible. Whatever precise anatomical meaning is intended, the narrative clearly communicates that the woman comes from Adam. This establishes a profound unity between them.

Adam will recognize this unity in verse 23 when he declares that the woman is “bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh.” The statement would be difficult to explain apart from the preceding divine action. The woman corresponds to Adam because she shares his humanity.

At the same time, the woman remains a distinct person. She is not simply another form of Adam. The narrative establishes unity without erasing distinction.

This is precisely the foundation needed for Genesis 2:24. The man and woman are two persons who become one flesh. The woman comes from the man, and the two will later enter a unique one-flesh union.

The sequence is therefore theologically elegant.

God takes from Adam.

God forms the woman.

Adam recognizes the woman.

The man holds fast to his wife.

The two become one flesh.

The physical origin of the woman therefore prepares for the covenantal and bodily union of marriage.

The verse also has important implications for the dignity of women. Because the woman comes from Adam, she is not presented as an unrelated or inferior species. She shares his human nature. She is his corresponding partner. Genesis 1 has already established that male and female together bear God's image.

The woman's distinction from Adam does not require inequality of human worth.

Instead, Genesis presents equality of human dignity alongside sexual distinction and relational complementarity.

The creation of woman also demonstrates that marriage is part of the original good creation. It does not arise because sin has entered the world. It is not a temporary concession to human weakness. It is part of God's creational design.

The Fall will profoundly damage marriage, but the Fall does not create marriage.

Genesis 2:21 also teaches human dependence. Adam is placed into a deep sleep while God provides what he cannot provide for himself. This is a recurring biblical pattern. Human beings are dependent upon God's provision at every level. Life is given. Creation is given. Companionship is given. Ultimately, redemption is given.

The passage therefore presents divine generosity at the center of human existence.

The verse also contributes to a theology of the body. The body is not incidental. The woman is formed from Adam's bodily substance, and marriage will involve bodily one-flesh union. Human identity is therefore embodied. Male and female embodiment belongs to God's creational purpose.

The Christological significance becomes especially rich when the verse is read canonically. Adam's creation and marriage establish the first major biblical pattern of a man and his bride. The New Testament later uses marriage as an analogy for Christ and the church.

Paul explicitly draws upon Genesis 2:24 in Ephesians 5, identifying the marriage relationship as a profound mystery pointing toward Christ and His church.

Christian tradition has also noticed a typological relationship between Adam's opened side and Christ's pierced side. This should not be confused with the direct meaning of Genesis 2:21, but it can serve as a theological reflection. Adam's side is opened as the woman is brought forth; Christ's side is pierced in His sacrificial death. Adam receives his bride in the beginning; Christ gives Himself for His bride in redemption.

The typological relationship is not the literal interpretation of Genesis 2:21, but the canonical connection illustrates how the biblical story progressively develops the themes introduced in Genesis.

Genesis 2:21 therefore stands at a crucial point in the biblical enterprise. It is about more than a rib.

It is about God's sovereignty.

It is about divine provision.

It is about human dependence.

It is about the unity and distinction of man and woman.

It is about the creational foundation of marriage.

It is about the dignity of women.

It is about embodied human identity.

And within the larger canon, it contributes to the imagery through which Scripture eventually describes Christ and His church.

The verse teaches that Adam could not solve his relational need through the animal creation. God Himself had to provide the corresponding human partner.

And God's provision was not merely external.

He created the woman from Adam himself.

The result would be a relationship characterized by profound unity.

The woman would be bone of his bones and flesh of his flesh.

The two would become one flesh.

Thus the movement of Genesis 2 is from solitude to companionship, from incompleteness to correspondence, from divine promise to divine provision, and from two distinct persons toward covenantal one-flesh union.

The entire process begins with God's initiative.

**Genesis 2:21 therefore presents the creation of woman as a sovereign and gracious act of God in which the LORD causes Adam to sleep, takes from his side, and closes the place with flesh, establishing the profound unity and complementarity of man and woman and laying the creational foundation for marriage, human relationality, embodied identity, and the later biblical picture of Christ and His bride.**
