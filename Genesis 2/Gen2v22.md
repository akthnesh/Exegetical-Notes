# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 2:22

## Genesis 2:22

> **“And the rib that the LORD God had taken from the man he made into a woman and brought her to the man.”**

— ESV

## Hebrew Text

**וַיִּבֶן יְהוָה אֱלֹהִים אֶת־הַצֵּלָע אֲשֶׁר־לָקַח מִן־הָאָדָם לְאִשָּׁה וַיְבִאֶהָ אֶל־הָאָדָם**

## Transliteration

*Vayyiven YHWH ʾĕlōhîm ʾet-haṣṣēlāʿ ʾăšer-lāqaḥ min-hāʾādām ləʾiššāh; vayəviʾehā ʾel-hāʾādām.*

## Literal Rendering

**“And the LORD God built the side/rib that He had taken from the man into a woman, and He brought her to the man.”**

---

## 1. The Verse in Its Immediate Context

Genesis 2:22 is the climax of the divine response to Adam's solitude announced in Genesis 2:18. The preceding verse described the LORD God causing Adam to fall into a deep sleep, taking one of his *ṣēlāʿ*—traditionally rendered “rib”—and closing the place with flesh. Verse 22 now describes what God does with what He has taken: He forms it into a woman and brings her to the man.

The verse is therefore centered on divine action. God is the subject of every major movement. He takes, He builds, and He brings. Adam receives.

This is one of the foundational passages for the biblical doctrine of marriage because the woman is presented not as an independent accident of human existence but as God's intentional provision of the corresponding human partner promised in verse 18.

---

## 2. “The LORD God”

Once again, the text identifies God as **YHWH Elohim**, the LORD God.

The same Creator who made the heavens and the earth is personally involved in the formation of humanity's relational order.

The creation of woman is therefore not outside God's creative purpose.

God Himself establishes the relationship between man and woman.

---

## 3. “He Made”

The ESV says that God “made” the rib into a woman.

The Hebrew verb **בָּנָה (*bānāh*)**, however, literally carries the idea of “building.”

This is an important feature of the verse.

God does not merely discover or uncover the woman.

He builds or fashions her.

The language portrays deliberate craftsmanship.

---

## 4. The Significance of “Built”

The verb *bānāh* is unusual in the creation account.

God “builds” the woman from what He has taken from Adam.

The imagery communicates intentional construction and design.

The woman is carefully fashioned by God as the fitting counterpart to Adam.

The word therefore contributes to the dignity and purposeful nature of her creation.

---

## 5. “The Rib”

The ESV translates *ṣēlāʿ* as “rib.”

As noted in Genesis 2:21, the Hebrew term can also mean “side” or “side portion.”

The precise lexical question should not obscure the central theological point.

God takes something from Adam and fashions it into the woman.

The woman therefore shares an intimate physical and human connection with the man.

---

## 6. “That the LORD God Had Taken”

The verse explicitly connects itself to verse 21.

The woman is formed from the very thing God took from Adam.

This establishes continuity between Adam and the woman.

She does not come from an unrelated source.

The narrative deliberately emphasizes her derivation from Adam.

---

## 7. “From the Man”

The phrase “from the man” is crucial.

The woman comes from Adam.

This explains Adam's recognition of her in verse 23.

He will identify her as “bone of my bones and flesh of my flesh.”

The creation account therefore provides the basis for the profound unity between man and woman.

---

## 8. “Into a Woman”

God forms what He took from Adam into a woman.

The Hebrew noun **אִשָּׁה (*ʾiššāh*)** means “woman” or “wife,” depending on context.

The corresponding relationship between *ʾîš* (“man/husband”) and *ʾiššāh* (“woman/wife”) becomes important in the following verse.

The linguistic relationship reinforces the correspondence between the man and the woman.

---

## 9. The Woman as God's Direct Creation

The woman is directly fashioned by God.

This means that the biblical account gives woman the same divine origin that it gives man.

Adam was formed from the dust of the ground.

The woman is formed from what God takes from Adam.

In both cases, God is the Creator.

Neither human being is self-created.

---

## 10. Different Origin, Same Humanity

The method of creation differs, but the result is the same human nature.

Adam comes from the ground.

The woman comes from Adam.

Both are creatures of God.

Both are human.

Both belong to the image-bearing humanity described in Genesis 1:27.

The difference in origin does not imply a difference in human dignity.

---

## 11. The Woman as the Answer to Genesis 2:18

Genesis 2:18 announced that God would make a helper corresponding to the man.

Genesis 2:20 demonstrated that no animal could fulfill this role.

Genesis 2:21 described the preparation.

Genesis 2:22 now gives the answer.

The woman is the corresponding helper.

The narrative has therefore moved from divine diagnosis to divine provision.

---

## 12. “And Brought Her”

The final major action in the verse is that God **brought her to the man**.

This is significant.

God not only creates the woman.

He personally brings her to Adam.

The first human marriage is therefore introduced by divine presentation.

Adam does not discover the woman independently.

God brings her.

---

## 13. Divine Initiative in Marriage

The action of bringing the woman to Adam emphasizes divine initiative.

Marriage is not introduced merely as a human arrangement.

God creates the woman and brings her to the man.

This establishes the creational foundation of marriage.

The relationship is ultimately grounded in God's design.

---

## 14. The First Marriage Ceremony

The scene has often been understood as the first marriage presentation.

God forms the woman.

God brings her to Adam.

Adam receives her.

Genesis 2:24 then explains the nature of the resulting marital union.

While the text does not provide a later formal wedding ceremony, the basic pattern of divine provision and presentation is unmistakable.

---

## 15. God as the Author of Marriage

Genesis 2:22 provides strong support for understanding marriage as a creational institution.

Marriage is not invented by fallen society.

It appears before sin.

God Himself establishes the man-woman relationship.

Later biblical teaching concerning marriage therefore rests upon this original creational pattern.

---

## 16. The Woman Is God's Gift

The woman is given to Adam.

This does not mean that she is property.

Rather, the narrative presents her as a divine provision.

She is God's answer to Adam's need.

The language of gift must therefore be understood relationally and covenantally, not commercially.

---

## 17. The Woman Is Not an Animal

The previous verses have already demonstrated that the animals do not correspond to Adam.

Verse 22 provides a decisive contrast.

God creates a woman.

She is human.

She corresponds to Adam.

She is capable of the one-flesh relationship described in verse 24.

The distinction between humanity and the animal creation is therefore reinforced.

---

## 18. The Woman Is Not an Afterthought

The woman is not a secondary patch applied to a failed creation.

God had already declared the problem in verse 18.

The entire sequence moves toward her creation.

Her arrival is purposeful.

She belongs to God's original design.

---

## 19. The Woman and the Goodness of Creation

The creation of woman occurs before Genesis 3.

Sin has not yet entered the world.

Therefore woman, marriage, sexual differentiation, and human companionship all belong to the original good creation.

They are not products of the Fall.

The Fall will distort these realities, but it does not create them.

---

## 20. Human Complementarity

The woman corresponds to the man because she shares his humanity while being distinctly female.

This is complementarity.

The two are neither identical nor unrelated.

They are different yet corresponding.

The distinction is essential to the marital union described later in the chapter.

---

## 21. Unity and Distinction

Genesis 2:22 establishes a theological pattern that becomes central to biblical anthropology.

The man and woman are united in human nature.

Yet they remain distinct persons.

They are male and female.

The Bible therefore does not require sameness in order to establish equality of dignity.

Unity and distinction can coexist.

---

## 22. The Dignity of Woman

The manner of the woman's creation should not be interpreted as humiliation.

She is personally fashioned by God.

She is brought by God to Adam.

She becomes the object of Adam's joyful recognition.

The passage therefore gives woman a position of extraordinary significance within the creation account.

---

## 23. The Woman as Image-Bearer

Genesis 1:27 explicitly teaches that both male and female are created in God's image.

Genesis 2:22 should be read in light of that declaration.

The woman is fully part of image-bearing humanity.

Her physical derivation from Adam does not diminish her divine image-bearing dignity.

---

## 24. The Meaning of “Built”

The building language also has an interesting canonical resonance.

The Old Testament frequently uses building language for structures, cities, houses, and other carefully constructed things.

Here God “builds” the woman.

The imagery emphasizes intentionality.

She is not presented as an incidental by-product.

She is purposefully fashioned.

---

## 25. God's Personal Involvement

Genesis 2 is deeply personal in its description of God's activity.

God forms.

God plants.

God brings.

God commands.

God causes sleep.

God takes.

God closes.

God builds.

God brings the woman.

The Creator is intimately involved with the life of His creatures.

---

## 26. Divine Providence and Human Relationship

The verse demonstrates that human relationships exist within divine providence.

Adam's need is known by God.

The woman is created by God.

She is brought by God.

The relationship therefore unfolds under divine supervision.

This does not remove human responsibility, but it places human relationship within God's sovereign ordering.

---

## 27. Adam's Passivity and Reception

Adam remains passive in the creation of the woman.

He was asleep when the material was taken.

God formed the woman.

God brought her.

Adam's role begins with recognition and reception.

This is significant because it emphasizes that the relationship is fundamentally God's provision.

---

## 28. The Woman Is “Brought” to Adam

The verb used for bringing communicates purposeful movement.

God does not merely leave the woman somewhere in Eden.

He brings her to Adam.

This is relational presentation.

The Creator places the two human beings together.

The narrative therefore moves from creation to relationship.

---

## 29. The First Human Encounter

Genesis 2:22 prepares for one of the most beautiful human encounters in Scripture.

Adam has not yet seen the woman.

God brings her.

Verse 23 records Adam's response.

His response is poetic and joyful.

The creation narrative therefore moves from divine craftsmanship to human recognition.

---

## 30. Adam's Recognition in Verse 23

Verse 22 cannot be fully understood apart from verse 23.

God brings the woman to Adam.

Adam declares that she is bone of his bones and flesh of his flesh.

This confirms the significance of the woman's origin.

Adam recognizes the profound correspondence between them.

---

## 31. The Linguistic Connection Between Man and Woman

Genesis 2:23 highlights the relationship between the Hebrew words for “man” and “woman.”

The linguistic wordplay reinforces their relationship.

The woman is not an unrelated human being.

She is the corresponding female counterpart of the man.

Their names reflect their shared identity and distinction.

---

## 32. The Foundation for Genesis 2:24

Verse 22 establishes the origin.

Verse 23 establishes recognition.

Verse 24 establishes the marital principle.

This sequence is important.

The one-flesh union is grounded in the preceding creation of the woman.

Marriage is therefore connected to creation itself.

---

## 33. From One to Two and Two to One

The narrative presents a profound movement.

God takes from one man and forms a woman.

Two distinct persons now exist.

Yet the two will become one flesh.

Thus Genesis provides the foundational structure for biblical marital union.

The relationship is neither absolute individuality nor loss of individuality.

It is covenantal union.

---

## 34. The Body and Personal Identity

Genesis 2:22 shows that bodily identity matters.

The woman is formed from Adam's bodily substance.

Adam's response will identify her according to bone and flesh.

The Bible therefore treats embodiment as meaningful.

Human persons are embodied creatures.

---

## 35. Marriage and Bodily Union

The physical origin of the woman prepares for the physical and covenantal union of marriage.

The one-flesh language of verse 24 is not merely metaphorical.

It concerns an embodied union that expresses covenantal relationship.

Genesis therefore integrates body, personhood, sexuality, and covenant.

---

## 36. Marriage Before the Fall

The woman is created and brought to Adam before Genesis 3.

This means marriage belongs to the original order.

The institution of marriage is therefore fundamentally good.

Later biblical teaching must be understood against this pre-Fall foundation.

---

## 37. Theological Implication: God's Sovereignty

God sovereignly creates the woman.

He determines the manner of her formation.

He brings her to Adam.

The entire event is under divine control.

Marriage therefore begins within God's sovereign creative purpose.

---

## 38. Theological Implication: Divine Wisdom

The woman is exactly the kind of counterpart that Adam could not find among the animals.

The narrative demonstrates divine wisdom.

God knew Adam's need.

God demonstrated the inadequacy of the animal creation.

God provided the appropriate human counterpart.

---

## 39. Theological Implication: Divine Goodness

God's provision is good.

The woman is not a curse.

Marriage is not a punishment.

Companionship is not an indication of creation's failure.

The woman is part of God's good answer to the condition declared in verse 18.

---

## 40. Theological Implication: Human Dependence

Adam receives the woman.

He does not manufacture her.

This emphasizes human dependence upon God.

Even one of humanity's most important relationships is presented as a divine provision.

---

## 41. Theological Implication: Human Relationality

Human beings are designed for relationship.

Adam's solitude was declared “not good.”

God responds by creating another human person.

Relationality is therefore embedded within the creational structure of humanity.

---

## 42. Theological Implication: Equality of Dignity

Because the woman is created by God and shares Adam's humanity, she possesses equal human dignity.

The passage does not portray her as an inferior type of creature.

She is God's image-bearer and Adam's corresponding human partner.

---

## 43. Theological Implication: Sexual Complementarity

The woman is created as woman.

The man is man.

Their bodily and personal distinction belongs to the creational design.

The one-flesh union presupposes this complementarity.

---

## 44. Interpretative Challenge: “Rib” or “Side”?

The ESV follows the traditional rendering “rib.”

The Hebrew *ṣēlāʿ* has a broader range and can mean “side.”

Both observations should be acknowledged.

The essential theological point is that God took a portion from Adam and built the woman from it.

The exact anatomical identification should not become the center of interpretation.

---

## 45. Interpretative Challenge: Does “Built” Mean Manufactured?

The verb *bānāh* should not be reduced to modern manufacturing terminology.

The imagery emphasizes purposeful construction.

God fashions the woman intentionally.

The language is theological and literary as well as descriptive.

---

## 46. Interpretative Challenge: Does Derivation Imply Inferiority?

No.

The woman being derived from Adam does not make her less human.

Indeed, Adam's later response emphasizes their profound unity.

Genesis 1 establishes equal image-bearing dignity.

Genesis 2 develops relational correspondence.

---

## 47. Interpretative Challenge: Is Marriage Merely About Reproduction?

No.

The woman answers Adam's relational solitude.

Marriage includes companionship and correspondence as well as sexual union and procreation.

The full biblical theology of marriage includes covenant, companionship, fidelity, mutual love, and shared vocation.

---

## 48. Interpretative Challenge: Is This Only About Adam and Eve?

The immediate event concerns Adam and the woman who will become Eve.

Yet Genesis 2:24 generalizes the creational pattern to marriage.

Therefore the event has significance beyond the first couple.

It provides a foundational model for subsequent human marriage.

---

## 49. Christological Significance

Genesis 2:22 contributes to the larger biblical theology of bride and bridegroom.

Adam receives the woman whom God brings to him.

Later Scripture develops marriage imagery to describe God's covenant relationship with His people.

The New Testament ultimately uses marriage imagery to describe Christ and the church.

---

## 50. Christ and the Church

Ephesians 5 draws explicitly from Genesis 2:24.

Paul presents marriage as a profound mystery pointing toward Christ and His church.

The husband-wife relationship therefore becomes part of a larger redemptive picture.

Genesis 2:22 provides part of the creational foundation for that imagery.

---

## 51. Adam and Christ

Adam is the first representative man.

Christ is identified in the New Testament as the last Adam.

Adam's creation begins the history of humanity.

Christ's redemptive work establishes the foundation of a redeemed humanity.

The contrast between Adam's original role and Christ's saving work becomes central to biblical theology.

---

## 52. The Bride Motif

The Bible develops the imagery of a bride belonging to a covenant relationship.

The church is ultimately portrayed as Christ's bride.

The final biblical vision culminates in the marriage supper of the Lamb.

Genesis 2 stands at the beginning of this extensive biblical motif.

---

## 53. Adam's Side and Christ's Side

Christian theological tradition has often noticed a typological parallel between Adam's side being opened and Christ's side being pierced.

This is not the direct grammatical meaning of Genesis 2:22.

Nevertheless, within canonical theology, the image can be used carefully as a typological meditation.

Adam's bride is formed through an act involving his side; Christ gives Himself sacrificially for His bride.

---

## 54. Theological and Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 2:22 establishes that the woman is intentionally created by God, shares the man's human nature, corresponds to him as female, and is brought to him as God's provision.

The verse provides a foundation for the doctrines of creation, human dignity, marriage, sexual complementarity, human relationality, divine providence, and the later biblical imagery of Christ and His church.

---

## 55. Implications for Marriage

Marriage is rooted in God's creative purpose.

The first woman is created by God and brought to the man.

This means marriage is fundamentally more than a private human contract.

It is a creational institution ordered by God.

---

## 56. Implications for the Dignity of Women

Woman is directly fashioned by God.

She is not an inferior creature.

She is the corresponding human partner to the man.

Her origin from Adam emphasizes unity of humanity rather than inferiority.

---

## 57. Implications for Human Community

The creation of woman demonstrates that human fellowship belongs to God's good design.

People are not intended to exist in absolute isolation.

Relationships are part of God's purpose for humanity.

---

## 58. Implications for the Theology of the Body

The body is integral to biblical anthropology.

The woman is physically derived from Adam.

The man and woman will become one flesh.

The biblical understanding of marriage therefore involves embodied persons and embodied union.

---

## 59. Implications for Christian Family Theology

The family begins with God's creational ordering of man and woman.

The later biblical household structure grows out of this foundation.

Marriage is therefore not merely a later social institution.

Its roots are found in creation.

---

## 60. Canonical Significance

Genesis 2:22 is foundational for reading later passages concerning marriage and covenant.

It stands behind Genesis 2:24.

It contributes to Jesus' teaching on marriage in the Gospels.

It is explicitly used by Paul in Ephesians 5.

It also contributes to the larger biblical bride motif.

---

## 61. Relation to the Whole Biblical Enterprise

The Bible begins with creation.

Creation establishes the original relationship between God, humanity, and the world.

Sin later fractures those relationships.

Redemption restores what sin has corrupted.

The New Testament presents Christ as the Redeemer and the church as His bride.

The biblical enterprise therefore moves from the creation of the first human pair toward the final redeemed community united to Christ.

Genesis 2:22 stands near the beginning of that trajectory.

---

## 62. Final Exegetical Reflection

Genesis 2:22 is the moment in which God's answer to Adam's solitude becomes visible. The animals have been named, but none corresponds to Adam. The man has been placed into a deep sleep, and God has taken from him. Now God builds what He has taken into a woman and brings her to the man.

The verse is striking because God Himself performs every decisive action.

The LORD God takes.

The LORD God builds.

The LORD God brings.

Adam receives.

This structure teaches that the creation of woman is fundamentally God's work.

The woman is not an afterthought. She is the divinely intended answer to the problem announced in Genesis 2:18. God had declared that it was not good for the man to be alone and that He would make a helper corresponding to him. After demonstrating that the animals could not fulfill this role, God creates the woman.

The use of the verb *bānāh*, “to build,” adds a beautiful dimension to the narrative. God is portrayed as deliberately constructing the woman. The image is one of purposeful craftsmanship. The woman is not an accidental by-product. She is carefully fashioned according to God's design.

The phrase “from the man” also establishes the profound unity between Adam and the woman. She comes from him. This explains Adam's response in verse 23. When God brings the woman to him, Adam immediately recognizes her as corresponding to himself.

The woman is therefore neither another animal nor an unrelated human being.

She is his counterpart.

She is of his own flesh.

She shares his humanity.

Yet she remains a distinct person.

This unity and distinction form the foundation of biblical marriage.

The verse also provides a powerful theological foundation for the dignity of women. The woman is personally fashioned by God. Genesis 1 has already declared that male and female are created in God's image. Genesis 2 does not reverse that declaration. Instead, it gives the image-bearing male-female humanity a relational and covenantal context.

The woman is not less human because she is formed from Adam.

Her derivation establishes her intimate correspondence with him.

The narrative therefore presents equality of human dignity together with sexual distinction.

The woman is female.

Adam is male.

They are distinct.

Yet they belong to one humanity and are designed to correspond to one another.

The final action of the verse is equally significant: God brings the woman to Adam.

This is more than transportation.

It is presentation.

The Creator Himself brings the woman to the man.

The first marriage therefore begins with divine initiative.

God creates the bride.

God brings the bride.

Adam receives the bride.

Genesis 2:24 will then explain the permanence and nature of the resulting union.

This gives marriage a creational foundation.

Marriage is not introduced after the Fall.

It is not a concession to human weakness.

It is not merely a cultural invention.

It belongs to God's original good design.

Sin will later distort marriage, producing conflict, selfishness, domination, and pain.

But those distortions do not define the original purpose of marriage.

Genesis 2:22 presents the original design.

Theologically, the verse also demonstrates divine providence.

God knew Adam's condition.

God announced the need.

God demonstrated the inadequacy of the animals.

God caused Adam to sleep.

God took from Adam.

God built the woman.

God brought her.

The entire sequence is ordered.

Nothing in the narrative suggests divine improvisation.

The woman is God's intentional provision.

This also teaches human dependence.

Adam cannot create his own counterpart.

He must receive what God provides.

His sleep is a striking picture of human inability and divine action.

Adam's greatest relational gift arrives while he is incapable of producing it.

The pattern anticipates a broader biblical theology of God's grace: human beings ultimately depend upon God for what they cannot accomplish for themselves.

The Christological implications are also significant when the verse is read within the whole canon.

The first man and his bride establish the foundational biblical imagery of marriage.

Later Scripture develops the bride motif.

Israel is sometimes portrayed through marital imagery.

The New Testament identifies the church in relation to Christ using bridegroom and bride language.

Ephesians 5 explicitly draws upon Genesis 2:24 and presents marriage as a mystery pointing toward Christ and the church.

Christian tradition has also observed a typological connection between Adam's side and Christ's pierced side. This should be held carefully. Genesis 2:22 is not grammatically predicting the crucifixion. Its immediate meaning concerns the creation of the woman. Yet within the canonical shape of Scripture, the image of a bride connected with the man's side can serve as a meaningful theological pattern.

The comparison becomes especially striking when Adam and Christ are viewed as representative heads.

Adam stands at the beginning of the human story.

Christ comes as the last Adam.

Adam's history leads into the Fall.

Christ's obedience leads toward redemption.

Adam receives the woman God brings to him.

Christ sacrificially loves and gives Himself for His bride.

The typology should not erase the original meaning of Genesis 2:22, but it demonstrates the richness of the biblical storyline.

Ultimately, Genesis 2:22 teaches that God's creation of woman is purposeful, personal, relational, and good.

The woman is built by God.

She comes from the man.

She corresponds to the man.

She is brought to the man.

She will be recognized by the man.

And together they will become one flesh.

The passage therefore provides the foundation for a biblical theology of marriage that begins not with human autonomy but with divine creation.

It teaches that the relationship between man and woman is neither arbitrary nor accidental.

It is embedded within God's design for humanity.

The woman is God's answer to Adam's solitude.

She is not an inferior substitute for the animals.

She is not an afterthought.

She is not merely a reproductive instrument.

She is a human person created by God to correspond to the man.

Thus Genesis 2:22 stands as one of the central creational statements concerning human relationality and marriage.

**Genesis 2:22 therefore reveals the LORD God as the sovereign Creator who intentionally builds the woman from what He took from the man and brings her to Adam as the divinely provided counterpart, establishing the unity and dignity of male and female and laying the creational foundation for marriage, one-flesh union, human relationality, and the later biblical imagery of Christ and His bride.**
