# Exegetical Notes on Genesis 2:3

## Genesis 2:3

> **“So God blessed the seventh day and made it holy, because on it God rested from all his work that he had done in creation.”**

— ESV

## Hebrew Text

**וַיְבָרֶךְ אֱלֹהִים אֶת־יוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי וַיְקַדֵּשׁ אֹתוֹ כִּי בוֹ שָׁבַת מִכָּל־מְלַאכְתּוֹ אֲשֶׁר־בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים לַעֲשׂוֹת**

## Transliteration

*Wayḇāreḵ ʾĕlōhîm ʾet-yôm haššəḇîʿî wayqaddēš ʾōtô kî ḇô šāḇaṯ mikkol-məlāḵtô ʾăšer-bārāʾ ʾĕlōhîm laʿăśôṯ*

## Literal Rendering

**“And God blessed the seventh day and sanctified it, because in it he ceased from all his work which God created to do.”**

---

## 1. “So God blessed” — וַיְבָרֶךְ אֱלֹהִים (*wayḇāreḵ ʾĕlōhîm*)

Genesis 2:3 begins with the statement:

**“So God blessed…”**

The Hebrew verb is **בָּרַךְ (*bāraḵ*)**, meaning **“to bless.”**

This is the third major divine action associated with the seventh day.

God has:

1. finished His work,
2. rested from His work,
3. blessed the seventh day.

The blessing is significant because Genesis has already used the language of blessing in connection with the living creation.

God blessed the sea creatures and birds in Genesis 1:22.

God blessed humanity in Genesis 1:28.

Now God blesses a **day**.

The seventh day is therefore uniquely marked by divine blessing.

The text does not merely say that God rested.

It says that God **blessed the day on which He rested**.

This gives the seventh day a distinctive place within the created order.

---

## 2. “The seventh day” — יוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי (*yôm haššəḇîʿî*)

The object of God's blessing is:

**“the seventh day.”**

The Hebrew expression is:

**יוֹם הַשְּׁבִיעִי (*yôm haššəḇîʿî*)**

The seventh day has already been emphasized in Genesis 2:2.

Now Genesis 2:3 gives the day a further distinction.

It is not merely the seventh chronological day.

It is a day that God **blesses** and **sanctifies**.

The repeated emphasis demonstrates that the creation week culminates in something more than the completion of God's work.

It culminates in a divinely designated day.

The pattern is therefore:

**Six days of divine work → Seventh day of divine rest → Seventh day blessed and sanctified.**

---

## 3. “And made it holy” — וַיְקַדֵּשׁ אֹתוֹ (*wayqaddēš ʾōtô*)

The second divine action is:

**“and made it holy.”**

The Hebrew verb is **קָדַשׁ (*qādaš*)**, meaning **“to make holy,” “to sanctify,” “to set apart as holy.”**

The text therefore teaches that God did not merely bless the seventh day.

He also **sanctified it**.

To sanctify something is to set it apart for a special purpose or status.

The seventh day is therefore distinguished from the other days.

This is the first explicit occurrence in Scripture of God **sanctifying** something.

The concept becomes enormously important throughout the rest of Scripture.

Objects, places, people, and times can be sanctified—set apart for God.

Here, the seventh day is the first thing explicitly identified as having been sanctified by God.

---

## 4. Blessing and holiness

The two verbs in Genesis 2:3 should be distinguished:

**God blessed the seventh day.**

**God sanctified the seventh day.**

Blessing concerns God's favorable designation and purpose for the day.

Sanctification concerns its being set apart as holy.

Together they communicate that the seventh day possesses a unique divine status.

The day is not intrinsically divine.

It is not another deity.

It is not worshiped as a god.

Rather, the day is **created time that God Himself has set apart**.

This preserves the Creator–creation distinction while establishing the theological significance of sacred time.

---

## 5. “Because on it” — כִּי בוֹ (*kî ḇô*)

The conjunction **כִּי (*kî*)** introduces the reason for God's blessing and sanctification.

It may be rendered:

**“because”**

The phrase **בּוֹ (*ḇô*)** means:

**“in it”** or **“on it.”**

The reason the seventh day is blessed and sanctified is now explained:

**God rested on it.**

The sanctification of the day is therefore directly connected with God's own activity.

God did not arbitrarily choose the seventh day without explanation.

The day is marked because it is the day on which God ceased from His creative work.

The divine pattern establishes the significance of the day.

---

## 6. “God rested” — שָׁבַת (*šāḇaṯ*)

The verb **שָׁבַת (*šāḇaṯ*)** means **“to cease,” “to stop,” or “to rest.”**

This is the same verbal root associated with the concept of **Sabbath**.

The seventh day is therefore the day of God's cessation from His creative work.

As emphasized in Genesis 2:2, this does not mean that God became tired.

God's rest is not the rest of recovery from exhaustion.

It is the rest of **completion**.

God ceased because His work had been accomplished.

This is why Genesis 2:3 connects the sanctification of the seventh day directly with God's cessation from His work.

---

## 7. “From all his work” — מִכָּל־מְלַאכְתּוֹ (*mikkol-məlāḵtô*)

God rested:

**“from all his work.”**

The phrase is comprehensive.

The creation work was complete.

God did not merely stop one portion of His activity.

He ceased from **all** the creative work associated with the six days.

This echoes Genesis 2:2:

**“from all his work that he had done.”**

The repetition reinforces the completeness of creation.

Genesis 2:1 declared that the heavens and earth and all their host were finished.

Genesis 2:2 declared that God finished His work and rested.

Genesis 2:3 now gives the theological consequence:

**The seventh day was blessed and sanctified because the work had been completed.**

---

## 8. “That he had done in creation” — אֲשֶׁר־בָּרָא אֱלֹהִים לַעֲשׂוֹת

The final phrase refers back to God's creative activity.

The Hebrew includes the verb **בָּרָא (*bārāʾ*)**, the same important verb used in Genesis 1 to describe God's creation.

The verse therefore explicitly identifies the work from which God rested as His **creative work**.

Creation was God's work.

Completion was God's work.

Rest was God's response to completed creation.

The seventh day therefore receives its significance from God's creative activity.

This gives the Sabbath pattern a foundation in creation itself.

---

## 9. The first sanctified time

Genesis 2:3 is significant because it identifies a **day** as holy.

The holiness of the seventh day is not derived from Israel's later covenant.

It precedes the Mosaic covenant.

Israel's Sabbath command comes much later in the biblical story, but its foundation is already present here.

Exodus 20:11 explicitly appeals to creation:

> “For in six days the LORD made heaven and earth…”

and then connects God's rest with the seventh day.

Thus, the Sabbath has a creational foundation before it receives its covenantal expression under Moses.

This is an important distinction.

Genesis 2:3 gives the **creation pattern**.

The Mosaic covenant later gives Israel the **covenantal command**.

---

## 10. The seventh day as God's gift

Because God blesses the seventh day, the day can be understood as a divine gift within creation.

God's blessing establishes a rhythm in which labor is not the whole of human existence.

Human beings are created to work, but they are also created to cease from ordinary labor and recognize their dependence upon God.

The sanctified day therefore confronts the temptation to define human existence exclusively by productivity.

Human beings do not exist merely to work.

The Creator Himself establishes a rhythm of work and rest.

The seventh day reminds humanity that the world is ultimately God's work, not humanity's achievement.

---

## 11. The Sabbath and human dependence

Genesis 2:3 establishes an important theological principle:

**Humanity depends upon the Creator.**

The Sabbath interrupts the illusion of human self-sufficiency.

When humanity rests, it acknowledges that God's providence does not depend upon uninterrupted human labor.

The world continues because God sustains it.

This becomes particularly important in Israel's Sabbath theology.

Israel was commanded to cease from ordinary labor and trust God's provision.

The Sabbath therefore has a theological dimension of **faith and dependence**.

Rest declares:

**God is God, and humanity is not.**

---

## 12. The goodness of sacred time

Genesis 1 emphasizes the goodness of created space, creatures, and humanity.

Genesis 2:3 adds a distinctive emphasis on **time**.

The seventh day belongs to God's good creation.

Time itself is part of the created order.

God therefore establishes not only where human beings live but also a rhythm within which they live.

The sanctification of the seventh day demonstrates that God's purposes encompass the temporal dimension of human existence.

Human beings are not merely creatures of physical space.

They are creatures who live within God's ordered time.

---

## 13. The seventh day and worship

Genesis 2:3 does not explicitly use the word **“worship.”**

Therefore, the verse should not be forced to say that Adam and Eve were given a complete liturgical order on the seventh day.

What it explicitly teaches is that God blessed and sanctified the day.

Later biblical revelation associates Sabbath observance with Israel's covenant worship and devotion to God.

Thus, the proper canonical approach is to distinguish what Genesis 2:3 directly states from what later revelation develops.

Genesis establishes the **holy character of the seventh day**.

Later Scripture develops the practical and covenantal implications of that holiness.

---

## 14. Genesis 2:3 and the rhythm of creation

The creation account has a carefully ordered rhythm.

God works.

God completes.

God rests.

God blesses.

God sanctifies.

This means that the climax of the creation week is not merely the production of more things.

It is the establishment of an ordered rhythm.

The world God creates is a world governed by His purposes.

The seventh day therefore functions as a sign that creation has reached its intended order.

The day itself becomes a recurring testimony to God's completed work.

---

## 15. Genesis 2:3 and God's kingship

The sanctification of the seventh day also communicates God's authority.

Only the Creator can declare something within creation holy by divine appointment.

The day is holy because **God made it holy**.

This is an expression of divine authority.

God determines the purpose and order of creation.

He determines not only what exists but also how created existence is ordered.

The seventh day therefore bears witness to God's kingship.

The Creator establishes the rhythm of creation because creation belongs to Him.

---

## 16. Genesis 2:3 and the Creator–creature distinction

God's rest must be carefully distinguished from human rest.

God is not subject to physical limitations.

Human beings are.

God's rest is therefore paradigmatic rather than identical to human rest.

He ceases from creation because His work is complete.

Human beings cease from labor because they are creatures who require rest and are commanded to recognize God's authority.

The Sabbath therefore teaches humility.

Human beings cannot sustain the universe.

They cannot create the world.

They cannot control every outcome.

They depend upon God.

The sanctified seventh day becomes a recurring reminder of this dependence.

---

## 17. Genesis 2:3 and the later Sabbath command

The connection between Genesis 2:3 and Exodus 20 is explicit.

Exodus 20:11 gives creation as the reason for Israel's Sabbath observance.

The pattern is:

**Six days of creation → seventh day of divine rest → seventh day blessed and sanctified → Israel commanded to remember the Sabbath.**

This establishes a continuity between creation and covenant.

At the same time, the later Sabbath command belongs to Israel's covenantal situation and therefore has additional legal and redemptive dimensions that Genesis 2:3 itself does not spell out.

Genesis provides the foundation.

Exodus develops the covenant command.

---

## 18. Genesis 2:3 and Hebrews 4

The theology of God's rest eventually receives significant development in Hebrews 3–4.

Hebrews looks back to God's rest and argues that the promise of entering God's rest remains.

This means that Genesis 2:3 establishes a theological category that extends beyond the weekly calendar.

God's rest becomes a picture of the believer's participation in God's completed work.

Hebrews 4:10 states that whoever has entered God's rest has also rested from his works as God did from His.

The connection is profound:

**God's rest after creation becomes the foundation for the biblical theology of entering God's rest.**

The ultimate significance of the theme therefore reaches beyond the seventh day toward God's saving purpose.

---

## 19. Genesis 2:3 and Christ's completed work

The New Testament presents Christ's saving work as a completed work.

At the cross, Jesus declares:

**“It is finished.”**

John 19:30 explicitly uses language of completion.

This should not be interpreted as saying that Genesis 2:3 is directly predicting the crucifixion.

Genesis 2 concerns creation.

John 19 concerns redemption.

Nevertheless, the canonical pattern is significant:

**Creation work completed → rest**

and later:

**Redemptive work accomplished → Christ's death and resurrection**

Hebrews then brings God's rest and Christ's completed work into the same broader theological framework.

Christ does not merely offer another human attempt at salvation.

He accomplishes the work necessary for redemption.

---

## 20. Creation, redemption, and consummation

Genesis 2:3 stands at the beginning of a much larger biblical pattern.

The Bible moves from:

**Creation**

to

**Fall**

to

**Redemption**

to

**Consummation.**

Genesis 2:3 belongs to the creation stage, before sin enters the world.

The holy rest of God therefore belongs to the original good creation.

After the Fall, rest becomes intertwined with humanity's experience of sin, labor, curse, and redemption.

The New Testament then develops the promise of God's rest in Christ.

The biblical story ultimately reaches the renewed creation.

Thus Genesis 2:3 contributes to the Bible's larger theology of creation and restoration.

---

## 21. Literary function within Genesis 1–2

Genesis 2:3 is the climax of Genesis 1:1–2:3.

The structure is remarkably deliberate:

**Genesis 1:1**  
God creates the heavens and earth.

**Genesis 1:2–31**  
God forms and fills the world.

**Genesis 2:1**  
The heavens and earth are completed.

**Genesis 2:2**  
God finishes His work and rests.

**Genesis 2:3**  
God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day.

The creation account therefore ends not merely with:

**“God finished.”**

It ends with:

**“God blessed.”**

and

**“God made holy.”**

The conclusion emphasizes the goodness, order, and sacred significance of God's completed creation.

---

## 22. The central theological proposition

Genesis 2:3 can be summarized in this central proposition:

**God blessed and sanctified the seventh day because He had completed His creative work and ceased from it, establishing the foundation for the biblical theology of Sabbath and divine rest.**

Or more simply:

**God made the seventh day holy because His creative work was complete.**

The holiness of the day therefore rests upon God's action.

The Sabbath is not ultimately about human achievement.

It begins with God's completed work.

---

## Exegetical Outline

**Genesis 2:3**

**“God blessed the seventh day”**

→ The seventh day receives divine blessing.

→ The day is distinguished within creation.

**“and made it holy”**

→ God sanctifies the seventh day.

→ Sacred time is established within creation.

**“because on it God rested”**

→ The holiness of the day is connected with God's cessation from creative work.

→ Divine rest is not exhaustion.

**“from all his work”**

→ The entirety of God's creative work was complete.

→ Nothing essential remained unfinished.

**“that he had done in creation”**

→ The rest specifically concerns God's completed creative activity.

→ Creation is the foundation of the Sabbath pattern.

**Central idea:**

**God blessed and sanctified the seventh day because His creative work was complete and He ceased from that work, establishing the foundational pattern of Sabbath rest within the created order.**

---

## Doctrinal Implications

Genesis 2:3 establishes or reinforces these major doctrines:

1. **Creation** — the world exists as God's completed work.
2. **Divine sovereignty** — God determines the order and purpose of creation.
3. **Divine blessing** — God can designate created realities for His purposes.
4. **Sanctification** — God sets apart the seventh day as holy.
5. **Sabbath theology** — the Sabbath has a foundation in creation.
6. **Human dependence** — humanity is not sustained by uninterrupted labor.
7. **Human vocation** — work and rest both belong to God's good order.
8. **Creator–creature distinction** — God's rest is unique and establishes a pattern for creatures.
9. **Divine providence** — creation does not depend upon continuous human activity.
10. **Creation's goodness** — sacred rest belongs to the original good creation.
11. **Biblical anthropology** — human beings are creatures who live within God's ordered rhythm.
12. **Christological development** — the New Testament connects completed work and divine rest with the saving work of Christ.
13. **Eschatological hope** — God's rest ultimately points toward the fuller rest promised to His people.

---

## A Concise Preaching/Exposition Framework

Genesis 2:3 can be structured around three propositions:

### I. GOD BLESSES THE SEVENTH DAY

**“So God blessed the seventh day…”**

The seventh day is uniquely marked by divine blessing.

### II. GOD SANCTIFIES THE SEVENTH DAY

**“…and made it holy…”**

The seventh day is set apart by God's own authority.

### III. GOD ESTABLISHES REST AFTER COMPLETED WORK

**“…because on it God rested from all his work that he had done in creation.”**

The seventh day derives its significance from God's completed creative work.

**Main proposition:**

**Genesis 2:3 teaches that God blessed and sanctified the seventh day because He had completed His creative work and ceased from it, establishing the foundational pattern of Sabbath rest and pointing forward to the greater rest promised in God's redemptive purposes.**

The canonical progression can be summarized:

**Genesis 2:3**  
God blesses and sanctifies the seventh day.

**Exodus 20:8–11**  
God commands Israel to remember the Sabbath.

**Psalm 95:7–11**  
God's rest becomes associated with His covenant promise.

**Hebrews 4:3–11**  
God's people are called to enter His rest.

**John 19:30**  
Christ declares His redemptive work accomplished.

**Revelation 21:1–5**  
God brings His redemptive purposes to consummation in the renewed creation.

Genesis 2:3 therefore closes the creation account with a profound declaration:

**Creation is complete.**

**God's work is finished.**

**The seventh day is blessed.**

**The seventh day is holy.**

And the biblical story begins to establish a pattern that will eventually lead the reader from the rest of creation to the rest promised through God's completed redemptive work.

**The Creator's completed work establishes the foundation for the creature's holy rest.**
